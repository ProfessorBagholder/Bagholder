Overview

The Ninepoint Cash Management Fund is a high-quality, short-term vehicle for investors who want to earn a better rate of return on their idle cash. 

  • No term commitments

    Unlike GICs and Term Deposits, there is no penalty for redemption

  • Daily liquidity 

    Easy access to cash

  • Now available as an ETF series

    Easier to incorporate into your portfolio model and bulk trade

  • A better alternative for cash balances

    The Fund commonly invests in high-quality interest-bearing securities with short maturities and low credit risk. Typical investments include short-term government bonds, treasury bills and commercial paper.

Fund Objective

The objective of this Fund is to maximize yield on cash balances, while providing easy access to investments with daily liquidity. The Fund invests in high interest savings accounts offered at Schedule 1 Canadian Banks and/or high-quality money market securities.

 

No Guarantees

Like most mutual funds, this Fund does not have any guarantees. Mutual fund securities are not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or any other government deposit insurer. There can be no assurances that the fund will be able to maintain its net asset value per unit at a constant amount or that the full amount of your investment in the fund will be returned to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is it?

    It is a money market fund that holds a carefully selected portfolio of high-quality securities. To qualify as a money market fund, it must adhere to certain rules. For example, the maximum term of its holdings (no more than one year) and credit quality (rated A or higher by all major rating agencies). Additionally, the fund’s average term to maturity must remain under 180 days, and no single issuer (except the government) can exceed 10% of the portfolio. These rules are designed to ensure high credit quality and minimal risk. As a result, funds like this maintain a stable net asset value (NAV) of $10 per unit.

  • Who is it for?

    This fund is ideal for investors looking to hold cash while earning a competitive return. Typically, money market fund yields exceed both the Bank of Canada’s overnight rate and standard bank deposit rates. It also offers greater liquidity than a GIC, which often has redemption restrictions. Within an investment account, this fund functions much like an overnight deposit, providing flexibility and ease of access.

  • Is it guaranteed?

    No, the fund is not guaranteed. Unlike a GIC or a deposit at a federally regulated financial institution (which may be insured up to $100,000), this fund carries no formal guarantee. However, its strict investment criteria help manage risk effectively.

  • Why might the portfolio yield differ from the 7-day yield?

    Several factors can cause variations:

    1. Fees – The portfolio yield is presented before fees, while the 7-day yield reflects actual distributions after fees.
    2. Timing differences – The 7-day yield is calculated daily, whereas portfolio characteristics are computed and reported once a month (at month end). The difference in timing means the most recent characteristics will be at least two weeks older than the latest 7-day yield.
    3. Interest rate changes – A sudden rate cut by the Bank of Canada, for instance, could impact short-term yields.
    4. Accounting methodology – Money market funds use amortized cost accounting, whereas traditional mutual funds use mark-to-market valuation, leading to differences in how yields are presented.

  • What kind of securities are in the fund?

    A breakdown of the fund’s securities is available on our website. Every security in the fund must adhere to the criteria outlined earlier—For example, an A or better credit rating and a maturity of less than one year. Several types of securities meet these requirements, including:

    For illustrative purposes only

    1. Short-Term Corporate Bonds

    • These can be either longer-term bonds that now have less than one year to maturity or bonds specifically issued with a 1 year term, designed for money market funds.
    • Large banks regularly issue 6-month or 1-year floating-rate deposit notes, which adjust with the Bank of Canada’s rate, typically offering a slightly higher return. These securities are suitable for the Fund, providing attractive yields with credit risk comparable to an uninsured deposit at the issuing bank.

     

    2. Schedule I Bank High-Interest Savings Accounts (HISA)

    • These are overnight deposits are currently held with TD Bank (Feb 2025),
    • The yield on these deposits typically tracks closely with the Bank of Canada’s overnight rate.

     

    3. Short-Term Federal Government Bonds

    • Government of Canada bonds with 1 year or less to maturity.
    • AAA-rated, offering the highest credit quality but typically among the lowest yields in the fund.

     

    4. Asset-Backed Securities (ABS)

    • These securities are rare but sometimes available with a maturity of 1 year or less.
    • They have fixed maturity date, are AAA-rated and backed by consumer-related assets such as credit card receivables, mortgages, or other financial instruments.
    • Their high rating comes from overcollateralization (more assets backing the bonds than their total value) and the presence of lower-ranked investors who absorb initial losses.
    • Important distinction: This is not the same as Asset-Backed Commercial Paper (ABCP), which many competitor money market funds use extensively. The key difference is transparency—ABCP lacks clear visibility into its underlying assets, whereas ABS follows strict rules and monthly reporting requirements.

     

    5. Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

    • AAA-rated, short-term instruments issued by the Government of Canada.
    • Used for short-term funding and typically offer the lowest yields in the fund.

     

    6. Commercial Paper

    • Short-term debt issued by Canadian and foreign corporations.
    • To qualify, it must have a high credit rating, which may exclude well-known issuers.
    • Maturities are generally under three months, but supply has become limited, and yields are often low, making it a less attractive option in today’s market.
