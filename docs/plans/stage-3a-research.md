## 4. Cboe delayed chains (2026-09-24, replies in $TMPDIR/research/cboe and `rust/crates/sources/tests/replies/cboe-options`)
- A chain states its own times. `timestamp` is when Cboe made it, in UTC (the reply's `Last-Modified` agreed within seconds on every capture: 03:30:07 / 03:30:10, 02:08:13 / 02:08:16). `data.last_trade_time` is the underlying's last trade, Eastern with no offset written (`2026-09-22T15:59:59`, `2026-09-22T16:00:00`: the regular session's end). Each contract states `bid`, `ask`, `last_trade_price` and `last_trade_time` (Eastern; null, with a price of 0, for a contract never traded: 20 of BBAI's 248), `change` and `prev_day_close`.
- So the session a chain carries is the day of the underlying's last trade, and whether its prices are the closing ones is told by `timestamp` against that session's settle (16:30 Eastern: options trade to 16:15, delayed fifteen minutes). QNC's chain, made 2026-09-22 18:28 UTC (14:28 Eastern) with its underlying last trading 14:09, is an in-session chain: it quotes, and closes nothing.
- The earlier note here keyed on `timestamp` alone and read a chain as "a session behind"; that was wrong. A chain whose times show an older session is that session's data, as stated.
- `prev_day_close` is not used: its day is not stated in the reply. For a contract not traded the session before, it is the midpoint of the closing quote (0.00499999988824129 for a 0 / 0.01 market).
- Held contracts' chains observed on 2026-09-24 still carried the 2026-09-22 session (made that night): Cboe published nothing for the 2026-09-23 session by 14:32 UTC on the 24th. The reader writes the 22nd's closes it states and leaves the 23rd due.
- The symbol in the URL is the listing's own (`BRK.B`); `BRKB` and `BRK-B` answer 403, as any symbol Cboe does not list. The OCC symbols drop the point (`BRKB260925C00270000`).
- How this was settled, and how it should have been (brief 05): the question was which session a chain's prices belong to. Everything needed was already recorded before any poll ran: `timestamp` had been confirmed as UTC against `Last-Modified`, and the reply states the session in `data.last_trade_time` and each contract's `last_trade_time`. Reading those fields settles it; the plan's method ("chains captured before and after a session's close on the same day, and across a weekend") and the ten-hour poll that followed it answered nothing the fields did not already state. The poll was stopped on 2026-09-24 at 14:28 UTC and nothing has written to its folder since; its captures serve only as fixtures.
- Superseded by the owner's decision of 2026-09-24 (brief 06 §1–§2, `docs/decisions.md`): no option close is recorded any more and the after-close read is gone. Cboe's chains remain the source of a held contract's price on screen, read when shown. The field reading above is what that reader relies on.
- Decision (the plan, "Option closes", before brief 06): a contract's close for the session is the closing bid/ask midpoint where both are quoted, as the live price is (`SPEC.md` §2), else its last trade where the stated time falls on the session, else none.

## 3. The Bank's rates before 2017 (2026-09-24)
- Valet daily averages (FX<CUR>CAD) begin 2017-01-03; Valet LEGACY_NOON_RATES (IEXE0101 = USD_NOON) cover 2007-05-01..2017-04-28.
- Statistics Canada table 10-10-0008-01, "Foreign exchange rates in Canadian dollars, Bank of Canada, daily" (noon spot rates, 44 series), covers 1950-10-02..2017-04-28 through its Web Data Service: USD noon is vector 121716. It equals the Bank's own noon series where both exist (2008-01-02 0.9927, 2008-01-03 0.9905, 2008-01-04 0.9991 in both) and answers 1995 (1995-01-03 1.4035).
- StatCan's table 33-10-0036-01 (daily averages) returns nothing for USD before 2017 (2003 range empty), so it does not extend Valet's daily average backward.
- StatCan answers JSON numbers (read from their text); it states a non-business day explicitly (value null, statusCode 1); it refuses bursts (empty body), so it is paced.
- Decision: the Bank's daily average (Valet) from 2017-01-03; before that, the Bank's noon rate from Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 (one source, 1950 onward); Valet's legacy noon group is not needed. No pre-2007 gap.

## 3, continued. Which currencies each archive holds (2026-09-24, replies in $TMPDIR/research/statcan)
- Valet's `LEGACY_NOON_RATES` group lists 65 series, all ending 2017-04-28; 58 of them state 2007-05-01. A label does not name one series: `USD_NOON` is both IEXE0101 ("U.S. dollar (noon)") and IEXE0105 ("U.S. dollar noon, 90-day", a forward rate); `ARS_NOON` is IEXE2702 ("Argentine peso (local)", 2007-05-01..2012-03-15) and IEXE2703 ("Argentine peso", 2010-01-04..2017-04-28), which overlap and differ (2010-01-04: 0.2690 and 0.2734); `MMK_NOON` is IEXE3801 ("Myanmar (Burma) kyat (fixed)", 2007-05-01..2012-03-30) then IEXE3802 ("Myanmar (Burma) kyat", 2012-04-02..2017-04-28); IEXE0124 and IEXE0125 are forward-point spreads labelled with their own codes.
- Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 (1950-10-02..2017-04-28) holds noon spot rates for 12 currencies still in use: USD, DKK, JPY, NOK, SEK, CHF, GBP, AUD, HKD, NZD, MXN, EUR (members named in English, e.g. "Mexican pesos, noon spot rate"), plus eight retired European currencies and forward, closing, high and low series.
- So USD alone does not settle it: many currencies Valet published at noon from 2007 (CNY, INR, SGD, ZAR…) have no archive before 2007-05-01.
- Decision (replaces the earlier "Valet's legacy noon group is not needed"): three eras per currency, never overlapping: the Bank's daily average (Valet `FX<CUR>CAD`) from its first day; before it, the Bank's noon rate from Valet's legacy noon series; before that, the noon spot rate from Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 where it holds the currency. Both archives ended in 2017 and never change, so which series is a currency's noon spot rate is a fixed table in the adapter, written from the Bank's and Statistics Canada's own metadata (series code, its description, its first and last day; USD is IEXE0101 and StatCan member 1; ARS is IEXE2702 until 2010-01-01 and IEXE2703 from 2010-01-04, the plain-named series standing where both publish; MMK is IEXE3801 then IEXE3802), and every read checks the reply's description against the table (a different one is a mismatch). A day before a currency's oldest archive is a gap named `rate-not-held`: no source holds a published Bank rate for it. It is never `rate-missing`, which names a failure of the source.

## Findings while building (2026-09-24)
- **TMX's distributions do not say which were paid in cash.** Its rows carry an ex-date and an amount, and a record, pay and declaration date where it has them. For QCN, four of the five rows with no pay date (2018-12-31 0.2768, 2021-12-30 1.74703, 2022-12-29 1.374, 2023-12-28 0.35705) equal, to the digit, Mackenzie's own yearly tax total less its listed cash rows: the year-end distributions paid in units. The fifth (2021-03-22 0.72086) is a cash payment Mackenzie lists with its pay date and TMX lists with none of its dates. Decision: a TMX row with a pay date is cash; a row with none is read as paying no cash, its amount kept as the part in units. Only the latest distribution gone ex feeds a figure, and a year-end distribution in units is the row that would otherwise stand as the latest for three months.
- **TMX's quote `datetime` is when TMX served the quote**, in session and out: a request at 14:28:18 UTC during 2026-09-24's session answered `10:28:18-04:00`, and one at night answered its own moment too (00:20 Eastern for a price that last traded at the close). TMX offers no other time field (`lastTradeTime`, `tradeTime`, `quoteTime` are refused, each suggesting `datetime`). Decision: the time kept with a TMX quote is that served time, which says the price is current then; it is never presented as a trade's time.
- **Coins' closes.** The Exchange lists no CAD pair (BTC-CAD's candles answer 404), and every held coin is priced in CAD. `SPEC.md` §2 (Coinbase) already says what the close is then: the USD market's close for the UTC day, converted at the day's Bank of Canada rate. The close is stored in USD as the Exchange states it, and the engine converts it at that day's rate where a figure uses it (its closes now carry their currency). The spot price's reply now says `Cache-Control: no-store`, so its stamp is the reply's `Date` with no allowance.
- **Cboe Canada's quote** carries `trade_time` with its offset (`2026-09-23 16:00:00-04:00`): a real trade time.
- **The Bank's holiday page** lists each closure to come as an article (date and name), not as a table, so it is read from those and the HTML table reader stays where the fund exposures use it.
