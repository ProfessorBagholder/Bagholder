# Stage 3a research log (verbatim evidence; folded into the plan's Verification)

## 1. Split adjustment of daily closes (2026-09-23)
- Yahoo chart `close` is split-adjusted: NVDA 2024-06-04 close reads 116.437 (traded ~1164 before the 10:1 split of 2024-06-10). The same reply carries `events.splits` {date 1718026200, numerator 10, denominator 1} when asked with `events=split`. Undoable from its own reply.
- TMX getTimeSeriesData is split-adjusted too: SHOP 2022-06-24 close 49.709 (traded ~497 before the 10:1 split effective 2022-06-29), identical to Yahoo's adjusted series; the reply states no split. Not undoable from its own reply.
- Decision: daily closes of listings from Yahoo, stored as traded by undoing the adjustment with the splits in the same reply; TMX not used for closes.

## 4. Cboe delayed chains (first capture 2026-09-24T01:57Z, after the 2026-09-23 session)
- The chain served at 01:57Z on the 24th has timestamp "2026-09-23 03:54:59" (UTC per Last-Modified), still lists contracts expiring 2026-09-22, and its SPY current_price = prev_day_close = close = 773.38, which is Yahoo's close for 2026-09-22 (Yahoo's 2026-09-23 close: 767.81). The chain had not been updated for the whole 2026-09-23 session.
- So: a chain can be a session behind. Its own timestamp must be checked against the session it is supposed to carry; a stale chain is a meaning failure, not data.
- Next capture: during and after the 2026-09-24 session.

## 3. The Bank's rates before 2017 (2026-09-24)
- Valet daily averages (FX<CUR>CAD) begin 2017-01-03; Valet LEGACY_NOON_RATES (IEXE0101 = USD_NOON) cover 2007-05-01..2017-04-28.
- Statistics Canada table 10-10-0008-01, "Foreign exchange rates in Canadian dollars, Bank of Canada, daily" (noon spot rates, 44 series), covers 1950-10-02..2017-04-28 through its Web Data Service: USD noon is vector 121716. It equals the Bank's own noon series where both exist (2008-01-02 0.9927, 2008-01-03 0.9905, 2008-01-04 0.9991 in both) and answers 1995 (1995-01-03 1.4035).
- StatCan's table 33-10-0036-01 (daily averages) returns nothing for USD before 2017 (2003 range empty), so it does not extend Valet's daily average backward.
- StatCan answers JSON numbers (read from their text); it states a non-business day explicitly (value null, statusCode 1); it refuses bursts (empty body), so it is paced.
- Decision: the Bank's daily average (Valet) from 2017-01-03; before that, the Bank's noon rate from Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 (one source, 1950 onward); Valet's legacy noon group is not needed. No pre-2007 gap.

## 5. Benchmark sources (2026-09-24, replies in $TMPDIR/research/bench)
- FRED `fredgraph.csv?id=SP500` answers `observation_date,SP500`, one row per weekday from 2016-09-26 to 2026-09-23 (2,608 rows): the trailing ten years only. Levels are written with two decimals (`2026-09-23,7706.03`). A US market holiday is a row with an empty value (`2016-11-24,`; 96 such rows), not a missing row and not `.`.
- Stooq (`stooq.com/q/d/l/?s=^spx&i=d`) now answers every request with a JavaScript proof-of-work page ("This site requires JavaScript to verify your browser"), not data. Passing it would be defeating a bot check, so Stooq is dropped as a source.
- S&P Dow Jones Indices' own site (`spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/equity/sp-500/`) answers 403 from Akamai to a plain request.
- Yahoo's chart for `^GSPC` answers the whole span asked (2016-01-04 onward in one reply; exchange zone America/New_York, currency USD), its closes written as binary float leftovers (`7706.02978515625` for FRED's `7706.03`). On the 2,511 days both carry, they agree within half a cent except nine: 2018-11-29 (FRED 2737.76, Yahoo 2737.80), 2019-08-12 (2883.75 / 2882.70), 2020-05-11 (2930.32 / 2930.19), 2020-10-02, -05, -06, -07, -09 (differences of 0.01 to 0.03), 2021-08-11 (4447.70 / 4442.41). Neither is S&P's own statement; the one reachable publisher of the index does not answer.
- TMX `getTimeSeriesData` for `^TSX` and `^TX60` answers any span in one reply, newest first, each row's `dateTime` at 16:00 with its offset (`2026-09-23T16:00:00-04:00`), levels with two decimals; `^TSX` history begins 2001-12-11 (a 1995–2026 request returns 6,198 rows, the oldest 2001-12-11; 1990–2000 returns an empty list). TMX carries no S&P 500 (`^SPX` and `^GSPC` answer an empty list; `SPX` is an unrelated listing).
- Decision: S&P 500 levels from FRED, and from Yahoo's `^GSPC` for days before FRED's window (the only reachable source for them), each level stored as its source wrote it and under its source's name; a day both carry is taken from FRED and the other's different value is recorded beside it (the cache's rule for a disagreeing close). `^TSX` and `^TX60` from TMX, from 2001-12-11; a day before that is before the series exists. An empty FRED value is the source stating no level for that day (a holiday), read as such, never as a failure. Stooq is removed from SPEC §2.

## 3, continued. Which currencies each archive holds (2026-09-24, replies in $TMPDIR/research/statcan)
- Valet's `LEGACY_NOON_RATES` group lists 65 series, all ending 2017-04-28; 58 of them state 2007-05-01. A label does not name one series: `USD_NOON` is both IEXE0101 ("U.S. dollar (noon)") and IEXE0105 ("U.S. dollar noon, 90-day", a forward rate); `ARS_NOON` is IEXE2702 ("Argentine peso (local)", 2007-05-01..2012-03-15) and IEXE2703 ("Argentine peso", 2010-01-04..2017-04-28), which overlap and differ (2010-01-04: 0.2690 and 0.2734); `MMK_NOON` is IEXE3801 ("Myanmar (Burma) kyat (fixed)", 2007-05-01..2012-03-30) then IEXE3802 ("Myanmar (Burma) kyat", 2012-04-02..2017-04-28); IEXE0124 and IEXE0125 are forward-point spreads labelled with their own codes.
- Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 (1950-10-02..2017-04-28) holds noon spot rates for 12 currencies still in use: USD, DKK, JPY, NOK, SEK, CHF, GBP, AUD, HKD, NZD, MXN, EUR (members named in English, e.g. "Mexican pesos, noon spot rate"), plus eight retired European currencies and forward, closing, high and low series.
- So USD alone does not settle it: many currencies Valet published at noon from 2007 (CNY, INR, SGD, ZAR…) have no archive before 2007-05-01.
- Decision (replaces the earlier "Valet's legacy noon group is not needed"): three eras per currency, never overlapping: the Bank's daily average (Valet `FX<CUR>CAD`) from its first day; before it, the Bank's noon rate from Valet's legacy noon series; before that, the noon spot rate from Statistics Canada 10-10-0008 where it holds the currency. Both archives ended in 2017 and never change, so which series is a currency's noon spot rate is a fixed table in the adapter, written from the Bank's and Statistics Canada's own metadata (series code, its description, its first and last day; USD is IEXE0101 and StatCan member 1; ARS is IEXE2702 until 2010-01-01 and IEXE2703 from 2010-01-04, the plain-named series standing where both publish; MMK is IEXE3801 then IEXE3802), and every read checks the reply's description against the table (a different one is a mismatch). A day before a currency's oldest archive is a gap named `rate-not-held`: no source holds a published Bank rate for it. It is never `rate-missing`, which names a failure of the source.

Companies: each declares in a release, which states the amount, record date, pay date and the schedule in its own words ("declared a quarterly dividend of $0.4375 per common share, payable on October 15, 2026 to shareholders of record at the close of business on September 15, 2026"). All nine publish through Cision (newswire.ca, one page layout); BNS, ATD and ALV also through InvestorRoom/MediaRoom sites (one form), CP through its Q4 JSON feed (its pages answer only a browser). Their own history tables are stale or partial (BCE's stops at Feb 2025, ATD's lacks 2026, TD's lacks two 2005–06 rows, ALV's is an image). Only TD states an ex-date; for the rest the ex-date is the record date (T+1 since 2024-05-27) or the business day before it (T+2 before), the exchange's rule.

What the pages show beyond that: rows missing (QCN and QUU have no Q1 2020; ZMMK no July 2025; SIXY no 2026-01-15), rows repeated (MSTY to 2025-12; GSWO 2025-12-23), a typo (MSTY record 2036-07-30), a page lagging its own schedule by weeks (Harvest), two of the payer's own publications disagreeing (Ninepoint CCHI March 2026: page 0.27, its PDF 0.26).

Decisions:
- One adapter per fund company, reading the publication in the table; one per company's release form (Cision, InvestorRoom/MediaRoom, Q4), with each company's sentence read strictly; frequency from the payer's own words (a closed list per adapter, anything else a mismatch).
- Where the payer states a distribution's cash and reinvested parts, both are stored as stated; the per-unit amount the figures use is the cash per unit, since the figures are income paid (SPEC §2 "Annual income").
- A page's rows are its statement: a repeated row identical in every field the adapter reads is one row; two rows for one ex-date that differ are a meaning failure naming both; a date outside the fund's life (a record date ten years ahead) is a meaning failure.
- A site that answers only a browser is not read by presenting one: that is getting past a bot check. Global X is read from its own releases; Mackenzie and WisdomTree have no other first-party publication found: by the owner's exception (2026-09-24) and brief 02, their distributions come from the listing's exchange-side record (TMX, Yahoo), marked with that source, and their schedule only where a source states it, never worked out from dates.
