# Brief 27: the record-by-change build as built (#507 at `d8237f76`): sound, merge after the fixes below

**Reviewed:** PR #507 at `d8237f76` (base `d6824aa7`; it carries #505 and #506 underneath) against brief 26's fifteen required changes and CTO's three questions: migration 028 in full, `book/src/{changes.rs, lib.rs, identity.rs, clear.rs, links.rs}`, `engine/src/{families.rs, engine.rs, ledger.rs, input.rs, identity.rs, trades.rs, cashflow.rs}`, `server/src/{figures.rs, csv_import.rs, capacity.rs, engine_inputs.rs, clear.rs, wire/build.rs}`, `wealthsimple/src/generated.rs`, and the tests the PR names (`by_change.rs`, `changes.rs`, `successions.rs`, `tests_ledger_log.rs`, the two linking tests, the in-suite capacity test, run at `d8237f76`). Six readers, one per area; each finding challenged by two more. 27 findings were raised; 23 survived; they are merged below where they share a cause. The record is in `27/review.json`.

## Verdict

The design landed as reviewed, and most of it is right. The log is written by triggers with `AUTOINCREMENT`; a succession logs everything it re-points; the log and every keyed read are taken in one snapshot under the engine's lock; the families with brief 26's added couplings are matched again; and `Matched` and `Identity` are compared whole against a fresh build. Linking runs master's rule, kept byte for byte as the test oracle. The gate has a depth pair, and the owner's copy shows identical figures at a tenth of the work. Merge after the fixes below, all in this PR: 1, 2 and 4–7 leave wrong figures on screen until a restart, and 3 is why the test suite did not find 2.

## CTO's three questions

1. **`Matched::replace` and `Identity::replace` attribute correctly.** Entries keyed by transaction go to the region of their transaction's node, rows of a transaction that left the ledger go, and rows are put in place with `partition_point` after equal keys. The fault is downstream: which accounts' broker checks are recomputed is read after the replace (2).
2. **The one-snapshot read is sound.** Another writer's later rows get a higher seq and are never dropped unread; an apply that fails leaves the log for the next pass; `settle_trades`' own writes are read back at once. Three gaps remain. The inner catch-up discards what it names (5). Two failure paths drop the log before the state that makes dropping it safe (6). And the racing test brief 26 asked for is not there (13).
3. **The canonical order matches master on a fresh build**, for trades, positions and cash rows. Two differences reach a screen (11), and no test compares the presented order at all (11).

## Required fixes

1. **A write under a joined member logs only the member, so the head is never read again** (high). Sightings, refs, option terms, `listings_named` and `instruments_described` are written under the instrument's own id (`identity.rs:562`, :608, :939), and their triggers log only that id (`028:62–64`, :75–112, :295–306). The book reads them under the head through `group_sql` (`names` :433–439). `delta` reads the member as `None` (`instrument_as_read`, `identity.rs:259`) and never touches the head. After a succession every new trade arrives under the successor's id, so this is the ordinary case, not a rare one. Shown on a copy of the branch: buy under OLD, sell under NEW (joined), then a sale under NEW named "Charbone Renamed Inc."; `book.names(head)` changes, `changes_since(..).instruments` is `{member}` only, and the engine keeps the old name. Fix: every member-keyed trigger also logs the instrument the row is read as (`SELECT k UNION SELECT into_id FROM instrument_joins WHERE instrument_id = k`), or `changes_since` expands each instrument key to `canonical(id)`. Add a test: a join, then a record, a ref and a removal under the member, `same_as_fresh` after each.

2. **An account that loses its last holding of a reached instrument keeps a stale broker check.** `reach.accounts` takes `held_by` of the reached instruments *after* `Matched::replace` (`engine.rs:640–642`). An account that held the instrument before the change and holds none after is rechecked only by chance. Scenario: A's transfer-out of 10 X is linked to B's transfer-in, which names no instrument, so the lots land in (B, X) and B's statement agrees. A's transfer-out record is removed. A fresh build shows B a units difference (own 0 against the broker's 10); the incremental engine keeps B's old, agreeing check. Fix: take the accounts in `held_by` of the region both before and after the replace.

3. **The property test must generate what brief 26, 4 named, or it cannot see 2.** `by_change.rs` writes no `brokers`, `trades` or `groups` (`:140–152`), so `checks` is always empty, `Identity`'s `trade_of`, `moved`, `joined` and `unclaimed` are never filled, and groups are never rebuilt under test. Revisions only add one to cash (`:221–227`), so a row never moves family, account, link or problem. Fix: generate broker statements of cash and held units for every account, trades on openings, groups spanning uncoupled instruments, revisions that change a row's instrument, account, quantity or kind, `leg-unstated` added and removed with and without an instrument, an adjustment changing alone, and a change to an instrument or an account alone.

4. **Linking misses three kinds of change, so an imported fill can stay counted twice.**
   - (a) A broker record that becomes *taken* queues none of its other legs' keys. `link_records` has no trigger, and `supersede` writes only the from-records. Scenario: F2 is ambiguous over B (a two-leg options order) and B′. F1 then takes B through B's other leg. Master links F2 to B′ on the next pull; the branch never re-examines F2's key, so the contract reads 3 held against the broker's 2.
   - (b) Re-importing a file whose rows are already stored queues nothing, so the report no longer says "1 not linked" for a row that is still ambiguous (SPEC trade-42). The oracle filters the whole pass to the touched keys, so it cannot see this.
   - (c) The queue lives only in memory. It is taken before the pass and lost on an error, a restart (the in-app update) or a rebuild, and a start queues nothing, so release builds have no path that ever runs the whole rule again.

   Fix: queue every key of the `to` records when a supersede is written (or put a trigger on `link_records`); for an import, queue the keys of every row the file read, stored or unchanged, and have the oracle assert the ambiguous set for those rows whole; put the keys back on any error; at a start or rebuild, queue every key that holds a live file row, so the first `link` after it runs today's rule over all of them. That last step makes the keyed pass self-healing.

5. **The inner catch-up's names are thrown away.** `catch_up` drops the `Named` that `catch_up_named` returns (`figures.rs:88–90`). `settle_trades` calls it (:173), and it applies and forgets every writer's rows past the watermark, including a concurrent pull's new listing and broker id. That pull's own `pulled` then finds nothing to name. The order ticket cannot name the new listing to the broker (`orders/ticket.rs:116`) until a restart. Fix: accumulate `Named` across every catch-up in a `pulled` call and reload the union. Add a test that commits a refs change from a second connection between the two catch-ups.

6. **Two failure paths drop the log before the engine that makes that safe is in place.** `built` stores the watermark, swaps the movement index and forgets the log (`figures.rs:183–186`) before `settle_trades` and the caller's install (:265). `Book::clear` deletes `ledger_changes` inside the clear (`book/src/clear.rs:217`), and several fallible steps (`renew_id`, `clear_cache`, removing `snapshots/`) sit before `f.rebuild` (`server/src/clear.rs:156–177`). An error on either path leaves the old engine showing every cleared record. Every later change applies on top of it, and master's next whole read used to heal that; this build has nothing that will, until a restart. Fix: store the watermark and forget the log only after the new engine is installed; drop the delete from `Book::clear` (`built` already forgets what it built from); on an error in `rebuild`, set the engine to `None` so the next `state_zone` builds whole.

7. **A failed prune fails the pull after the apply.** `forget_changes` runs with `?` inside `catch_up_named` (:112–114). An `SQLITE_BUSY` or a full disk then skips `settle_trades`, the broker statements for `stated_for`, the names reload and the version bump: rows without their statements, the case `pulled` exists to prevent (2026-10-09). The watermark already protects correctness, so make the prune best-effort and log its failure.

8. **The PR body's depth claim contradicts its own table.** "Both operations stay within their wall-time budgets at four times the owner's deepest", but add-trade depth is 53.6 ms on the runner, 112.6 ms on a Pi, against 100. `judge` records the miss; listing the op under #420 hides it, and a listed op is measured once, not as the median of seven (`capacity.rs:839–848`, against §16). Correct the body and #420, and mark a listed op's wall-time miss in the table.

9. **The in-suite growth test cannot catch a whole read in the import path.** In test builds `link` runs the whole-ledger oracle (`csv_import.rs:429–456`) inside the import probe's measured window (`capacity.rs:665–667`). That test passes at `d8237f76` with three whole reads and a quadratic scan inside the window. Take the oracle out of the window, and add a planted failure: a probe with one `live_records(csv)` read must fail.

10. **The capacity probes must measure what they say.** The breadth target is the first held share by symbol, since the build stores no closes. At four times the book it is about 2.4× deeper (owner ×1: 10 rows, ×4: 24; small: 5, ×4: 12), so a depth cost shows as breadth growth. The depth target is held only by chance: every deep round trip sells everything, and `deepest()` falls back silently. With the suite's seed it measures the same 15-row listing at both sizes. Fix: keep the planted deep listing open; refuse when the chosen listing is below the planted depth; pin the breadth target to one depth at both sizes, and print each target's row count in the table.

11. **What the page shows in order must be tested in order.** `differences` keys every list into a map (`engine.rs:209–211`), so order and duplicates are invisible; no test compares `trades`, `positions` or `cash` as vectors. Two differences already exist:
    - Two stock-dividend rows of one event share the anchor's id and tie under `shown_order`. `binary_search_by` inserts the second before the first (`engine.rs:873–875`), so the Cashflow tab reverses them after any change to the family.
    - The header's "took more than the book held" sentences are now sorted by each row's own place (`in_match_order`, `ledger.rs:2401–2403`). Master kept the order rows were applied in, so within a day the sentences can swap.

    Fix: assert the three vectors equal a fresh build's, in order, on every step; insert with `partition_point` after equal keys (or break the tie on the instrument in both paths); either sort the match's lists by the place each row was applied or state the new within-day order in `SPEC.md`.

12. **The names cache is reset when the engine is first built.** `names()` caches an empty set when the engine is not built yet (`figures.rs:398–399`), and `state_zone`'s first build does not clear it, while `rebuild` does. Master healed it on every record change; the keyed reload does not. Clear it wherever `built` replaces the engine.

13. **The racing test brief 26, 3 asked for.** The PR ticks "Racing writers" against `each_writer_leaves_the_engine_as_a_fresh_build_would`, which runs on one thread. Add the two-writer test: a read-back fill against `fills_give_way`, with the commit forced between the log read and a keyed read, then `same_as_fresh` and no trade orphaned.

## Depth: the gate's call

Measured at four times the owner's deepest listing: 46,063 SQLite steps for an added trade, of which about 41,000 are `Book::names` rebuilding every name run from every sighting. Allocations grow 3.8× from matching the whole family again. My recommendation, in order:

1. **Keep name runs as rows** (option 3), maintained where sightings are written. This removes most of the steps and brings the wall time under 100 ms on a Pi. It is contained, and it is held by one equality: the stored runs equal `Book::names`' fold on every book the tests build.
2. **Resume the match from the family's state** (option 1), only once 1 is measured, and only with strict guards. It must keep the matcher's state, not only `Matched`, taken before the final `expire_before(today)`. It applies only to rows placed strictly after the family's last place: a later trade day, or the full `order_key` on the same day. It falls back to the whole family when a change touches `ended_on_record`, a coin's `places`, an adjustment, a transfer link to an earlier row, or terms. The property test must include back-dated corrections, expiries and same-day ties.
3. **Rebuild only the round trips whose content changed** (option 2) on its own does not bend the growth, since the match stays as deep as the family. It is the complement 2 needs.

Until then add-trade and import stay listed for depth under #420 and #419, with the cause written there, as `OVER_BUDGET` is meant to be used. At the owner's real deepest listing (349 rows) an added trade is 13.8 ms on this Mac today.
