The record is in `26/review.json`.

## Verdict

Go with changes. The design is the standard one: a change log captured in the store's own transaction, a delta applied by key, a partitioned recomputation (DBSP's "redo only the keys a change touches"), and equality with a full build as the test. Triggers over writers naming their changes is the right call (question 2 below). But three of the plan's own mechanisms would lose changes outright (1–3), its equality test cannot see most of what it re-keys (4), and the linking redesign changes which rows link, in ways that count a fill or a dividend twice (5–7). None of this needs a different design; each is a stated rule and a test.

## CTO's three questions

1. **Are the five couplings all?** They are correct as far as they go, and every Matcher field the readers traced sits inside one of them. Four more are needed: a record's legs for ordering (8), saved groups at derivation (9), an adjustment's anchor row and a transfer that joins instruments, not only accounts (both 10). A succession is not a coupling at all; it is a change the log does not name (1), and it is the most serious gap.
2. **Triggers or writers naming changes?** Triggers. They fire in the writer's transaction on any connection, a rollback leaves nothing, and a raw connection that bypasses `Book` (the demo facts, the successions test) is still caught. The condition the plan misses: a trigger knows the row it fires on, but the engine's ledger is *derived* through joins (`COALESCE(j.into_id, …)` in `transactions` and `trades`, the canonical underlying in `option_terms`, the names fallback). The log must name every key whose *read* changes, not only the row's own (1, 11).
3. **Does depth need handling now?** In the gate, yes; in the code, not yet. The ×4 capacity book grows breadth only (`sized` multiplies accounts, instruments and trades together, so about four round trips per instrument at both sizes), and the probe picks an instrument by name, not the deepest. The growth rule therefore reads ≈1.0 whether the family match is linear or quadratic in its rows, and the 100 ms budget is held on a family a twentieth of the owner's deepest (349 rows). Measure depth (12); whether a family needs finer incrementality is then a number, not a guess.

## Required changes

1. **A succession re-points rows the log does not name.** `transactions` reads the instrument as `COALESCE(j.into_id, t.instrument_id)` (`records.rs:609–617`), `trades` the anchor the same way (`trades.rs:285–288`), `option_terms` the canonical underlying (`identity.rs:423–430`). `settle_successions` (`identity.rs:707–726`) rewrites only `instrument_joins` (and on a part, `derived_version` of contract records). The plan logs one `instrument` key for it and re-reads the instrument row. Failing scenario (the repository's own, `book/tests/successions.rs:223–234`): a buy under OLD and a sale under NEW, joined; an event row arrives and parts them. The log carries the event's record and the instrument keys; the engine keeps the sale on the joined head while a fresh build reads it on NEW: a "took 400 more than the book held" problem on OLD, 1,000 held on NEW against 600, wrong realized P&L, until a restart. Required: the `instrument_joins` trigger (and `option_terms`) logs a `record` key for every record whose transactions name either side, a `trade` key for every trade anchored on either, and an `instrument` key for both sides and every contract whose underlying is either; or the reader expands an `instrument` key that way. A join and a part in the property test's writes.

2. **The log's `seq` must be `AUTOINCREMENT`.** Without it SQLite gives the next row `max(rowid)+1`, and 1 in an empty table. The plan prunes every row at or below `MAX(seq)` at a start and keeps that `MAX` as the holder's applied seq. Failing scenario: 300 rows at a start, pruned; the person adds a trade; it gets seq 1; `WHERE seq > 300` reads nothing; the trade is in the book and in no figure, and so are the next 299 changes. Same after Clear data. `notifications` already uses `AUTOINCREMENT` (`v27.sql:315`). A test: start, prune, one write, the figure moves.

3. **One snapshot, applied under the lock, the watermark monotonic.** `pulled` reads before it takes the engine's lock (`figures.rs:420–428`) on an autocommit connection, so the log read and each keyed read are separate snapshots. Failing scenarios: (a) order read-back stores fill F (seq 8) and reads it live; the pull's `fills_give_way` supersedes F by B (seq 9), reads {8, 9}, applies first, watermark 9; read-back then applies its older read: F live again beside B, the fill on the book twice, and nothing names F again. (b) A reader reads record R, a pull moves trade T from R to R2, the reader reads T on R2: `identify` lists T unclaimed and `settle_trades` orphans it and cuts its note loose, in the book. Required: the log and every keyed read in one read transaction (WAL gives one snapshot), the applied seq taken inside it, applied under the lock, and a read whose base is older than the watermark re-read, never applied; say what happens to the log rows the engine's own writes under the lock append (`settle_trades`). A test with two writers racing on one record.

4. **The equality test must compare what the plan re-keys.** `Engine::differences` (`engine.rs:644–652`) compares trades, positions, cash rows, payers, equity, checks, benchmarks and `BookFig`; it never compares `Matched` (trips, books, units, roles, beyond, dust, arrived, last_price, cash, disagreements, unit_disagreements, unapplied, stock_dividends) or `Identity`, which are exactly what part B replaces by family. Failing scenario: a transfer link removed splits a family; the old family's cash share is not dropped; with no broker statement in the generated book `checks` is empty, `differences` is empty, the test passes, and the next cash statement shows a difference a fresh build does not. Required: compare `Matched` and `Identity` whole (both derive `PartialEq`), and have the generator write broker statements, saved groups, successions, a leg-unstated row and a multi-leg record on two uncoupled instruments.

5. **Linking must re-run each file row's own rule over every key a change touches, not over the rows it stored.** Today `link` re-examines every live file row on every import and pull (`csv_import.rs:325`, `broker_reads.rs:422`), so an ambiguity resolves when a competitor is taken, revised or removed. Failing scenarios: (a) F1 ambiguous over B1 and B2; F2 from a second file takes B2; today the next pull links F1 to B1; under "a pull that stored nothing links nothing" F1 stays live, the holding reads 15 units for 10, indefinitely. (b) A pull removes the duplicate candidate (`pull.rs:248`): it stored nothing, so F is never linked and the position is doubled. (c) A revision moves B2 to 4 units: the lookup under the new movement never finds F. (d) Looked up from a stored broker row's side, two identical fills stored together would link F to the first although today F is ambiguous. Required: an operation yields the movement keys it touched (every record stored, revised, removed or superseded, before and after), and for each key today's rule runs over the live file rows and free broker rows under it, file rows in the ledger's order (account, trade day, occurred_at, record, leg), feed tier before statement tier. The exclusion sets (`live_records`, `superseding`) are read by key, not whole.

6. **The index key must be today's three matches, not one.** Fills match on (account, trade day, instrument, kind, quantity) with the price per pair (`:370–371`); movements on an instrument on (account, kind, instrument, quantity, cash) with *any* of three day pairings (`:396`); cash moved on (account, cash) with *no kind* and two day pairings (`:412`). Failing scenarios: a file "withdrawal" of −50 CAD and the feed's TransferOut of −50 the same day link today and not under a kind-keyed index (the account's cash reads −100); a file dividend dated the pay day links today through the broker row's settle day and not under a single (trade day, settle day) key, so the dividend counts twice on the Cashflow tab and in the payer's rate. Each broker row indexed under each of its days, each file row looked up under each of its own.

7. **Hold linking equal to today's, and reword the count criterion.** The engine's equality test cannot see linking: `link` writes the book and both sides of the comparison read it. Criterion 132 ("a pull that stored nothing compares none") asserts the regression of 5. Required: an oracle test with today's whole-ledger `link` kept as the reference (as `compare.rs` keeps whole builds), over random imports and pulls (duplicates across files, prices at several places, rows stored, revised, removed, superseded), the supersede links and the ambiguous set equal after each step; the count criterion as "reads only the rows under the keys the operation touched".

8. **A record's legs order as one record.** `record_rank` (`:690–700`), `by_record` and the one-leg look-ahead (`:2214`) are over every leg of a record. A Wealthsimple corporate action before the person enters its adjustment has legs on two instruments no coupling joins (`mapping.rs:692–715`). Failing scenario (timed): record R with legs X −100 and Y +50 at instant T and a sale S of 100 X at T. Full match: R ranks 1 (a leg acquires) and takes the 100 units; S is beyond what was held. Family(X) alone: R's X leg ranks 2, ties with S, order falls to the source key; with S first the sale realises P&L. Required: compute `by_record`, `record_rank` and the one-leg test over the whole record from the held by-record map, iterating only the family's legs; or make a record's instruments a coupling. Say which.

9. **A saved group is rebuilt when any member's trip is.** `build_trades` pools every member trip's slices into one `TradeKey::Group` and suppresses those trips (`trades.rs:305–338`); members are any trade ids, on any instrument and account (`book/src/trades.rs:408–429`, `SPEC.md` trade-15). Failing scenario: G = {a trade on X, a trade on Y}; a sale of X is added; family(X) is rebuilt, G keeps its old P&L and slices, and if X's trip is emitted alone the Trades list shows it twice. Required: an index trip → group; a recomputed trip rebuilds every group holding it, with the `used` rule over all groups in book order.

10. **Three coupling rules made exact.** (a) A no-instrument row whose record carries `leg-unstated` taints every holding of its account from its day (`ledger.rs:1309–1317`, `:840–849`); the plan's "no instrument → the account's cash family, cash only" sentence contradicts its own fifth coupling. State that this row makes its account one family and is matched with it, and that the `record_problems` trigger (the problem row decides the coupling) recomputes families. (b) The adjustment coupling is the union of the anchor row's instrument and every leg's `from` and `to`: the anchor's `apply_event` writes the legs' books (`:1786–1799`), and a spin-off's parent is the person's choice, not necessarily the event row's instrument. (c) The transfer coupling joins the two sides' instruments as the engine reads them, not only their accounts: the moved lots land on the receiving transaction's own instrument, and nothing keeps a link's two sides on one instrument (a revision clears a record's transactions but keeps `transfer_links`; the statements linker pairs by cash and day). Failing scenario: a coin sent out as X and received as Y; the out row is revised; family(X) is rematched and the receiving account's holding of Y keeps the old lots. The plan's line 97 makes the fourth coupling span accounts only.

11. **Every table the read derives from has a trigger, found by the test.** `names()` falls back to `listings_named` and `instruments_described` for an instrument no live record names (`identity.rs:404–417`, `watched.rs:117–127`); neither is in the trigger list. And `work::statements_naming` (#505) counts statements naming a table you already know; it cannot list the tables a read touches. Required: add both tables (key `instrument`), and enumerate with rusqlite's authorizer (`Connection::authorizer`, `AuthAction::Read { table_name, .. }`, rusqlite 0.32 with `hooks`, already enabled), on a fresh connection so cached statements are prepared again, over `engine_inputs::ledger` and `facts().adjustments` on a book seeded with an instrument that has no live sighting; assert each table read has INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE triggers. Run it on a freshly migrated book so a later table rebuild that drops a trigger fails it.

12. **The gate measures depth.** Add a depth pair beside the breadth pair: the owner's accounts and instruments, with the probed listing carrying the owner's deepest row count, and its ×4 four times deeper; the add-trade and import probes target the deepest family. Judge it by the growth rule like the breadth pair. Then the plan's claim ("a twentieth of the book at worst") is measured, not asserted.

13. **Keyed collections keep every row and the order the page shows.** `beyond`, `unit_disagreements`, `stock_dividends` and `unapplied` hold several rows per transaction (one per event leg, `:1792–1799`, `:1949–1967`); keyed by transaction, all but one are lost, and the status page prints one sentence where today it prints two. Key them by (transaction, instrument), merge `unapplied`'s gaps per transaction, and keep the header's sentences in the match's order (by day), not the map's: record ids are v7 uuids, so a map orders by when a row was stored, and a back-dated import's sentence moves. And `trades`, `positions` and `cash` are presented in the engine's order (trades by last day then key, `trades.rs:339`; cash rows newest first; `Portfolio.positions` and `Cashflow.rows` are indexes into them): keep the sorted vectors as the presented order with a map behind them for replacement.

14. **The boundary criterion is held by a test that can see it.** `whole_reads` matches free-function calls only and excludes method calls by design (`tests_boundary.rs:213`, :258); `book.trades()`, `book.transactions()`, `live_records` and `superseding` are methods, so criterion 131 adding `transactions` and `trades` finds nothing. Either extend the scanner to `book.<name>(` with the allowlist by file, or hold the criterion by work counted (a one-row `record_changed` whose steps do not grow between the small book and four times it).

15. **The names cache follows the delta.** `pulled` drops it on every record change (`figures.rs:458`) and `Names::load` reads every instrument's and account's refs (`wire/build.rs:30–46`): O(book) per change, outside every probe's window. Maintain it from the delta (an `instrument` key reloads that instrument's refs, an `account` key that account's), or put the reload in a probe's window so the gate sees it.

## Accepted as is

The change log over writers naming changes; the delta replacing whole `Change::Ledger`, `Trades`, `Groups` and `Journal`; transactions held by record with an iterator in the record's order (`match_lots` sorts by its own key anyway); the five couplings as far as they go (every `Matcher` field traced lies inside one); per-(account, family) cash shares (exact at any size a book reaches); `build_positions`, `payer_rates`, `broker_checks` and `identify` by key; `Change::Closes` on an underlying by its family; `Engine::past` and the start's whole build unchanged; Clear's per-row triggers (slower, correct); other processes appending to the log (the server's re-read is idempotent); the day turning still a full match; the right to refuse.

## For the owner

Nothing to decide. Nothing on screen changes. Two of the faults the review found would have shown you wrong numbers until a restart: a ticker change leaving a sale unmatched, and an imported fill or dividend counted twice. Both are caught before any code is written.
