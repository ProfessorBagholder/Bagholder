# Brief 15: the app against the standard

**Reviewed:** `master` at `734221d7` (v2.0.7), the whole app: its structure and domain model, the data flow to the screen, storage, the code that moves money and reads sources, running it with and without Docker, the page as a person uses it, what it costs the machine, and the instruction files that steer the building agent.

**How:** sixteen reviewers, two per area with different lenses; each area then checked by two adversarial verifiers, one on the code (every cited line opened, every measured number re-taken where cheap) and one on the standard (is that really how the leading products and top teams do it; is it a deliberate tradeoff; is it the owner's decision; does the finding itself judge from today's configuration); then a completeness critic and six follow-up reviews on what it found missing, each verified the same way. 299 findings were raised; 35 were merged into another with the same root cause, 15 stand contested with the refuting verifier's reason beside them, and none was refuted outright. Every measurement was taken on the release binary; the follow-up on scale built a book of the owner's size (5,826 transactions, 331 trades, 28 accounts) and measured on it. The full findings, with the standard each is judged against, its evidence and its proposal, are in `briefs/15/` (one file per area) and are cited here by id.

**The question every finding answers:** would the best engineers building a trading journal have built it this way, in its structure, its behaviour, how it looks and responds, and what it costs the machine?

---

## For the owner

### The verdict

The migration built a sound core: exact money, a book of records nobody rewrites, one engine for every figure (its return arithmetic matches an exact recomputation to fifteen decimal places), typed routes with generated page types, per-screen subscriptions, order and bracket state machines with an event log. That part is what a top team would build, and it stays.

Around that core the app is not yet what a top team would ship as a trading journal, in six ways:

1. **The cutover was never finished.** About 30,000 lines of the old app are still compiled into the binary and still run the market readers, the chart and filings tables, and the order path. The product runs on two identity schemes, two Wealthsimple clients, two sessions and two stores at once. A second brokerage would touch the server, the book, the broker crate and the core, not "one adapter". Fifty of the sixty-four fields on a Wealthsimple row are never read, and a row the mapping cannot place vanishes from every screen without a word.
2. **The code that guards money has holes.** A crash during a sale from the ticket, a stop refused at the broker, a token refresh that fails, an order cap that trips, an entry that fills in parts, a limit sale that expires: each leaves a position with no stop and nothing watching it, and the panel does not say so. Two identical submissions are two orders. A journal note or a manual trade saved on one screen does not reach the others until something unrelated happens. A sign-in is written to disk and pulled against before Wealthsimple has accepted it, and "connected" is whichever of two unrelated loops wrote last. The sign-in trusts whatever answers a fixed local port as its own browser: a second copy on the same machine captured the first one's bank login, measured. `BAGHOLDER_DRY_ORDERS=true` places live orders. One oversized import, or the app's own 1.6 GB model download, can take enough memory to get the process that guards the brackets killed.
3. **It does not stay light as the book grows.** On the made-up book it is light today (28-40 MB, idle CPU near zero). At your size, measured: after a year of use the scheduler pins one core for as long as any page shows a price, adding a trade takes 36-40 seconds, and the app takes 15 seconds to open its port. The causes are patterns, not the machine: every quote pass re-reads the whole market cache once per holding; balances are appended every five minutes for ever and read back by scanning; every change wakes every loop; every database access opens a fresh connection; every broker row is its own disk flush; every fresh browser reads every trade's chart ahead (530 requests on your book); and at first start the app downloads a 1.95 GB language model into memory and runs it for life, unasked.
4. **The screen was fixed at the old page and never held to the category.** The equity curve and Monthly P&L are not drawn on a time axis (a quiet year is as wide as a busy week). The same trade shows two P&L figures on two screens, and the same holding two hold times. Open trades read a green "$0.00". Two positions in one symbol are indistinguishable rows. A deposit draws as a gain on the Value curve. The card titled "Annualized returns" lists returns that are not annualized, and the tile's subtitle says "Over 1 year" for a three-month range. The order ticket reaches Submit with no connection and no price. Nothing reflows below 1340 px. Nothing can be reached from the keyboard; dialogs are not dialogs. Errors show for four seconds in a header that scrolls away. Sentences a small language model wrote about a filing are shown, notified and handed to Claude with nothing saying a model wrote them. The journal is a thesis, a grade and tags nobody analyses: no setups, no calendar, no breakdown by tag.
5. **Headless and Docker do not work as advertised.** A headless copy answers only to the name `127.0.0.1`, so it cannot be reached from another device; publish the port and anyone on the network can place orders, watch the bank sign-in window including the 2FA code and type into it, point the app at any folder on the machine and import it, swap in their own Wealthsimple session, or wipe the book. There is no health check, no log file, no backup, no way to be told of a fill, a failed rollback leaves no starting app, the container runs as root with an unsandboxed Chromium, and the Linux archive needs a newer system than a Raspberry Pi OS install has.
6. **The process steered toward symptoms, and the gate did not catch it.** The instruction files fix the screen at the old page, say fixes need no plan, hold nothing to the category's products or to the machine's cost, still point at branches that no longer exist, and carry migration-era decisions with no end. No test runs at your size, so the two worst costs shipped invisibly. My own verdicts designed from today's configuration and you reversed three of them.

None of this is fatal and none of it is the technology. It is fixable in a bounded programme, ordered money first.

### What needs your decision

Each is a real choice. My recommendation is first.

1. **The standard replaces parity.** Close the migration-era decisions ("the work is the migration", "UI and UX stay at parity", "only what was asked", "nothing built for expansion"). From now the bar is: what the leading trading journals and brokerage screens do, and what a small machine can carry; a departure from that is a defect, and a refusal on scope grounds is one line to you, never a decision made in a plan. **Recommended: yes.**
2. **The screen changes in one batch** (section 4 lists them). They change what you see: charts on a time axis; one P&L per trade and one hold time per holding; open trades showing their unrealized P&L; holdings grouped by instrument with the account shown; the returns card and tile named for what they are, with the current year marked YTD; deposits marked on the Value curve; the ticket guarded; layouts that reflow below 1340 px; errors that stay until dismissed; keyboard and dialog behaviour; a System theme; first run that offers CSV and manual entry; fees shown apart from price; machine-written text marked as such; rows the app could not place listed for you to resolve; fund exposure showing the underlying holdings the readers already parse, so a stock held directly and through funds is one exposure. **Recommended: approve the batch; anything you want kept as it is, name it.**
3. **Remote access with a sign-in.** For a headless or Docker copy to be usable from another device the app needs to answer to a name you give it and to require a sign-in (a key printed at first start, kept as a cookie), with the routes that touch credentials, the book and updates behind it. The Wealthsimple sign-in on a headless copy becomes a hand-over from your own browser rather than a bank window streamed over plain HTTP. The desktop copy on your own machine stays as it is. This reverses the 2026-09-26 deferral. **Recommended: yes, since headless must work.**
4. **The local language model becomes opt-in.** Today every install downloads 1.95 GB at first start (the docs say 1.1), into memory, hashes it twice at every boot and runs an inference server for life, so that filings get one-sentence summaries. **Recommended: a setting, off by default, with the size stated; summaries appear once it is on, marked as machine-written.**
5. **Backups and export.** Today the README says "copy the folder" (unsafe while the app runs, and it copies your broker login), Clear data deletes the only snapshots, and the journal, the one thing that cannot be re-pulled, has no export. This reverses "no backups unless asked". **Recommended: a Back up / Restore action and a journal export.**
6. **Cost basis of a holding.** Today the holding's average cost is the FIFO residue, so after a partial sale it differs from Wealthsimple's own screen and from what the CRA uses (average cost). Trades keep FIFO for P&L, as every journal does. **Recommended: average cost for holdings; the Portfolio figures of partly sold holdings change to match the broker, and the broker's stated book value is reconciled against it.**
7. **Win, loss and expectancy scored in the trade's own currency**, with the CAD totals kept. Today a USD trade can count as a win on the Dashboard and a loss on its own page when the currency moved. **Recommended: yes.**
8. **The journal grows into a journal.** Setups and mistakes as tag categories, a monthly calendar, a breakdown by tag, and the stop and target the app itself placed carried onto the trade as its plan and risk (R-multiple). These are what the category's products exist for and the app lacks. **Recommended: yes, as its own stage after the fixes.**
9. **Portfolio under a date range.** Sharesight and IBKR show the positions held at the range's end; today the app shows holdings held at any time in the range, valued now. **Recommended: as the category does.**

Nothing else is yours. Everything below is engineering, and it goes to CTO as required work.

## What is right, and stays

- The book: source records kept as received with revisions, transactions derived by a versioned mapping, links instead of rewrites, the person's entries as records. (`docs/architecture.md` §6, built.)
- Exact money and quantities as decimals with currency; the page does no money arithmetic and formats exact text.
- One engine for every figure, pure, with 190 spec cases in 21 files written from `SPEC.md`. The time-weighted return it computes agrees with an exact recomputation from the daily values to 1e-15 (gap3, measured), and the chain's floating-point error cannot move a displayed digit.
- Typed routes with generated TypeScript; per-screen subscriptions; the page writes changes into the element showing them, held by element tests.
- Order and bracket state machines with an event log and a gate, tested against a misbehaving fake broker. (The holes in section 2 are gaps in the states, not the absence of a design.)
- The page opens on what it had (brief 13, as built): 1.06 s to figures on a first open of a fresh browser, 0.29 s on the second, nothing re-sent when nothing changed.
- Idle cost today on the made-up book: 28-40 MB resident, 0.00-0.03 s CPU over two idle minutes with no page or one page open (perf-A).

## 1. The cutover was never finished

- **Two engines, two identities, two stores** (arch-B-1, arch-A-2, storage-A-3, storage-B-5, storage-A-6). `market`, `model`, `store` and `ws` (29,752 lines) are compiled into the release and run news, filings, short interest, exposures, universes and chart bars, keyed by bare ticker and Wealthsimple security id, while the figures key by instrument id. A ticker change or a dual listing puts context on the wrong thing; a CSV-only or second-broker instrument gets no market context at all (`server/src/market_context.rs:199-214`). Daily closes are stored twice under two schemas; two listings of one ticker share one chart series (`sources/migrations/004:119`). The old `bagholder.db` is opened, re-migrated and written at every start and snapshotted beside itself.
- **Two Wealthsimple clients** (arch-B-3, arch-B-4, robust-B-2). Orders, quotes and read-backs go through the legacy `ws` client: outside the limiter, prices parsed leniently as floats, a bearer token re-sent to whatever host a redirect names (`ws/src/session.rs:462, 717`). The "read strictly" decision does not hold on the one path that moves money.
- **Broker specifics in the neutral layers** (arch-B-2, arch-A-1, storage-A-9, robust-B-7). The pull time is `America/Edmonton 14:00` as a server constant (`server/src/broker_reads.rs:33`); the coin minimum, account-type mapping and stop support are conditions in the server; orders and brackets are keyed by broker strings, not the book's ids; `wealthsimple` is named in 40 non-test files outside its adapter.
- **The mapping, read line by line** (gap2-1, gap2-5, gap2-8, gap2-4, gap2-3). Fifty of a row's sixty-four fields (fees, fxRate, withholdingTaxAmount, realizedPnl, the counter-currency amount, …) are never read and a value in one raises nothing. Three tables place one broker's vocabulary and disagree. The mapping raises seventeen problem codes and nothing consumes them: `Gap::RecordProblem` is declared and never constructed, so the first row of a kind your history never showed (an exercised long, a rolled spread whose fee makes the legs not net) drops out of holdings, trades and P&L silently (`mapping.rs:210, 383, 490`). Statement reconciliation runs only when cash already disagrees and reconciles cash movements only: a fill the feed already has gets its units corrected, a movement with no feed row that moves units without cash is never booked, and a month-end item in transit is reported as a movement no statement lists until the next statement arrives. A CSV re-import into a connected account double-counts every dividend and every row that is not a fill or bare cash (`csv_import.rs:227-262`), and a CSV import cannot bring in a coin or an event contract the book has not met: the row is kept unclassified and counts in no figure (gap2-V-3). Account-level fees, rebates and promotions count in no figure at all (gap2-V-1), and a cash kind the mapping treats as one-directional discards a stated sign that contradicts it and raises nothing (gap2-V-2).
- **A second monolith** (arch-B-6, arch-B-8, arch-A-8). `feeds.rs` is 4,161 lines holding eight subsystems with fourteen loop threads; the server crate is 34,702 lines and its boundaries are text scans over fifteen hand-listed files.
- **Engine correctness at the edges** (arch-B-9, arch-V-1, arch-B-7, arch-A-4, arch-A-7, gap3-3, gap3-8). An unknown instrument defaults to CAD and to a share; two record changes in flight can leave the engine on the older ledger; a holding can carry several open round trips and its note is the oldest lot's; the journal is the one table with no history, and an empty save erases a note for good; a split the broker's row does not state has no way in; a day on which one account has no stated value drops every other account's return for that day from the chain, silently, and an account whose statements stop ends the whole combined series on that day; one account statement in another currency stops the server at start rather than becoming that account's gap (the trigger is unverified: the request asks for CAD).

## 2. The code that guards money has holes

Each of these is a state the position can reach with no stop at the broker and nothing watched here, with nothing on the panel saying so:

- A bracket halted by the order cap keeps no stop and fetches no quote (robust-A-1, `core/src/bracket.rs:637`).
- A crash or kill during a ticket Sell leaves `closing-for-sale` for ever (robust-A-2, `orders/ticket.rs:876`).
- A limit sale from the ticket ends the bracket when the broker *accepts* it, not when it fills; a sale that expires leaves the shares unguarded overnight (robust-S-1, `orders/ticket.rs:933`).
- An entry that fills in parts arms nothing until the whole order ends: hours for a Day order, ninety days for GTC (robust-V-1 / S-2, `core/src/bracket.rs:786`).
- A token refresh that fails switches off the watched stops for up to thirty minutes (robust-B-1, `orders/tools.rs:92`).
- A refused stop's back-off also delays the emergency market sell, up to an hour (robust-A-6, `core/src/bracket.rs:602`).
- A stop the broker cancels ends the bracket silently, blaming the person (robust-V-2, `core/src/bracket.rs:820`).

And around them:

- A cancel whose answer was lost is never asked again: `cancelling` is a state nothing leaves (robust-A-3). "No such order" a second after an unclear send is final, and a bracket places its exit again (robust-A-4). One slow Wealthsimple call stalls every other bracket for up to 90 s (robust-A-7). The brackets poll the broker every five seconds around the clock, sessions closed or not: 17,280 requests a day to an unofficial API (robust-A-10).
- `POST /api/order` has no idempotency key: two identical submissions are two orders (robust-A-5, measured). The price the person confirmed is rounded after confirmation (robust-A-11). Prices leave the app as JSON floats (robust-A-12). Failures on the order path go to stderr only (robust-B-4). When the market-data read fails the ticket offers every order type, stop orders included (robust-B-10). A 401 or 429 is told to the person as "Wealthsimple rejected the order" (robust-S-4).
- **A sign-in is written to disk and pulled against before Wealthsimple has accepted it** (gap4-1, `server/src/session.rs:69`, `login.rs:575`, `broker_reads.rs:430`): the captured cookies are written to `session.json` before the refresh is posted, a transport failure during capture is memoised as a refusal and never retried, the broker loop pulls against the file, and `connected` is then whatever the pull pass or the token loop wrote last, two writers that do not know of each other; at the deadline the header replaces the real cause with "No session yet". Measured with a fake cookie and the token endpoint unreachable: the raw tokens on disk within 2 s, a pull of 0 rows, the connected flag flipping between the two writers. The browser may then rotate the single-use refresh token first, leaving the app on a dead copy.
- **The sign-in trusts whatever answers the fixed DevTools port 18765 as its own browser** (gap4-V-1, gap6-V-1, `login.rs:21, 549, 637, 1150`): nothing checks that the endpoint belongs to the child it launched. Measured: a second Bagholder on the same machine, its own data folder, captured the first instance's login into its own `session.json` within 8 s and pulled with it, and its Cancel closed the first one's window with a misleading message. Any local process or user bound to the port first becomes the window the person types the bank password and one-time code into.
- **A write to the book never reaches the stream** (dataflow-B-1, uiux-B-1): the book's connections carry no commit hook and the journal and entries routes do not signal, so a note, a grade or a manual trade is stale on every other screen until an unrelated change happens; measured: 0 stream messages after two journal edits, the patch arriving only after a later unrelated request. On an empty book "Trade added" leaves the same empty page.
- **`BAGHOLDER_DRY_ORDERS=true` places live orders** (ops-B-6 / A-2, `orders/mod.rs:109`): the one money switch honours only the literal `1`, while every other switch is on for any value. Measured.
- **No single-instance lock** (ops-B-3): two servers on one data folder each replace the same stop and book the same fill; the port roaming (ops-A-8) makes it easy.
- **The process that guards the brackets can be killed by memory** (gap6-3, gap6-4, ops-V-1 / S-1): twelve concurrent 31 MB imports took the server to 6.7 GB and 340 % CPU, every one timed out at 120 s and the work kept running after the answer was gone (measured); every upstream body is read whole with no ceiling, the 1.95 GB model included, and the client follows redirects to any host after the model's host allowlist was checked on the first URL only. On a Pi the kernel kills the process holding the live brackets.
- **A failed update after a migration leaves no starting app** (storage-A-1, ops-A-3 / B-2): the rollback restores the binaries and not the book, and the old binary refuses the new schema; the snapshot that would fix it is deleted by any Clear (storage-V-1).

## 4. The screen was fixed at the old page and never held to the category

Each of these changes what you see, so each is in decision 2. The product that shows the standard is named in the finding.

- **Charts without a time axis** (uiux-A-3): the equity curve places points by index (`web/src/lib/Dashboard.svelte:45`) and Monthly P&L drops empty months; measured: the hover at 25 % of the width is 2 Dec '24, at 50 % it is 10 Feb '25. The slope and the drawdown's length, which is what a trader reads them for, are wrong. **The Value curve draws the stated value alone** (gap3-5): a $60,000 deposit into empty accounts draws as a vertical rise indistinguishable from a gain, and the flow-adjusted index the drawdown is measured on is computed and never drawn.
- **The returns are never named** (gap3-1, gap3-2): the card titled "Annualized returns" lists calendar-year and partial-year period returns (the 2026 row is YTD, and under a date range a cut year is listed under its bare year); the "Avg annualized" tile under a three-month range shows the period return with the subtitle "Over 1 year" (measured: +0.16 %, "Over 1 year"; a range of four months, "Over 2 years"); the footer "Outperformed in N of M years" counts partial and cut years as years, and a benchmark whose levels begin after a year's start is measured from its first level, over a shorter span than the account, with no sign (gap3-V-2). IBKR and Sharesight name the method (time-weighted) and mark a partial year.
- **One trade, two P&L figures** (uiux-A-2): the Trades list shows the trade's own currency, the Dashboard CAD, neither labelled, and the column sorts by a third. **One holding, two hold times** (my own check): the Trades list says 946 days for the BTC position, the holding page 825, because the holding's Hold is the quantity-weighted age of its lots (`engine/src/positions.rs:202`) while `SPEC.md` says "the days so far". **Fees inside the price** (gap2-2): a single-leg fill's fee is never separated, so the trade's Fees reads 0 and its Entry carries the fee.
- **Open trades read a green "$0.00"** (uiux-A-4 / B-9), and By symbol shows "0 trades" beside a five-figure P&L; zero is coloured as a gain (`fmt.ts:175`).
- **Two positions in one symbol are identical rows** and identical Allocation slices, the account only on hover (uiux-B-6 / A-10). The Allocation ring is per position, so one name in two accounts reads as two holdings.
