### Wealthica (aggregator that reads Wealthsimple and other brokers)
- treatment: resets cost to market value (fetched the page)
- rule: Wealthica does not model transfers; it displays what the receiving institution reports. Wealthica's own post says that for stocks moved into Wealthsimple, some users saw the book value shown as the market value on the transfer date instead of the original cost. Wealthica added a toggle (Book Value vs Adjusted Book Value) and a manual override to correct it.
- quote: "transferring stocks from another institution to Wealthsimple resulted in the book value being shown as the market value at the time of the transfer instead of the original"
- url: https://wealthica.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/43671
- check: misquoted or changes meaning — The quote is on the page word for word. I checked this in the raw JSON of the post, "✍️ Wealthsimple Adjusted Book Value", after stripping the markup. The summary overstates the page in two places. (1) It says Wealthica added "a manual override" to correct the figure. The page describes only a toggle: "You can toggle the option in order to show the right one", with steps to Edit the institution, select "Use Book Value instead of Adjusted Book Value", save and re-sync. It mentions no manual override. (2) It says "Wealthica does not model transfers; it displays what the receiving institution reports". The page says nothing like that. It is the researcher's inference, not a statement in the post. Two smaller points: the page ends the quoted sentence at "the original", not "the original cost", though that reading is reasonable. The page also says the toggle "works for Wealthsimple Trade and Invest connectors". The core claim does hold: some users saw book value shown as market value at the transfer date, and a Book Value vs Adjusted Book Value toggle is the fix.
- notes: Post 'Wealthsimple Adjusted Book Value', dated 2024-06-06 (also at https://wealthica.com/blog/%E2%9C%8D%EF%B8%8F-wealthsimple-adjusted-book-value/). The wording 'some users have reported' makes it a vendor report of a symptom, not a stated rule. Workaround text: 'Use Book Value instead of Adjusted Book Value', then sync the institution; stated to apply to the Wealthsimple Trade and Invest connectors. Wealthica's earlier post of 2024-05-30 (https://wealthica.com/blog/%F0%9F%94%8D-adjust-your-book-value-manually-2/) says: 'This can be handy if you have transferred stocks from one institution to another' and 'the book value being reported by the new institution is not accurate', and offers a manual override. Inference: the reset originates in the broker's data (Wealthsimple), and an aggregator can only show it or let the user override it. Wealthica's help centre had no transfer article that I could find.

### Sharesight (Canada help site; transfers between a user's own portfolios)
- treatment: other (fetched the page)
- rule: No self-service transfer. The help page says transfers must be entered by hand: a 'Transfer out' trade in the sending portfolio, priced at the market close on the transfer date, then in the receiving portfolio either an opening balance or a buy, entered at the sender's original per-share cost base. The cost carries by hand. The receiving-side entry is dated the transfer date, so the acquisition date does not carry unless support moves the whole holding. Support can move the holding with its full trade history on request.
- quote: "Such transfers need to be manually updated on Sharesight."
- url: https://help.sharesight.com/ca/how-to-record-share-transfer-between-portfolios/
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page with WebFetch and again as raw HTML with curl. It returned 200 with no redirect. The quote is on the page word for word, in the intro: "Such transfers need to be manually updated on Sharesight." The steps match the summary. In the sending portfolio, the user notes the holding's cost base per share from the Summary tab. They add a trade of type "Transfer out", dated the transfer date, with the unit price set to the market closing price on that date. In the receiving portfolio, the user records either an opening balance (date of transfer, trade cost base = units x the sender's cost base per share) or a buy (trade date = date of transfer, unit price = the sender's cost base per share). Support (chat or email) will move the holding to another portfolio on request, and "All the transaction history associated with the holding will be moved to the new portfolio." One point is an inference, not page text. The page never says the acquisition date fails to carry. It follows from the receiving entry being dated the transfer date and from support being the only route that keeps the buy, sell and dividend history. That is a fair reading and does not overstate the page. "No self-service transfer" is likewise the natural meaning of "manually updated".
- notes: Other verbatim lines (same page, also fetched at https://help.sharesight.com/how-to-record-share-transfer-between-portfolios/): 'Trade type = Transfer out'; 'Unit price = market closing price on the transfer date.'; 'This is the sender's original cost base, which you will carry over to the receiving portfolio in Step 8.'; 'Trade cost base = Transferred unit * cost base per share from step 2'; 'Unit price = cost base per share from Step 2.'; and for the full-history move, 'All the transaction history associated with the holding will be moved to the new portfolio.' The page is titled Sharesight Canada Help but contains no ACB or CRA text and no tax rules; it does not say whether the Transfer out trade shows as a disposal in the CGT report. Staff statements on the community forum (fetched): Piumi_Sharesight, 21 Feb 2024: 'this feature is not available to our customers at the moment' (https://community.sharesight.com/t/holdings-transfer-between-portfolios/815); RickyYeo, 5 Dec 2023, apparently staff: 'we haven't got any plan to build this feature on the front' (https://community.sharesight.com/t/move-shares-between-portfolios/1460). A user, Eurotimmy (4 Jun 2023), said the earlier sell-and-buy workaround 'is an issue for my past tax calculations'. That is user testimony, not a Sharesight rule.

### Portfolio Performance (desktop, open source)
- treatment: carries original cost and date (fetched the page)
- rule: Models the move as a single Security Transfer that appears as a paired Transfer (Outbound) and Transfer (Inbound). It creates two open trades, source and target. Both start on the original purchase date and keep the original buying price. The date and quote price entered in the transfer dialog are used for performance tracking, not for the trades' cost or start date.
- quote: "Both trades are recorded as starting from the date of the original security purchase."
- url: https://help.portfolio-performance.info/en/reference/transaction/transfer/
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page with WebFetch and again as raw HTML. The sentence "Both trades are recorded as starting from the date of the original security purchase." is there word for word, in the Security Transfer section. The page says the transfer is "technically one transaction" that Portfolio Performance splits into two "virtual" transactions, a Transfer (Outbound) and a Transfer (Inbound), and treats "as a single entity". It says the transfer creates "two open trades", and that "both trades share the same buying price". It adds that "the date and quote price entered with the Security Transfer do not appear to be utilized in this process", meaning trade creation. The "Setting the quote price" section says the quote price is required for "performance tracking", and its worked example uses the 1 March transfer date in the IRR calculation. The summary is fair on all of this. It is slightly firmer than the page's hedge "do not appear to be utilized", and "original buying price" is the page's "same buying price" read in context.
- notes: Same page, verbatim: 'Additionally, both trades share the same buying price, although an argument could be made to use the quote price of the transfer instead.' and 'It should be noted that the date and quote price entered with the Security Transfer do not appear to be utilized in this process.' It also says 'Why can't you just transfer the shares from one account to another? The short answer is performance tracking.' and that without a quote the source account's performance is badly distorted. The page does not mention FIFO or purchase lots. The manual is phrased as an observation ('do not appear to be'), not a guarantee. Forum thread (user replies only, no staff or developer reply on the page; 26 Mar 2024): Sn1kk3r5 wrote 'In real life the original price is moved as well.' and 'The price you set here has no influence on the gain when selling.' (https://forum.portfolio-performance.info/t/why-is-a-quote-price-needed-with-a-security-transfer-transaction/27874). Delivery (Inbound/Outbound) is the separate no-cash 'appears by magic' type (https://help.portfolio-performance.info/en/reference/transaction/delivery/); that page does not say how it sets cost.

### Wealthfolio (open-source portfolio tracker)
- treatment: carries original cost and date (fetched the page)
- rule: Models transfers explicitly. An internal transfer is a TRANSFER_OUT on one Wealthfolio account paired with a TRANSFER_IN on another. The cost basis travels to the receiving account, and no gain is realised. A transfer in from outside Wealthfolio opens one new lot at whatever cost basis the user supplies.
- quote: "The receiving account inherits the original purchase cost, so your gain/loss calculations remain accurate."
- url: https://wealthfolio.app/docs/concepts/activity-types/
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page with WebFetch and again with curl (HTTP 200, no redirect), then searched the extracted text. The quoted sentence is there word for word, in the "Securities Transfers" section. That section says Wealthfolio preserves the original cost basis, with a TRANSFER_OUT on the source account and a TRANSFER_IN on the destination account for the same security and quantity. The summary matches the page on each point. (1) Internal transfers are paired: "paired with a matching activity on another Wealthfolio account. Cost basis travels between accounts." (2) TRANSFER_IN internal is "paired with TRANSFER_OUT from another Wealthfolio account, cost basis preserved", and TRANSFER_OUT internal is "cost basis exported to the destination Wealthfolio account". (3) External TRANSFER_IN is "opens a new lot at the cost basis you provide". One caveat: the page's literal words "no gain realised" appear only for an external TRANSFER_OUT. For internal transfers, "no gain is realised" is an inference from the cost basis moving over unchanged and "No new capital enters or leaves your tracked portfolio". That inference is sound and does not overstate the page.
- notes: Other verbatim lines: 'Internal: paired with TRANSFER_OUT from another Wealthfolio account, cost basis preserved.'; 'External: opens a new lot at the cost basis you provide.'; 'External: lots closed via FIFO with no gain realised (the asset leaves your portfolio).' Cost basis & Lots page (fetched, https://wealthfolio.app/docs/concepts/cost-basis-and-lots/): 'Both halves of the lot share the original cost basis.', 'Wealthfolio doesn't realize a gain; moving shares isn't selling them.' and, for transferred-in basis, 'your broker's "transferred-in basis" if you have it, or to the average cost as of the transfer date'. Release notes 3.0.5 (fetched, https://wealthfolio.app/changelog/3_0_5/): 'Internal transfers between accounts now carry over tax lots, so your original cost basis is preserved instead of being reset.' That implies older builds reset the cost. Caveats: the fetched text states cost basis explicitly but does not state the acquisition date for transferred lots (the lots model, with a purchase-date column, implies it but I did not find it said). The default method is FIFO, and the page says 'Until WAC ships, your Wealthfolio realized-gain numbers will diverge from a moving-average tax calculation', which matters for Canadian ACB. A user-filed GitHub issue dated 22 Sep 2026 (https://github.com/wealthfolio/wealthfolio/issues/1782) says in-kind transfers recorded at original cost leave a performance mismatch. It is not documentation and shows no maintainer reply.

### Fund Manager (desktop portfolio software)
- treatment: carries original cost and date (fetched the page)
- rule: Models transfers. A 'Transfer Between' dialog records one Transfer Out in the source investment and one Transfer In per lot in the destination, keeping each lot's tax cost basis and date. A Transfer In keeps the date of ownership and market value separate from the tax basis and its date. Transfers do not appear in the capital gains report.
- quote: "one Transfer In transaction for each lot transferred, to maintain the original tax cost basis value and date."
- url: https://fundmanagersoftware.com/help/dlg_transfer_btw.html
- check: misquoted or changes meaning — The quoted sentence is on the page (the fetch, a model-processed rendering of the page, returned it as "There will be one Transfer In transaction for each lot transferred, to maintain the original tax cost basis value and date."). The first part of the summary is fair: one Transfer Out in the source investment, one Transfer In per lot in the destination, original tax basis and date kept. The last two sentences go beyond the page. "A Transfer In keeps the date of ownership and market value separate from the tax basis and its date" is unsupported: the page has a Market Value field ("The value of the transferred shares") but never mentions a "date of ownership" and does not say market value is kept apart from tax basis. "Transfers do not appear in the capital gains report" is also unsupported: two separate fetches both reported that the page never mentions capital gains or reports. The nearest text is "in a tax-free manner", which is not a statement about a report. Those two claims may come from a linked page such as "About Transfer Transactions", but not from this one. Direct curl returned 403 from the proxy, so I relied on WebFetch only.
- notes: Same page: 'The total transferred out tax and OOP basis is equal to the total transferred in tax and OOP basis.' and 'Un-sold shares are transferred out using a first in first out method.' The page has a transfer Date field and a Market Value field ('The value of the transferred shares') and says nothing about performance. Definition page (fetched, https://www.fundmanagersoftware.com/help/def_transfer.html): 'Transfer In transactions allow you to differentiate the date you took ownership and market value' from the tax basis, and Transfer Out removes shares 'without generating a taxable event (an entry in the capital gains report)'. The claim that performance tracking restarts at the transfer came from a search excerpt only, and I did not find it on the fetched help pages. Fund Manager forum threads (2014 and 2018) with user reports of garbled basis came from search excerpts only.

### Sharesight (broker-to-broker transfer of shares the user keeps owning; Australian tax content)
- treatment: depends on account types or tax status (fetched the page)
- rule: For a plain broker change where the owner stays the same, the product says no entries are needed. The portfolio continues as one position with its full history, so the original cost and acquisition dates are preserved, and the page says there is no CGT event. If the beneficial owner changes, the page treats it as a disposal.
- quote: "Your Sharesight portfolio continues as-is — your full trade history, cost base, and acquisition dates are all preserved."
- url: https://help.sharesight.com/how-to-record-share-buybacks-share-transfers-and-broker-transfers-in/
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page with WebFetch and again as raw HTML. The quoted sentence is in the "Broker transfer" section word for word. The summary is fair, with two small caveats. (1) The page says "In most cases, you do not need to do anything in Sharesight", so "no entries are needed" drops the "in most cases" qualifier. The page does list cases where changes are needed: a change of tax entity, or a user who wants separate portfolios per broker. (2) On a change of beneficial owner, the broker-transfer section says "the ATO treats this as a CGT event" and then points to the rollover or CGT transfer steps "depending on whether a rollover applies". The "disposal" wording is in the Shares transfer section: "the ATO treats any transfer where the beneficial owner changes as a disposal (CGT Event A1)". That section also lists court-order and deceased-estate transfers as rollovers. Neither caveat reverses or overstates the rule for a plain same-owner broker change.
- notes: Same section, verbatim: 'In most cases, you do not need to do anything in Sharesight.' and 'As long as you remain the same tax entity (the shares are still in your individual name), there is no CGT event.' The fetch summary (not a verbatim quote) says: where the beneficial owner changes or there is a rollover, the receiving portfolio gets an Opening balance dated the transfer date with a total cost base equal to the sender's original cost base; if the transfer pre-dates Sharesight, use an opening balance when only the total cost base is known. The tax content is based on Australian law (ATO, CGT Event A1) and the page does not mention Canada. It is relevant here only as evidence of a tracker's rule that depends on tax entity.

### Quicken (Windows, personal finance software with a Canadian edition)
- treatment: carries original cost and date (fetched the page)
- rule: Has a dedicated 'Shares Transferred Between Accounts' transaction. Lots to move are chosen by the user, and if not specified, the earliest purchases move first (FIFO). The help page states that cost basis is transferred for securities that are set up correctly.
- quote: "Cost basis will be transferred for all securities that have been correctly set up."
- url: https://info.quicken.com/win/how-do-i-transfer-shares
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page raw with curl (HTTP 200, no redirect), as well as through WebFetch, and the quote is there word for word, in the page's Notes section. The summary matches the page on each point. (1) The page tells the user to pick "Shares Transferred Between Accounts" from the Enter Transaction list. (2) The user chooses lots through the "Transfer method" options, First Shares In or Lot Identification (Specify Lots button). (3) If the user enters nothing in the Specify Lots dialog, "Quicken assumes you're transferring the first shares that you purchased (known as First In, First Out, or FIFO)", which is the earliest-purchases-first default. (4) The cost-basis sentence is a general note. The researcher's wording that basis is transferred "for securities that are set up correctly" is a faithful reading of it and does not claim more, such as per-lot basis tracking or a Canadian-specific rule (adjusted cost base, ACB). The page does not say that Canadian ACB is handled, and the summary does not claim it.
- notes: Same page: 'In the Enter Transaction list, select Shares Transferred Between Accounts.' and, for the lot default, 'the first shares that you purchased (known as First In, First Out, or FIFO)'. Fetched page text states cost basis only. That original acquisition dates and per-lot cost carry (one Removed entry in the source, one Added entry per tax lot in the destination) is from Quicken community forum answers seen as a search excerpt only, not as fetched text, and is user and moderator testimony, not documentation. The same excerpt reports users hit mismatches in the Canadian average-cost case (the removing side used average cost, the adding side used each lot's original cost). I could not read the Canadian thread itself (https://community.quicken.com/discussion/7548659/share-transfer-changes-cost-basis-cdn-quicken-2017-home-business-for-windows returned only its title).

### Ghostfolio (open-source portfolio tracker)
- treatment: does not model transfers (executions only) (fetched the page)
- rule: Has no transfer activity type for holdings; the maintainer declined to add one. A move is not represented. Users either log a SELL in one account and a BUY in the other, or export the activities, change the accountId and re-import them.
- quote: "Currently, no. I don't see a big advantage that would justify the increase in complexity by introducing a new activity type."
- url: https://github.com/ghostfolio/ghostfolio/discussions/1899
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page twice with WebFetch, and both reads gave the quote. dtslvr, a maintainer, wrote it on Sep 15, 2023, answering elnigno's question "Is a new `TRANSFER` transaction type being considered?". That is a refusal to add a transfer activity type, with added complexity as the stated reason, so "the maintainer declined to add one" is fair. The word "Currently" makes it a decline for now, not permanent. The page also has dtslvr's Aug 2023 workaround: export the activities, change `accountId` in the JSON, and re-import, or edit the database directly. A user (ups7, Mar 2026) suggested logging a SELL in the old account and a BUY in the new one. That user noted it loses the original history and cost basis. A second user shared a script that remaps `accountId` values and re-imports. Later commenters kept asking for a transfer type, and no maintainer reply after Sep 2023 appears on the page. The summary's claim that users either do SELL/BUY or export, change accountId and re-import matches the page. A direct curl of the page returned 403 from the proxy, so I could not check the raw HTML. WebFetch's markdown conversion is what I relied on.
- notes: Maintainer dtslvr, 15 Sep 2023, answering whether a new TRANSFER type is being considered. Earlier maintainer comment, 18 Aug 2023: 'If it's just a one-time thing, you could export your activities, modify the accountId in the JSON file'. Neither comment mentions cost basis. The search excerpt said community members describe the sell-and-rebuy workaround as discarding cost-basis history and possibly distorting performance charts; I did not verify that in fetched text. A 2026 comment about a user script that keeps cost basis is also from the search excerpt only. A search of the API spec (excerpt only) listed the activity types BUY, DIVIDEND, FEE, INTEREST, LIABILITY, SELL. The official FAQ (https://ghostfol.io/en/faq) returned only its title when fetched.

### RBC Direct Investing (Canada), online Transfer Stock between own accounts
- treatment: depends on account types or tax status (fetched the page)
- rule: In-kind move between personal non-registered accounts with no change in ownership is done at book value. A change in ownership is done at market value. A move into a registered plan (TFSA, RRSP, FHSA) is a contribution at market value and may be a deemed disposition. The rule is keyed to ownership and registered status, not to the position's history.
- quote: "A transfer of stock allows you to transfer shares directly from your personal investment account to your TFSA, RRSP, FHSA or other personal non-registered account, without having to sell the securities (known as an in-kind transfer). Your contribution amount is the market value of your securities at the time of transfer. Contributing securities to your registered plan may result in a deemed disposition based on any capital gain/loss that may be triggered. When transferring securities between your personal non-registered accounts, if there is no change in ownership, the transfer amount is the book value. If there is a change in ownership, the transfer amount is the market value of the securities at the time of transfer."
- url: https://www.rbcdirectinvesting.com/learn/en/di/reference/article/transfer-funds-faqs/iw8kfpr8
- check: confirmed — I fetched the page two ways, with WebFetch and with a raw curl (HTTP 200) whose HTML I stripped of markup. The quoted passage is on the page word for word, as consecutive sentences in the "Stock Transfers" section. The next sentence is "Please consult with a professional tax advisor if you require tax advice." The summary is fair. Book value applies to non-registered to non-registered with no change in ownership. Market value applies when ownership changes. A move into a registered plan is a contribution at market value and "may result in a deemed disposition", which the summary renders as "may be a deemed disposition". The last sentence, that the rule depends on ownership and registered status and not on the position's history, is the researcher's inference, but the page states no history-based condition. The deemed disposition depends on the gain or loss that results, but that is a consequence and not a condition for choosing book or market value.
- notes: This is the clearest Canadian statement found for a broker's own rule. The page footer is (c) Royal Bank of Canada 2025. It says the transfer is not a sale.
