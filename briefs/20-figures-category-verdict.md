**Reviewed:** `docs/plans/figures-category-practice.md` at `265c3e31` against `master` at `30dd7039`: `engine/src/positions.rs` (the book as Σ lot value), `engine/src/ledger.rs` (`apply_transfer`, lot primitives), `engine/src/scope.rs` (`outcome`, `kpi`, the by-symbol rows), `engine/src/fx.rs::rate`, `wealthsimple/src/read.rs` and `broker/src/pull.rs` (what is stored of a position), the recorded positions replies, `sources/src/adapters/yahoo.rs` (closes), `SPEC.md` §1, §5, §6 Portfolio. Sources re-read 2026-10-04: Wealthsimple's help centre (through its JSON API), Tradervue's P&L reporting page, IBKR's TWS P&L page, Sharesight's portfolio page, CRA's pages on identical properties (through search excerpts of canada.ca).

## Verdict

Go with changes. Decisions 6 and 9 are right and well sourced; decision 7 is a sound choice but not "as the category does it", and the plan should say so. Nine required changes below; none alters what the owner sees beyond what the three decisions already do.

## Sources, checked

- **Average cost (6): confirmed, and better sourced than the plan has it.** Wealthsimple's own article (4409775037083): "Your cost base/share hasn't changed, it's still $10/share. This is always the case for any sales you make." Its returns article (37274145854875): "Unrealized Return ($) = Current market value - Book cost". CRA: the average is the total cost of identical properties purchased, including the expenses of acquiring them, over the number owned. IBKR: average price = "your cost (execution price + commission)" over the position. The plan can drop "summary only" for Wealthsimple.
- **One claim has no source:** "IBKR shows the same split between TWS and its statements". The cited IBKR page does not mention FIFO, average cost or any difference from statements. Remove it.
- **Win or loss (7): the sources point the other way, honestly stated in the plan.** Tradervue (3425): "For trades settled in other currencies, the reports will use the converted P&L in the base currency to aggregate and compare performance across all trades"; its blog says winning and losing trades are aggregated in the base currency. No journal documents scoring a trade in its local currency. The plan's own survey says this; what it then calls "as the category defines" is not.
- **Past range (9): confirmed.** Sharesight: values at "the current price or the closing price of the selected end date"; IBKR's open-position summary is as of the period's end. Neither page says what happens when the end date is not a trading day.

## Required changes

**Decision 6, a holding's cost**

1. **Fees are in the average**, as at CRA and IBKR, with an engine case that has a commission on a buy.
2. **One place changes lots, and the average lives there.** The plan keeps "the average per position as lots arrive; a sale removes cost in proportion" beside the lots. Lots are changed in several places (`open_lot`, `take`, `close`, the corporate-event adjustments, transfers, option expiry and assignment); a second running figure that each must remember to update is how two figures drift apart. Derive the average from the same primitives that mutate the lots, and add an invariant test over generated sequences that includes transfers, splits, return of capital, stock dividends, option expiry and assignment and a short: the units the average divides by equal the open lots' units, and total cost equals Σ lot value minus cost removed. A short's average is what it brought in, and an option's is premium per contract: one case each.
3. **A transfer between two of the person's accounts: the position continues across the same registration, and is a disposition at market value across a registered boundary.** Corrected 2026-10-08 after the owner asked how the category handles it (an earlier version of this item accepted the mismatch; the version before that said every transfer resets the cost). Thirty-six sources read and every quote re-fetched (`briefs/20/transfers.md`):
   - **Trading journals model no transfer at all.** Tradervue, TradeZella, TradesViz, Trademetria, CrossTrade, Edgewonk and Chartlog see executions only: a moved position is keyed in by hand with whatever cost the person types, or the import starts from a flat account. The category gives no rule here; Bagholder modelling a transfer at all is already beyond it.
   - **Every tracker and broker that models a move between a person's own accounts carries the original cost and date.** Portfolio Performance ("both trades are recorded as starting from the date of the original security purchase"), Wealthfolio ("the receiving account inherits the original purchase cost"), Fund Manager (one transfer per lot "to maintain the original tax cost basis value and date"), Quicken ("cost basis will be transferred"), Sharesight (a broker change: "your full trade history, cost base, and acquisition dates are all preserved"); Interactive Brokers ("the original cost basis is maintained in between IBKR accounts"), Schwab (lots move with their basis), Fidelity ("adjusted cost basis and holding period will accompany the transfer"), RBC Direct Investing ("processed at the average book cost" between sole accounts in the same name), Wealthsimple ("transferred at their book cost" between accounts of the same type); US law requires the transfer statement to carry basis and acquisition date (26 CFR 1.6045A-1).
   - **A move across the registered boundary is a disposition at fair market value, by law and at every Canadian broker.** CRA: an in-kind contribution to a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA "is considered disposed of at fair market value (FMV) at the time of the contribution" (a gain is taxed, a loss is denied); an RRSP investment moved to a TFSA is "withdrawn … at the fair market value"; a direct transfer between two accounts of the same kind is neither a withdrawal nor a contribution. Questrade: Margin to registered, "you will be considered to have sold the security at fair market value … and re-purchased it in your registered plan"; FHSA to Margin, "a new adjusted cost basis that equals the market value". RBC: into a registered plan, "your contribution amount is the market value". Wealthsimple: "the asset's book cost in the new account is updated to reflect its current market value".

   The ledger moves the lots with their cost and dates in every case (`apply_transfer`: "the lots move with their cost and dates"), so it is right for the first kind and wrong for the second. Required: a transfer between accounts of the same registration continues the round trip as today; a transfer across a registered boundary (unregistered to registered, registered to unregistered, or RRSP to TFSA) closes the round trip in the sending account at the value the broker's row states for the move and opens a new one in the receiving account at that value, on the transfer day, with the day's Bank of Canada rate; no stated value is the existing `BasisUnknown` gap, waiting for the person's cost, never a made-up price. The engine already knows each account's registration (`core/src/account.rs`, `Registration`). `SPEC.md` §1 states the rule with the sources; the realized figure on the sending side is the economic result and the spec says the tax treatment of a denied loss is not the journal's. Engine cases: each pair of registrations, with and without a stated value. The acceptance check "Avg equals Wealthsimple's book value ÷ units" then holds for every holding, transferred or not, and the Right to refuse is not triggered. Nothing changes on the owner's screens today, since the book holds no transfer of shares; the rule is built so that it is right when one arrives.
4. **Read the broker's stated book value, which the app drops today.** The positions reply carries `bookValue` and `marketBookValue`; `wealthsimple/src/read.rs:137` and `broker/src/pull.rs:352` store `book_value: None`, though the statements table has the column. The acceptance check above needs it. Read it strictly (which of the two is the cost, and in which currency, settled from the recorded replies) and store it. No new header sentence about cost: not now (decisions 5 and 8, 2026-10-04).
5. **A partly sold position's realized and unrealized parts are on different bases, so no figure may add them.** With buy 100 at 10, buy 100 at 20, sell 100 at 30 and the price still 30: FIFO realized 2,000, FIFO remainder 100 at 20 so unrealized 1,000, total 3,000; average cost realized 1,500, unrealized 1,500, total 3,000; the plan's pair is 2,000 (the trade) and 1,500 (the holding), which sum to 3,500. The holding page shows the open trade and its unrealized P&L together. State in `SPEC.md` that the two are the category's own figures on their own bases and do not sum for a partly sold position, check every screen and export for a sum of them, and add the property the plan already asserts in words: **for any sequence that ends flat, Σ realized under FIFO equals Σ realized at average cost.** That test is the one that catches a wrong cost rule in the new code.

**Decision 7, win or loss**

6. **Say what the basis is.** The choice stands, but on this ground only: the owner's rule puts per-instrument figures in the instrument's own currency and a trade's page shows its P&L that way, so its Win or Loss must read from that same figure (the alternative, scoring on the converted P&L as Tradervue's aggregates do, would put a green +$50 under a "Loss"); and the attribution standard and Sharesight separate local return from currency return. Write that in `SPEC.md` and the plan; do not write "as the category does it". This is my own error carried over: brief 15 presented it as settled by the category, and it is not.
7. **One definition of a win.** Three places decide it on `pnl_cad`: `outcome()` (`scope.rs:174`, the **Result filter**), `kpi` (`scope.rs:511-540`) and the by-symbol rows (`scope.rs:602`). The plan names two. All three go through one function on the trade's own-currency P&L, and one test holds them together: for every filter state, the Dashboard's win and loss counts equal the lengths of the Trades list under Result = Win and Result = Loss. The rare trade (won in USD, down in CAD) adds its negative CAD amount to Gross W; `SPEC.md` says Avg win and Profit factor use those sums as they stand and are never clamped, with one case.

**Decision 9, Portfolio under a past range**

8. **The close "of the last day" is the last close on or before it.** The plan says a day with no close is the gap; under that, a Saturday or a holiday as the range's end makes every instrument a gap. Use the walk-back the engine already uses for today (`positions.rs:99`, `range(..=today).next_back()`) and the Bank of Canada rate by the same walk-back as `fx::rate`; a gap only where no close exists on or before. No source says what Sharesight or IBKR do here, so the rule is stated in `SPEC.md` as the engine's own convention. Splits are not a concern: the Yahoo adapter stores each close as traded, so units as of the day and its close are on the same basis (checked).
9. **No figure on the page mixes dates.** The plan names Avg, Book, Market and P&L. The Portfolio also shows Net asset value ("as Wealthsimple states it now"), Margin used and Available margin (stated now), Cash, 1d change, and the Last and Change columns, which are live. Under a past range each is either as of the range's end (Net asset value from the account's stated value that day; Cash from the book as of that day; Last the close; the day change that session's move) or `—`, and the plan lists which. Say what a row's page and its Buy and Sell do under a past range (recommended: the page opens as of that day and offers no ticket). The cost of replaying the ledger to the end day is measured at the owner's size, beside the close reads, and cached by end date until the book moves.

## Accepted as is

The three definitions; FIFO kept for trades' P&L and CAD totals kept; "include sold positions" left out as a later feature; the three `old-app-mistakes.md` entries; the generated average-equals-cost-over-units test (extended per change 2); the blind engine cases; the browser test and screenshots at the four widths; the surfaces to check (the heatmap's holdings; `SPEC.md` §1, §5, §6).

## For the owner

Nothing to decide. One thing to know, because it was put to you as settled: for decision 7 the leading journals do not settle how a trade in another currency is scored. The plan keeps a trade's win or loss in its own currency, because that is what its own page shows; the totals stay in CAD. It only matters for a trade the currency moved against by more than the trade's own gain.
