# Brief 19: verdict on the stage 1 plan, "the money" (#348): Go with changes

(CTO asked for brief 18; 18 is the reserved-cash source brief already published, so this is 19.)

**Reviewed:** `docs/plans/stage-money.md` at `366ed241` against `master` at `4fc87ccf`. Read against the code: `core/src/bracket.rs` (`place`, `arm`, the exit-ended branch, `RETRY_SECONDS`), `server/src/orders/{ticket,brackets,gate}.rs`, `server/src/status.rs::unread_rows`, `sqlite/src/lib.rs` and `sources/src/cache.rs` (the commit hook), `server/src/events.rs` (the bell), `sqlite/src/migrate.rs` (snapshots), `server/src/clear.rs`, `SPEC.md` (*Arming*, *Failures*), the Cargo manifests. Sources: Interactive Brokers' attached-orders and TWS API bracket pages, Alpaca's orders page, NinjaTrader's ATM help, SQLite's `wal_hook` and `commit_hook` pages (all read 2026-10-04).

## Verdict

Go with changes. The plan covers every hole in brief 15 §2, in a sensible order, with a test per state and the owner's three one-liners put to them before building. What is wrong is narrow and specific: one owner decision is argued from sources that do not say what the plan needs, one is a rule that will leave positions unguarded in a way the plan does not see, and four of the parts have a design flaw that the stated tests would not catch. Eleven required changes, below.

## The owner's three one-liners

1. **A rejected stop is not resent on a timer.** Right in kind, wrong in its classes. The plan sorts refusals into "answered" (not resent) and "no answer" (resent), and puts "the shares" in the first. A refusal because the shares are committed to another open order (the owner's account today has an open sell on 500,000 units) is answered and goes away when that order ends. Not resending means the stop never reaches Wealthsimple, and a position guarded only by this app stays unguarded if the app is off. Required change 1.
2. **A stop from the first partial fill.** The direction is right and the plan's sources do not support the framing: the Interactive Brokers page says only "will not be submitted until the parent order fills" and its TWS API page is silent on partial fills; Alpaca says "completely filled" for the entry and is silent on a partly filled one. So neither is a rule the plan departs from; the question is open at both. NinjaTrader's own ATM help says all stop and target orders "will be automatically updated to reflect the new position size" when scaling in, and its forum documentation says the strategy's orders are initiated on a partial or complete fill and amended as fills arrive. Cite those; drop the "departs from IBKR and Alpaca" argument. Required changes 2 and 3 are what arming on a partial fill needs to be safe.
3. **Every record problem reaches the header.** Yes, and the existing sentence (`unread_rows`: one per account, counts by reason, the first day) is already the right form for it. Required change 11 keeps it that form.

## Required changes

1. **Classify refusals by whether the same request can succeed later, not by whether the broker answered.** Three classes: *invalid* (a tick, a decimal count, an order type not supported: the request is wrong; told once, never resent, watched here); *depends on state* (shares committed to another order, outside the session, a price outside the band that the quote's move can cure): re-asked on the event that changes the answer (the working-order list changes, a holding changes, the session opens, the quote crosses), never on a timer; *no answer* (transport, timeout, 401/403/429): resent when the cause clears. An answer of a shape not on record is *state* with a bounded number of event-driven retries, then watched here and said. The only rejection on record is a tick error, so the classing is from one case: the plan's own Right to refuse covers it, and the first release after it must show a table of every refusal answer seen and its class, from the order log.
2. **Arming while the entry is still working needs three states the plan does not list.** (a) *The stop fires while the entry still works:* the entry is cancelled at once, or the position buys more behind a stop that has already sold. (b) *The stop's size follows the fills:* changes to its quantity are coalesced (at most one modify per interval and per material change), never one per fill, and each is a modify, not a cancel-and-place, so no moment is unguarded. (c) *The entry ends:* the stop's size is final, and it is checked against the filled quantity once. One test each in `core/tests/brackets.rs`.
3. **Recorded as an owner decision with the accurate sources**, per change 2 of the verdict above, in `docs/decisions.md` when built. `SPEC.md` *Arming* already says "or ended with a partial fill": the new rule is stated as a change to that sentence.
4. **The signal after a commit must come after the commit.** Part E registers the cache's hook on the book's connections. That hook is SQLite's `commit_hook`, which by its documented veto ("if the callback returns non-zero, the commit is converted into a rollback") runs before the commit completes; the repository's own comment says the same ("it runs inside SQLite's commit"). The bell wakes a stream which then reads on another connection, possibly before the commit is visible, and then nothing signals again until the next change: the stale screen this part exists to remove, appearing on a slow disk. SQLite's `wal_hook` is documented as invoked "after the commit has taken place and the associated write-lock on the database released". Use that, or signal from `atomically` after `commit()` returns. The server test of "every writing route produces a stream message" must run with the write delayed (a hook that sleeps before returning, or a throttled filesystem) so a signal-before-commit fails it. This applies to the cache hook as well; fix both.
5. **`serde_json`'s `arbitrary_precision` is a workspace-wide switch.** Part B proposes it for exact decimals. Cargo unifies features across the build, so enabling it for the order path changes how every `serde_json::Value` number is parsed in every crate, and it is known to break `flatten`, untagged and internally tagged enums on numbers. Use `serde_json::value::RawValue` (feature `raw_value`) for the price and quantity fields only. The recorded web-app request stays the golden.
6. **The import slot is taken before the body is read.** "One import at a time with flat memory" holds only if a second import is refused (HTTP 409 and the page's own wording) before its body is accepted; with the slot taken after, twelve 31 MB bodies are still in memory waiting. The measurement the plan promises (twelve concurrent, flat memory) is the test, with the requests starting at the same instant.
7. **The ticket with a failed market-data read.** "Shows nothing to choose" blocks the person from selling when only an optional read fails. Keep the order types last read for that security (the broker's own answer, marked as of when), offer those, and refuse what was never read; if none was ever read, offer only market and limit and say stop types could not be checked. The failure is said in the ticket either way. This is the 2026-09-30 decision ("refuses what it cannot send"), not a departure from it.
8. **A repeat submit while the first is in flight.** The plan answers a repeat with the first result; a repeat that arrives before there is one returns "in flight" with the same id, never a second send. The page also disables Submit from the click to the answer.
9. **The captured sign-in's refresh can be consumed without a reply.** Part C retries transport failures and treats only `invalid_grant` or 401 as refusal. A refresh whose response was lost may have been consumed at Wealthsimple: the retry then answers `invalid_grant` though the broker accepted it. The code must tell the two apart (a refusal before any retry, versus after a lost reply) and say so to the person plainly ("sign in again"), never "refused".
10. **The sign-in browser's fixed DevTools port comes into part C.** Brief 15 put it in stage 5; the repository's own verification recipe makes it a stage 1 problem: `CLAUDE.md` has a scratch copy run beside the live app on one machine, and a second copy on port 18765 captured the first copy's login in the earlier measurement. Take a free port (or a pipe), and accept only an endpoint that belongs to the launched child (read from its own `DevToolsActivePort` file); fail the sign-in if the port is taken. The headless sign-in stays in stage 5.
11. **Snapshots are transient, not kept and cleared.** Part D clears the cleared kinds inside each snapshot. Cleaner and standard: a pre-migration snapshot exists only until the new version has started and answered, and is then removed, so at rest there is none for a Clear to find; a Clear while one exists clears inside it. Keep `snapshots/` bounded by `prune` as it is. (Decision 3's header sentence also stays aggregated: counts per reason with the first day, as `unread_rows` does; the list screen stays out, as decided 2026-09-30.)

## Open question 4, answered

The two candidates in the plan are "carry the account's last stated value with no flow" and "chain that day from the accounts that stated". The first puts a value on a day the broker never stated, drawn into a chart: the owner's rule is nothing synthetic. The second needs a weighting for a day with some accounts missing, which is a method of its own to invent. The method that invents nothing is the standard one for a composite whose members are valued on different dates: **measure every account's return between the same pair of dates.** Take the days on which every started account states a value; chain the combined return over consecutive such days, each account's return over exactly that interval taken from its own two stated values (and its net deposits on those two days). In the example that opened this finding (A: Mon 100, Tue 110, Wed 110; B: Mon 100, Wed 100) the combined days are Mon and Wed, A is +10 %, B is 0 %, the combined is +5 %, with no value invented. The code today forms each account's return between that account's own consecutive stated days instead of the combined days, which is what drops A's Tuesday. An account whose statements stop leaves the weighting at its last stated day and the gap is said. Read GIPS' composite provisions for this before building, as the plan says; I expect them to agree.

## Accepted as is

The build order A to F; parts A, B, C, D and E's bodies other than the points above (the halted bracket still fires; `closing-for-sale` recovery; a ticket sale ends the bracket only when it fills; the token check on a live access token; a cancelled stop told as a problem; an unclear cancel asked again; "not found" inside a window; each bracket on its own task with a 10 to 15 s timeout; brackets rest outside the session with the venue calendars held as sourced data and the quote's `marketStatus` as the override; the id minted at Review; the price on Review the price on the wire; 401/403/429 "not sent"; every failure an event on its order; sign-in kept in memory until accepted; one writer for "connected"; one boolean parser and the start line; the data-folder lock; the asked-for port; per-route body limits; a ceiling on every upstream body checked while reading; the rollback restoring the stores); all acceptance criteria, with these additions: one case per state in change 2; the commit-then-signal test in change 4; the simultaneous-imports measurement in change 6; the type-control cases in change 7; the lost-reply case in change 9; the foreign-port case in change 10; the combined-return case written blind from the method above.

## For the owner

Two of the three one-liners are yours, in their plain form below (the plan's wording is long). Nothing else.

1. **A stop that Wealthsimple refuses.** If the refusal can never succeed (a wrong price step, an unsupported type), the app tells you once and keeps watching your stop level itself, selling at market the moment the price crosses it. If it could succeed later (shares tied up in another order, the market closed), the app places the stop again when that changes, not on a timer. A send that got no answer is repeated as soon as the connection or sign-in is back. Recommended: yes.
2. **A stop from the first partial fill.** An order that fills in parts today has no stop until the whole order ends, which can be hours or up to 90 days. Recommended: place the stop for what has filled and grow it with each fill, as trading platforms that manage this for you do. Interactive Brokers and Alpaca's pages do not say what they do here.
3. **Every problem with a row from Wealthsimple is told in the header**, in the same sentence you have now (how many, why, the first day). Recommended: yes.
