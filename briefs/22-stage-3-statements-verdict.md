## Verdict

Go with changes. The design is the standard one: a Type 2 period per distinct value, the newest row found through an index, each change handler reading the key it was told about. `store_account_days` (`statements.rs:193`) already keeps a day this way, so the plan brings cash, buying power and net value to a rule the module already has. The plan says "the period's last moment is after the read" is what answers the 2026-10-09 decision, and that is right; what it does not say is which of the period's two moments the engine is given, and the broker check turns on it (1). Two more findings change what reaches the engine or the screen (4, 5); the rest keep the migration and the tests from passing while wrong.

## Required changes

1. **`Stated.cash.0`, and so `BrokerAccount::as_of`, is the period's `last_us`.** Today it is the newest row's `stated_at` (`statements.rs:402`, `engine_inputs.rs:125`), which after folding is `first_us` ("`stated_at` stays as written: the first moment"). The check (`equity.rs:122–126`, :146–148) is `pending = as_of > activity_read_at` and `confirmed = cash_read.is_some() && as_of > activity_read_at && cash == cash_read`. Failing scenario: the broker states 100 at t1; the activity is read in full at t2 > t1; the broker states 100 again at t3 > t2; the book's cash is 90. Today: a row at t3, `as_of` = t3 > t2, `confirmed` true, the difference is said. With `as_of` = `first_us` = t1: `confirmed` false for as long as the cash stays 100, the difference stays pending, and `pending` reads false where it read true. Say it in B, and hold it with a unit test of exactly that sequence through `stated` and `broker_checks`; the "every field equal before and after" criterion catches it only on a book whose folded runs straddle an activity read, which the generated books must therefore contain.

2. **The period store is observationally equal to the row store for every order of instants, held by a property test.** `stated` today orders by the parsed instant, then rowid (`statements.rs:394–400`); the plan folds "in the order written" and extends `last_us` to "this moment". Two scenarios break it. (a) A book with rows A (t5, 100) then B (t3, 100) written in that order and an activity read at t4: today `cash_read` is B (100, at t3); folded by rowid, the period is one row with `first_us` = t5 (or `last_us` < `first_us`), so no period has `first_us` ≤ t4 and `cash_read` is none. (b) The clock set back: the newest period's `last_us` is t10, the broker states the same cash at t9; "moves forward" to t9 moves `as_of` back, and `pending` flips. Define the fold and the store on instants, not rowids: a run is consecutive in `(instant, rowid)` order, `first_us` its least and `last_us` its greatest instant; a statement whose instant is not after the newest period's `last_us` is placed by its instant (extends the period it falls in or beside, else a row), never moved backwards. Then hold the whole of it with one test: random sequences of (instant, cash, holds), non-monotone and with ties, stored through today's row-per-read code and through the period code, `stated` equal for every activity-read instant in the sequence. That test replaces guesswork about which orders occur; `demo_facts.rs` and `capacity build` already write instants that are not "now". Say what two different net values at one instant do: `store_net_value` is `INSERT OR REPLACE` on `(account_id, stated_at)` (`statements.rs:286`), which writes over today.

3. **A re-statement is equal only when its holds are equal, NULL-safe, after `store_cash`'s nulling.** `store_cash` inserts the holds (:236–241) and then nulls the amount of each whose order has a fill (:245–264). Compared before that, or with SQL `=` (NULL is never equal to NULL), a statement carrying a filled order's hold never folds, so the accounts with working orders, the active ones, keep today's growth. Compared without the holds, it folds a statement whose holds moved, and `cash_read_holds` (:407) is the first statement's: the check subtracts a hold that no longer stood when the cash was stated (`equity.rs:137, :149–151`) and says a difference that is not there, or hides one. Compare the hold sets with `IS`-equality (`EXCEPT` in both directions does it), after the nulling, and add to the folding test a run whose only change is one hold's amount becoming NULL.

4. **`Change::Broker` recomputes one account's check, and the `brokers` probe measures `broker_changed`.** `engine.rs:549` sets `self.checks = broker_checks(&self.inputs, &self.matched)` for every account, and each account's check walks every ledger transaction (`equity.rs:114`). The plan moves the read to one account and leaves this, so a balances pass costs accounts × (accounts × transactions) in the engine every five minutes while a page is open. The `brokers` probe (`capacity.rs:471–476`) times `engine_inputs::brokers` alone, which after this plan is a startup path, so the job cannot see the cost the cadence pays for: at four times the owner's size it is sixteen times the work per account, and the growth rule, which is the gate (brief 21, item 2), would not be asked. Scope the check to the account, as `build_equity(…, Some(a))` on the line before already is, and make the probe time `broker_changed(account)`, read and apply. The acceptance line then reads "meets the growth rule and its budget", in that order.

5. **A pull's ledger and its statements reach the engine in one write.** Today `record_changed` applies the ledger and then each differing account's `Change::Broker` under one lock and logs one version (`figures.rs:416–446`, `moved` :273). The plan takes the statements out of it and has the pull call `broker_changed` per account: two or more versions, the lock released between them, and the feed thread reads `moved_since` between any two (`events.rs:507`). Failing scenario: a pull reads a fill (the ledger's cash −100) and the balances stating it. After `Change::Ledger` alone, the check compares the new ledger with the old `cash_read` and reports a cash difference; after `Change::Broker` it clears. A feed step between them sends the difference, which then goes: what 2026-10-09 forbids, and a regression from today. The pull's report carries the accounts it wrote a statement or an activity read for; `record_changed(now, touched)` reads `stated` for those only and applies them in the same write as the ledger. `broker_changed(account)` stays for the balances read alone (`broker_reads.rs:499`), where no ledger change rides with it. Held by a test: a pull that writes rows and statements advances `Figures::version` by one, so no feed step can read a state between them; it is listed under the 2026-10-09 decision in `docs/decisions.md`.

6. **The "every writer is followed by `broker_changed`" test is an oracle, not a list.** Writers today: `pull.rs:188, :193, :248` (activity reads), `:264` and `:706` (net value), `:374` (units), `:427` (days), `:744` and `:770` (cash, buying power); `broker/src/statements.rs` writes records, not statements; `clear.rs:145` rebuilds; `demo_facts.rs` writes before the build. A list goes stale with the next writer. Hold it instead as: after each of `pull`, `pull_activity` and `balances` in the server's tests, the engine's `inputs.market.brokers` equals `engine_inputs::brokers(&book)`, the whole read kept as the test's oracle. With 5, that is one assertion in three places.

7. **Units statements: define their `first_us`, and fold them or say why not.** `statements` holds `kind = 'units'` rows with `stated_at` NULL by its CHECK (`006:47`); the plan adds `first_us INTEGER NOT NULL` to the table, so the migration fails on any book with a units statement, which is every real one. Either the columns are nullable with `CHECK ((kind = 'cash') = (first_us IS NOT NULL))`, as `stated_at` is, or a units row takes its read's instant. And a units statement is one row plus a line per holding on every full pull and every pull a cash move triggers (`pull.rs:374`, `broker_reads.rs:303`), read newest by `ORDER BY as_of_day DESC, rowid DESC` (`statements.rs:419`), which the plan's index on `first_us` does not serve: it is the next grower of #417's class. Fold a units statement equal line for line to the newest (as 2 and 3 define equal), index `(account_id, kind, as_of_day)`, or measure it in the PR's table and say why it waits.

8. **Every instant the broker check hinges on moves to the integer, and the text is never read again.** The plan keeps `stated_at` and `broker_reads.at` as text beside `first_us` and `at_us`: two forms of one fact, written by every writer. `last_read` (`statements.rs:88`, the pull and balances cadences at `broker_reads.rs:282–324`) and `activity_read_at` (`statements.rs:316`, the check's hinge) stay `MAX` over text. jiff prints no fraction when it is zero and trims trailing zeros otherwise (`…:45Z`, `…:00.001Z`; its `Timestamp` docs), so `…:00Z` sorts after `…:00.001Z` (`Z` is 0x5A, `.` is 0x2E) and `MAX` can name an earlier read: issue #442, in the two queries the plan's new index is for. Add `at_us` to `activity_reads` too, read `MAX(at_us)` through the indexes, and either stop reading the text (written from the same instant as the integer, kept for the eye) or hold the two equal on the migrated books.

9. **A balances read does not re-read the account's day history.** `broker_changed(account)` calls `stated`, which reads every row of the account's `account_days` (`statements.rs:371–380`): one per day the account has existed, parsed every five minutes per account while a page is open, though a balances read never writes a day (`store_account_days` runs in the history step, `pull.rs:427`, and returns the days it changed). This grows with the book's age, which the capacity job's four-times book (more accounts, the same days) does not vary, so the job cannot see it. Read the days when the history step changed one; the balances path reads cash, buying power, net value now and the activity read. §13: "work is done because something changed … only that work is done".

10. **The migration as written cannot run: add the columns with a default, or rebuild the tables.** SQLite: "If a NOT NULL constraint is specified, then the column must have a default value other than NULL" (`lang_altertable.html`). Add with a default and fill, or rebuild through a new table and rename, which the runner supports (`foreign_keys` off for the migration and `foreign_key_check` after, `migrate.rs:191–203`); commit `schema/v26.sql` for `book/tests/schema.rs`; the folding's deletes take `statement_cash` and `statement_holds` with them (the plan says so) and the check after the migration holds nothing dangles. The pre-migration snapshot of a 1.8 GB book is a copy of the file on an SD card: the PR states what it took on the owner's copy.

11. **The boundary test names the whole readers' only callers.** `figures::build` (:60), `read_sources::engine` (:167), `compare.rs:268` and the capacity `startup` probe read the market and every account whole, rightly; the test fails on any other caller of `market`, `market_from_cache` or `engine_inputs::brokers`, the change handlers included.

## Accepted as is

A row per distinct value with its first and last moment, the moments between in `broker_reads`; the three indexes on `(account, [kind,] first_us)`; the newest by greatest `first_us` with rowid as the tie; `cash_read` as the latest period with `first_us` at or before the read (its moment is read nowhere but the before/after equality, `engine_inputs.rs:136` drops it: fine, say so or drop the field); the keyed cache readers, whose keys the cache's primary keys already lead with (`quotes (instrument_id, source)`, `daily_closes (instrument_id, day, …)`, `quoted_ex_dividends (instrument_id)`, `benchmark_closes (benchmark, day, …)`), `quote_of` taking the instrument's kind from `book.instrument(id)` rather than every instrument; `account_days` unchanged; no engine type changed; the determinism digest before and after; the owner's copy migrated and counted; the `old-app-mistakes.md` entry; the right to refuse.
