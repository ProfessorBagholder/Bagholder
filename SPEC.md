# Bagholder specification

This file is the authority on what the app shows and how each figure is computed, on every platform. Any change to a screen, whether it comes from a design handoff or from code, is checked against this file before it is committed. If a change needs a definition here to be different, this file changes first and the reason is recorded in the commit.

It defines every figure and every screen by itself: what each figure means, how it is computed, where it comes from, its currency, format and layout, and when it refreshes. No implementation is its reference; code is held to this file, never the other way round. The web page shows the figures the server computes and computes none of its own (no matching, no aggregation, no currency conversion), the one exception being dividing an annual figure by twelve to show it per month. Sections 2 and 3 define the figures for every platform; sections 4 to 7 describe the web page; section 8 describes the phone.

## 1. Principles

- **Accuracy over convenience.** A number on the page is either exactly what this file defines or it is not shown. A blank is rendered as an em dash.
- **Payout frequency is the payer's own, as a source states it; never worked out or assumed.** Each held payer's record of its distributions (§2, Distribution rate) is read when the payer is first held and again from a week before its next distribution is due by the schedule it states, once a day until that distribution is listed (once a week where no source states a schedule). A figure that needs a schedule no source states waits on it, named.
- **Currency.** Every figure that belongs to one trade, position, fill or distribution is in that instrument's own currency, with no prefix and no conversion. Every figure that adds trades or distributions together is in CAD, converted on the date of each fill or payment with the Bank of Canada's rate for that currency (every currency the Bank publishes; the previous business day's on a weekend or holiday); a value held today uses the Bank's latest published rate. The rate's date is shown nowhere; a Bank not read past 16:30 on a business day is a failed source in the header, as every failure is.
- **Time.** Times of day are in the viewer's local time; days, months, years and "today" are in the zone of the browser in use. Each page states its zone when it opens; the server uses the latest zone stated and keeps it for when no page is open. A date a source states as a date (ex, record and pay dates, a daily value's day, a close's session) is never converted through a zone; only moments are. The server's own zone is never used. The app is for Wealthsimple users, so CAD is the home currency and is never labelled: a currency code appears only in the FX column of a per-instrument row, never on a tile, a total, or as a prefix on an amount.
- **Raw data is never rewritten by Bagholder.** Wealthsimple activity rows are stored as received and never edited here. When Wealthsimple revises a row of its own (a dividend it files as a zero-cash placeholder on the record date becomes the paid dividend on pay day, under the same id), the stored copy is replaced by Wealthsimple's current version, keeping its id and so its journal. Everything derived is recomputed from the rows.
- **Nothing is added that was not asked for.** Nothing is on screen that the owner did not ask for; beyond that there is no rule against text. Browser tooltips (`title` attributes) do not belong anywhere on the page; wherever a hover tooltip is needed it is the app's own styled tooltip, with one look everywhere.

## 2. Definitions

### Trade

A trade is one round trip: a position in one account and instrument from its first fill until it is flat again. Lots are matched first-in first-out within that account and instrument; a symbol another security reuses is another instrument. Its id is Bagholder's own, given at its first fill and kept for good: a correction to the rows behind it keeps the trade and its journal. While any of it is held the trade is **open**, a position with no sale yet and a partly sold one alike, its P&L what it has realized so far (0.00 before a first sale); once flat it is **closed**. Each sale's P&L is realized on the day of that sale, whether the trade is open or closed. A sale of more units than the book holds closes what is held at its own cost; the units beyond have no cost on record, so they add nothing to P&L (none is made up for them, and they are never a short), and the broker check lists them. A round trip that goes flat without a sale (a position sent wholly out by a transfer) is not a trade, and neither is one held in an account the broker manages: its buys and sales are the broker's, so it is in no trade list, journal or trade statistic, while its holdings, cash, income and value count as every account's do. A saved group is open while any of its members is open.

- **Options.** Contracts are matched per contract. Only a multi-leg order's legs are a roll: the far leg is opened in the same trade and the whole chain, from the first short to the last buy-back or expiry, is one trade named after the last contract. Separate orders, even on the same day, are separate trades; a saved group joins them, as it does any trades. A contract past its expiry with no row from the broker closes at zero only when its underlying closed out of the money that day; at or in the money it waits for the broker's row (`no-expiry-record`), since OCC exercises a contract $0.01 or more in the money unless told not to, and only the broker's row says what happened. Assignment closes the option at zero, keeping the premium, and delivers the shares at the strike into their own trade. A contract's size is the one its terms state, never assumed to be 100.
- **Crypto.** Matched like shares. Staking rewards open a lot at zero cost and carry a `reward` flag. A transfer in is a deposit of an asset, not a buy made here: its lot costs what the person states it cost them, else what the source states the units were worth as they arrived (Wealthsimple states it in CAD on every coin moved in), and its later sale is a round trip of its own (never merged into a bought one), flagged `deposited`, scored like any other. Only an arrival for which neither is stated is `basis-unknown`: its sale stands on its own and is left out of the performance figures rather than scored on a made-up cost. A transfer out is not a sale: the coins come off the open lots first-in first-out at their cost, with no P&L, and the transfer is not a fill of the trade.
- **Splits.** Taken from what the broker's rows state (the units before and after, or the ratio the record carries) and applied to the open lots on the split date; never inferred from fill prices.
- **Moves between your accounts.** A holding moved from one of your accounts to another keeps its trade, cost and dates: it is neither a sale nor a buy.
- **Manual and imported trades** are records like the broker's and are matched the same way. A trade entered with no account goes to the Manual account. A contract entered by hand is named by its terms and has no size stated, so what depends on its size waits on it. `Import CSV` and `Load folder` read three layouts, each known by its headers: Wealthsimple's activity export (`transaction_date`, `activity_type`, `activity_sub_type`, `net_cash_amount`), its statement export (`date`, `transaction`, `description`, `amount`), and a plain one (`date`, `action`, `symbol`, `quantity`, `price`, `amount`); any other file is refused, naming its headers. Rows are read strictly: a day only as `YYYY-MM-DD`, a number only as digits with an optional sign, `$`, thousands commas and parentheses for a negative, a currency only where the row or the instrument states one. A row that does not read, or whose kind no layout places, is kept with its problem and counts in no figure; the report lists it by line. Each row goes to the account chosen in the dialog (Manual unless another is chosen), or to the account it names by the broker's id; one naming an account the book does not hold is kept with that problem. The same row imported again, from the same file or an overlapping export, is the row already held. A row that is a fill the broker has reported — the same account, day, instrument, side and quantity, and the price to the places the file states it — is linked to the broker's row and not counted twice, when there is exactly one such row; with more than one, nothing is linked and the report says so. A row imported before the broker's arrives is linked when the pull brings it.

Per-trade fields, all in the trade's currency unless stated:

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| Open | Earliest entry date among the trade's lots |
| Close | Latest exit date, once the trade is closed; an open trade's reads `Open` |
| Symbol | The instrument; for a rolled chain, the last contract |
| Exchange | Listing venue from the security record (TSX, TSX-V, CSE, Cboe Canada, NYSE, NASDAQ, NYSE American, NYSE Arca); `Crypto` for crypto |
| Qty | Units opened (shares, contracts or coins) |
| Entry | Quantity-weighted average entry price over the units opened |
| Exit | Quantity-weighted average exit price over the units closed so far; none before the first |
| FX | Currency code, CAD or USD |
| P&L | Realized so far: sum over the closed units of (exit − entry) × qty × multiplier for longs, reversed for shorts, minus entry and exit commissions |
| P&L % | P&L divided by the entry basis of the units closed (entry × qty × multiplier) |
| Hold | Calendar days from Open to Close, or to today while open |
| P&L (CAD) | The same P&L with each leg's notional converted on its own fill date; equals P&L for CAD trades |
| Grade, thesis, tags | Journal entries keyed by the trade id |

### What you enter

Two facts no feed carries are yours to state, each from its own record, as trackers such as Sharesight take them:

- **The cost of units that arrived.** Units moved in from another broker or a wallet cost what the source states they were worth as they arrived, where it states it; that is not always what they cost you. You can state what they cost you, in total in the instrument's currency, and the day you acquired them; they become the holding's lot at that cost and day. Entered in Add trade as an opening balance, against the arrival it prices.

**Add trade** (the menu): Date, Account (your accounts, or Manual), Symbol (a contract as `LUNR 15JAN27 12.00 CALL`), Side, Quantity, Price and Currency, Fees; Enter in any box adds it. A symbol the book holds in that currency is that instrument; one it has not met is a new one. While units that arrived without a cost wait on theirs, a `Trade · Opening balance` switch sits at the top, and Opening balance takes the arrival (its symbol, account, units and day), its total cost in the instrument's currency and the day acquired. What the server cannot read or stand behind is said under the form in its words, and nothing is kept.
- **What a corporate event did to cost.** For a spin-off, the share of the parent's cost each new holding takes, as the issuer published it; for a return of capital, the capital returned per unit of a distribution, as the issuer published it, which lowers the cost of the units held on the day the distribution is paid, the day its record carries (as Sharesight records it, on the day paid); units sold before that day keep their cost. Capital returned beyond what is left of a lot's cost is realized that day and the lot's cost is then zero. Entered on the trade page, beside the journal, while the event waits on it.

Each shows as entered by you. A value a source states for the same fact replaces yours; two entries of yours that disagree leave the figure waiting on which is right.

### Position

An open position is the lots still held in one account, instrument and direction, named by those three. Its journal is the trade's that opened it, so a note written on the position is the note of the trade it becomes.

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| Qty | Units held |
| Avg cost | Cost ÷ (qty × multiplier) |
| Book | Sum of lot qty × price × multiplier |
| Price | Live quote from the listing's own feed, refreshed every minute while a page shows it (TMX Money or Cboe Canada for shares and ETFs, Coinbase for crypto in the position's currency, and for a US-listed option the bid/ask midpoint while both are quoted, else its last trade, else its previous close); otherwise the last price read, which is kept with its day, no date printed; otherwise none (`—` and its word). Your own fill price is never shown as the market price |
| Market | Qty × Price × multiplier |
| P&L | Market − Book for longs, Book − Market for shorts, with the percentage over Book |
| Hold | Quantity-weighted days since each lot was opened |
| Allocation | Book ÷ total Book of all positions in scope |

### Distribution

A cashflow row is one Dividend, Interest, Withholding tax or Interest charge activity. Only Dividend rows feed the Cashflow page's totals, chart and holdings. Each row keeps its native amount and a CAD amount converted on its date.

### Distribution rate for a holding

Every held payer's distributions come from a record of them, read whole: its own company's publication where a reader exists for it (a company states a schedule change first), otherwise its market's record, the exchange's for a Canadian listing (TMX) and Yahoo's dividend events for a US one. A company reader that fails to answer is a failed source, shown as one and read again; it is never replaced by the market's record. A company whose publication does not carry the payer does not serve it, and the market's record is read.

1. **Per-unit amount:** the cash per unit of the latest distribution gone ex. A part reinvested, as the record states it, pays no cash: a distribution paid wholly in units is passed over. A market's record states an amount per unit and not whether it is paid in cash or in units, so the account's own record says which: a dividend paid on the payer between that ex-date and the next makes it cash; no dividend posted two business days after its pay date, in an account that held the payer the day before its ex-date, makes it units (a distribution paid in units that are then consolidated posts nothing). Until one of the two shows, the latest distribution's form is unknown and the rate waits on it (`form-unstated`).
2. **Payments per year:** the schedule the record states, never worked out from the gaps between dates and never assumed. Where no record states it, annual income waits on it (`schedule-unstated`).

Annual income for a holding = per-unit amount × payments per year × qty.

### Equity and returns

- **Equity series.** Each account's daily net liquidation value as Wealthsimple states it, and beside it the net deposits it states (money put in less money taken out, to date); a day's deposits and withdrawals are the change in those net deposits since the account's stated day before; none is known for the first stated day, nor for a day where either of the two states no net deposits. All accounts combined, a day counting once every account that has begun states a value for it, or one account when the account filter selects exactly one.
- **Yearly return.** Daily returns net of deposits and withdrawals, chain-linked over the calendar year. A day's return is its value less that day's deposits and withdrawals, over the value of the stated day before, less one: money moved in or out counts at the end of the day, so it earns nothing that day. A day whose deposits and withdrawals are not known forms no return. A year is measured from 1 January, over the value of the last stated day before it. A balance under 1 % of the account's all-time peak is pre-history: a year that never clears it is not shown, and a year in which the account first clears it part way through is measured from that first point, so the first real deposit is never read as a return. The S&P 500 is measured over the same span as the account's year.
- **Index.** The S&P 500, the S&P/TSX Composite or the S&P/TSX 60, each as a total return in CAD, the same kind of return as the account's: an ETF that tracks the index (SPY for the S&P 500; XIC, which tracks the capped Composite, for the Composite; XIU for the 60), each session's return its close over the previous session's close less the dividend going ex that session (a split that session counted back: close × new units ÷ old units), a USD tracker converted at each session's Bank of Canada rate. Measured over the same span as the account's year, from the tracker's last session before it.
- **Avg annualized.** Yearly returns compounded over the days they cover, counted from the day each year is measured over (the last stated day before it, or its first point clear of pre-history), and annualized only when those days make a year or more: a shorter span shows its return over the span, since only a period of a year or more is annualized (GIPS; Sharesight). Years shorter than 30 days are skipped.
- **Max drawdown.** Largest peak-to-trough fall of the flow-adjusted equity index, so money moved in or out is neither a gain nor a loss. Reported as a percentage, the CAD equivalent at the peak, and the trough date; an index that never falls has a drawdown of 0 and no trough date.

Each declared record carries its own fetch stamp, separate from the quote's, so the quote loop keeping a price fresh never makes the fund's distribution history look fresh.

**One refresh of a kind at a time.** Every background read of a source — quotes, market data, news, the heatmap universes, the chart archive — runs alone: a second call while one is in flight returns at once instead of queueing behind it. The page's own request may ask for a quote read when it finds prices stale, but only when none is running and not again until that kind's cooldown has passed (a minute for quotes, five for market data), so a source that is slow, rate-limited or down costs one attempt on its own clock rather than one per page load. Without this a request started its own reader, every reader dropped the cached model when it finished, and the next request rebuilt the model and started another reader: on a small machine the readers outlived the requests that made them and the app wound down over hours until nothing was left for the page.

**One connection per source.** Reads and the chart's GraphQL calls go over a connection kept open per host rather than a new one each time. For a request this small the TLS handshake is the whole cost: 7.7 ms of processor time against 1.1 ms on a connection already open here, and far more of the difference on a small machine, paid on every quote every minute. A proxy in the environment sends the request back through urllib, which knows how to reach one. The archive's own pace follows from the same measurement: each pass is timed, the batch grows on a machine that swallows it and shrinks on one that labours, and the loop then rests as long as the pass itself took, so the backfill never takes more than half of one core and a quick machine still finishes its sweep in seconds.

**Source outcomes.** Every request to a source records its outcome, and that is what the chart's empty state reports (see the trade chart). Nothing about sources appears in the menu. A symbol a source does not carry is not a failure of the source.

A USD transaction is converted at the Bank of Canada rate for its own date, looked up from that table when the model is built. The table is append-only, so a transaction's CAD value never changes once its day's rate is in. The Bank publishes a day's rate at 16:30 Eastern; it is read at that moment, so a USD trade made during the day is converted at its own day's rate from that afternoon. Before that it uses the latest earlier rate.

### Versions and the update check

A version is a GitHub release tagged `vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`; commits alone are not versions, and the desktop and the phone apps carry one number (how a release is cut is in `CLAUDE.md`). The running version is shown in the header as small muted text to the right of the Bagholder wordmark; on the web nothing about versions appears in the menu. At every start, and every hour while running, the app asks GitHub for the latest release; when its tag is newer than the running version the header shows, beside the version at the top left, an "Update to vX.Y.Z" button when the app can install it itself, else an "Update available" link to that release.

**Updating from the page.** `python3 bagholder.py` (at the repository root, or in an unpacked web archive) runs a small supervisor whose only job is to run the server as a child in the same console and start it again when it exits asking to be restarted. Pressing the update button: a git checkout on a clean master does `git pull --ff-only`; any other copy downloads the release's web archive (`bagholder-vX.Y.Z-web.zip`) and its `.sha256` from GitHub, checks the checksum, compiles every Python file in it, keeps the current files under the home folder's `previous`, and puts the new ones in place. The Rust port does the same with its own archive, `bagholder-vX.Y.Z-rust-<target>.tar.gz` (`.zip` on Windows), and in a git checkout builds the pulled sources with `cargo build --release --bins` in `rust/` before restarting, putting the previous commit back when the build fails. The header reads `Downloading…`, `Installing…`, `Restarting…`; the server then stops, the supervisor starts the new one on the same port, and the page reloads itself when it sees a new version answering. If the new server dies within twenty seconds the supervisor puts the previous files back (in a git checkout, the previous commit and the executables built from it, kept before the build) and starts that version again, and the header says the update failed. The button refuses while a sync is running, and a git checkout with local changes or on another branch is asked to pull by hand. Only a press of the button starts any of this. A release carries the archive and checksum as assets, so the process needs nothing but the GitHub release. The request carries nothing but the app's version in its user agent; a failed check, or no release yet, is silent.

### Notifications

Bagholder tells the person what happened while they were not looking through the system's own notifications, under its own name and icon: a banner, and an entry in the notification centre, the way any app on the computer does. Every event starts on the server, which is what keeps watching while the page sits in a background tab or is closed: one row per event in the store's `notifications` table, keyed so the same event is never told twice, the newest two hundred kept.

**How a row reaches the person.** Where the computer running Bagholder has a desktop, the server posts the row itself, and the banner is Bagholder's: on a Mac through a small app of its own, `Bagholder.app` in the home folder, compiled once from a two-line script with the system's own `osacompile` (rebuilt when the script, the app's address or the icon changes) and carrying the app's icon, so the banner and the permission prompt say Bagholder rather than a browser, a site address or Script Editor, and a click on the banner opens the app in the browser; on Windows through a toast under an app id registered, in the person's own registry hive, with Bagholder's name and icon, a click opening the app; on Linux through the desktop's notification service (`notify-send`) with the name and icon. The system asks once, the first time, whether Bagholder may notify, as it does for any app. A row posted this way is stored seen, so no page shows it as well. Where the server has no desktop (the container, a headless machine, or a second copy told to stand aside with `BAGHOLDER_NOTIFY=browser`, which a scratch copy on the same computer must be), the page shows the row through the browser's Notification API, under the browser's own permission and framing, once: a row a page has shown is marked seen (`POST /api/notifications/seen`) and no page shows it again, two pages open at once collapsing the same row into one banner, the row's id being its tag; a row made while no page was open is not raised later, it waits in the history. Clicking a banner brings the page forward, and for a fill or an order problem opens the Orders panel on the tab that carries it, for a disclosure the instrument's page. The status payload's `notify` carries the kinds, `native`, the channel the server posts through (`mac`, `windows`, `linux`) or empty where the page must, and `unread`.

**The history.** Every open page keeps the history whichever channel showed the banner: the header carries a Notifications button, the bell, between the Orders button and the filter button, with a badge counting the rows not yet looked at; it opens, over the page in the ticket's frame (400 px, the right edge, a scrim; Esc, the scrim and the × close it), the list under the heading `History`, newest first, the last fifty of the two hundred the store keeps: a card per row in the Orders panel's card grammar, the title on the first line with an accent dot before it while the row is unread, the body under it, when in the foot: **when the thing happened** — the release's own moment, the filing's own day (`Today 7:34 AM`, `Sep 8, 3:12 PM`, `Sep 8` for a filing, which carries a day and no time) — never when the app got round to telling it, which is the app's business and not the reader's; `Nothing yet.` when there is none; a body of several lines keeps them (a release's figures sit under its headline). Closing the panel is having looked: every row reads read (`POST /api/notifications/read`), and the badge clears; a row that arrives while the panel is open is read at once. `Clear`, at the right of the heading while there are rows, empties the history (`POST /api/notifications/clear`). **Every card opens the thing it is about**, since a card that answers a click with nothing is a card that should not have looked clickable: a release opens the item where it is published, in a new tab, as the News card's rows do; a disclosure opens its document the way the Disclosures table opens it (the regulator's own page for a SEC filing, the app's reader for a SEDAR+ one); a fill or an order problem opens the Orders panel at the tab it belongs to; a row carrying only a ticker opens that listing's page, held or merely watched. A row from an older version that carries neither a link nor a ticker does nothing, and is the only kind that does not. The page takes the history on its one stream to the server (`GET /api/events`, §2 Freshness), as a document of its own: the rows newest first and the unread count arrive whole when the page opens, and after that each row made, and each row read, seen or cleared, is sent as the change it is, never the list again; server-sent events, which a browser does not throttle in a background tab the way it does a timer, so the badge moves the moment something happens; a row the server posted itself arrives already seen and only joins the history.

**What is told, by kind.** Each kind is a switch in the menu, off until it is turned on:

- *Fills*: an order read back filled, the ticket's or one placed in Wealthsimple's app — `Order filled · QNC` / `Bought 5 at 1.75 · 🚀 Trading`; a bracket's stop, `Stopped out · QNC` / `Sold 5 at 1.64 · 🚀 Trading`; its target, `Target hit · QNC`; a partial fill on a pending order, `Partly filled · QNC` / `40 of 100 at 64.50 · 🚀 Trading`, once per quantity reached.
- *Order problems*: an order rejected (`Order rejected · QNC`, the order as the panel's second line says it, then Wealthsimple's reason), not sent (`Order not sent · QNC`), expired (`Order expired · QNC`), or cancelled at Wealthsimple rather than from here (`Order cancelled · QNC`; a cancel from this app passes through `cancelling` first and is not told), the order's words then the account; a bracket that ended for a reason other than its fill or the person's own Cancel, Remove or sale from the ticket (`Bracket off · QNC` / `Stop cancelled at Wealthsimple by hand · 🚀 Trading`); a leg Wealthsimple refused, on the first refusal only (`Bracket · QNC` / `Stop not placed: <reason> · trying again in a minute · 🚀 Trading`). A bracket's own leg that expires or is cancelled is the engine's to place again and is not told.
- *Connection*: the session expiring (`Sign in needed` / `The Wealthsimple session expired. Connect again from the menu.`), once per expiry; a sync that has failed three times in a row (`Sync failing`, with the reason), once per run of failures, a success starting the count over.
- *Updates*: a newer release found by the hourly check (`Bagholder v1.31.0 is available` / `Update from the header.`; a container copy, `Pull the new image.`), once per version.
- *Releases*: a press release not seen before on a ticker the person chose to follow — `Press release · QNC` and the headline, or `3 press releases · QNC` and the newest. A release that announces distributions carries the figures under the headline, since the headline alone (`Announces August 2026 Distributions`) says nothing a holder can act on: the amount a share, the payout frequency where the record states one, the day it goes ex and the day it is paid, and the amount it replaces when that differs — all from the issuer's own declared record (§2, Distribution), read again when such a release arrives so the notice is not a day behind it, and therefore the same figures the Cashflow tab pays from. A listing with no record carries what the source said beneath its own headline where it said anything (TMX, Yahoo and Seeking Alpha carry a summary with the item; Google carries none), cut at a sentence end to about four hundred characters, and nothing at all where a source only repeats its own headline. Which tickers is the person's choice, three sets under a `Releases` heading in the menu, each its own switch, as the Disclosures sets are: *Holdings*, every open position; *Watchlist*, every watched listing; *All tickers*. **One event is one notification.** What a thing *is* — a release, the story its headline tells; a filing, its source, date, kind, title and size — is what the app records having met, never the id a source handed out for it. The same release reaches the app from four sources under four ids, a week apart in their own timestamps, and drops in and out of a search's results between passes; keyed by id, that is four events and a bell each time, which is what the person saw. Every item the app meets is recorded as met, whether it was told about or absorbed as history, so a back catalogue brought by a source's first read can never ring later, and a release returning under another id is recognised and passed over. A release reaches the person from whichever source found it and is told once: the sources the news loop already reads (§4, News), each listing's news being a stream under the same rule as a source's filings, so what it carried when it was first met is history, as is everything a source brings the first time it is read for the listing, and a release already held under another source's copy, published within a day of it, is not told again, while a release repeating an older one's words months later is new; and, for a ticker no wire has carried a release for, the issuer's own filed news releases as the disclosures sweep finds them. A filed news release is this kind's, never the Disclosures kind's, so the two never tell the same thing twice; a current report (8-K, 6-K) is a filing and stays a disclosure.
- *Disclosures*: a filing not stored before on a ticker the person chose to follow — `New disclosure · QNC` / `Material change report · SEDAR+`, or `3 new disclosures · QNC` with the documents' titles (three at most, then `and more`) and the sources. A document is named by its own title, the one the Disclosures table shows (§4), because a filing's type is the form's code — `144`, `6-K` — which names the form and not what happened; a document without one yet is read for it here, only the few the notification names, and that read is kept on the row, so the table afterwards shows what the notification said. A document nothing could be read from is named by its form, in words where the app knows the form (`Material event (8-K)`, `Insider transaction (Form 4)`, `Notice of proposed sale (144)`) and by the bare code where it does not, since a code names the paperwork and not what happened. Under that line sits the sentence the document itself yielded where one has been read, since a form's code and a regulator's name say what arrived and never what it says. Which tickers is the person's choice: three sets under a `Disclosures` heading in the menu, each its own switch. *Holdings*, every open position; *Watchlist*, every watched listing; *All tickers*, every symbol the book has ever traded or held and every watched listing, an option trade counting for its underlying. A ticker outside those sets is followed by watching it. A contract or a coin has no filer and is left out. Only while a set is on, a sweep looks every five minutes for the tickers in the chosen sets whose list is older than thirty minutes and reads each again at the sources' own pace (§2, Market data), the issuer's stored SEDAR+ profile number going with the request so a re-read is a few paced actions at SEDAR+ and one request to EDGAR, no lookup; so a filing is told within the half hour of appearing at the regulator, plus whatever delay the regulator has in posting it. A disclosure is told by the document's own title, so the document has to be readable before the notice goes out: while the local model that reads it is still coming up, the pass leaves those tickers alone entirely — lists included, since a filing stored now would be part of what the app already held by the time it could be told, which is history and told to nobody — and the filing is told on a later pass, by its title rather than by `144`. The hold is bounded at two hours, after which the filing is told by what the row already says rather than never told, and there is no hold at all where no model is coming (none installed, or one that failed), since then the form's code is the best there will ever be, and thirty-odd Canadian issuers fit between passes without leaning on the source. A filing is known by what it is — source, date, kind, title and size — never by the id a source hands out, so an id scheme changing under the list tells nothing; and a list none of whose rows were there a moment ago is a re-read from scratch, told nothing, a baseline again. Each source's filings for a ticker are a stream, and every stream the app tells about follows one rule: what it held when it was first met is history and is told to nobody, and it carries a mark — the newest moment it has shown and what stood at that moment — so that afterwards only what is newer, or shares that moment without having been shown, is told. A ticker read for the first time, a regulator that begins covering one it never covered, a list re-keyed under it, a back catalogue delivered in one read, and a database rebuilt from nothing are all the same thing to it. The mark is kept in the store, so a restart never replays a stream. With every set off the disclosures pipeline stays on demand only.

**The menu.** A `Notifications` row in the menu, under Theme and in its form: the row names the state at its right — `On` when a kind is on and they can arrive, `Off` until then, and where the page is the channel, `Blocked` when the browser has refused the site and `Unavailable` where the browser offers no notifications (a page reached over plain HTTP from another machine) — and opens beside it, on hover or on a tap, the four kinds, each with a switch, then under a `Releases` heading and a `Disclosures` heading (uppercase, muted, in the table headers' form) the three sets of tickers each covers, every one with a switch, then `Send a test notification`. The kinds and the sets are settings on the server (`notify_settings` in meta, changed with `POST /api/notifications/settings`; `releasesHeld`, `releasesWatched`, `releasesAll` and `disclosuresHeld`, `disclosuresWatched`, `disclosuresAll` for the sets), so every browser that opens the app agrees on what is told. Where the server posts them, a switch simply turns its kind on or off, and the system's own prompt appears once, the first time a notification is posted. Where the page is the channel, whether this browser may show them is its own permission, asked the first time a switch or the test is pressed (browsers ask only on a click) and remembered by the browser: until it is granted every switch reads off and the first switch pressed after it is granted turns that kind on; refused, the switches and the test are dimmed and the row reads `Blocked`, which only the browser's own site settings undo. The test (`POST /api/notifications/test`) sends one row whatever the kinds say, `Bagholder` / `Notifications reach you here.`, the way to see where they land. No captions or helper text beyond these words.

**Notifications are desktop-only for now.** The phone apps carry the model, not the order engine or the sync, so nothing on a phone has an event to tell yet; their notifications are specified when something does.

### Freshness of what is on the page

The server tells the page what changed; the page never polls. It keeps one connection to the server, a stream of server-sent events (`GET /api/events`), on which the view it shows arrives whole once and afterwards only what moved in it: the entities and the fields that differ, keyed by each entity's own identity, written into the objects the page already holds, so the one element bound to a field is the one that changes. A quote tick rewrites the handful of figures that moved and nothing is rebuilt, which is what keeps a modest machine responsive when prices tick once a minute; a view that reads no price, the dashboard's closed-trade figures and the trades list, is sent nothing by it. Any new activity, NAV point, FX rate, benchmark close, distribution or quote sends its change, and so does the turn of the calendar day in the person's zone, so year tiles, YTD and anything measured to today roll over at midnight on their own, with or without new data. A whole view arriving again (the filters changed, the connection lost and made again) is matched against the one shown entity by entity, so a row still there keeps its element and only its differing fields are written. The browser never navigates or reloads: window and table scroll positions, the open position panel and the filter popover all stay where they were, and they stay because their nodes are never replaced, not because they are measured and restored around a teardown. The redraw is deferred until the next page change while a trade is open or a note is being typed, so nothing is overwritten under the user. The model the page fetches carries a trade's legs and fills only for the trade or holding open on the page (the lists never read them); a trade opened from a list fetches its own once, and a redraw that keeps the same trade, timeframe and fills leaves its chart in place rather than drawing it again. On the server the FIFO match is kept across quote ticks: a new quote rebuilds what is priced, not the book, which is matched again only when an activity row, a security or the day changes. A tick goes further than that: the closed trades, the cashflow, the equity curve and the accounts are kept as they stand and only the open positions are marked at the new prices, so a price that moves a cent costs the marking of a dozen rows rather than a rebuild of the whole book. Two fingerprints decide it — one of everything the model reads, one of everything but the quotes — and a build is full whenever the second has moved. Any source that writes a price, a headline or a heatmap tile leaves the match alone; only rows, securities, the journal or the turn of the day replace it.

| Value | Feeds from | Fresh within |
|---|---|---|
| Trades, executions, journal, dashboard tiles and cards, distribution history, YTD and All time income | Wealthsimple activities and NAV, converted with BoC rates | The next sync: weekdays after 2 PM Mountain, or Sync now |
| Annualized returns vs the index, drawdown | NAV from sync; the index's tracker from Yahoo Finance, converted with BoC rates | Sync for NAV; the session after it settles for the index |
| Position Price, Market, P&L, Allocation; Cashflow Market and Current yield | Live quote | One minute for shares, ETFs and crypto; for a US-listed option, while it is on screen, as Cboe's delayed chain allows |
| Cashflow Distribution, Projected, Yield on cost, Current yield | Declared record from TMX | A day, from a week before a distribution is due by the payer's stated schedule until it is listed; a week where no source states one (§1) |

Every instrument Wealthsimple offers has a live price source. TMX Money carries a Cboe Canada listing's declared record under the `:AQL` symbol form (the former NEO exchange), which the app asks for; its price still comes from Cboe's own feed, never from TMX's delayed quote.

## 4. Pages

All pages share the header (brand and version, sync status, orders, notifications, filter, menu), the tab row and the current filter set. Every page lays its tiles and card rows on one six-column grid with a 14 px gap: a tile is one column, a card a whole number of columns (Dashboard rows 4 + 2, Portfolio 2 + 4, Cashflow 3 + 3; Markets a full-width card over a `minmax(0,1fr) 420px` pair), so card edges are tile edges. With nothing open and nothing being typed, the Left and Right arrow keys move between the tabs in their order, Dashboard, Trades, Portfolio, Markets, Cashflow, stopping at the ends. Until the page has heard from the server (a page loaded while Bagholder is still starting), the sync status is a spinner alone: `Not connected` and `Synced` are only ever the server's answer. A short-lived notice from an action (session refreshed, trade added, folder scanned, or an error) takes the sync status's place for four seconds; it is red only when it reports an error. After Scan now or Watch folder it counts the rows the scan's files added that the book did not hold, `N new activities imported` (`1 new activity imported`), or reads `Folder scanned · nothing new`. A part of a pull that does not answer -- one account's buying power, the equity history, a listing's name -- is a sync error naming that part and why, and the figure stored before it stays; the reading of balances and buying power between syncs says its failures the same way, until a read answers. A source that failed is asked again after its rest, twice as long after each failure in a row and six hours at most, never in a loop. Every failure standing is said at once, in one line, each until its own next success and none replacing another: Wealthsimple's, a failed update of the figures, and each market source whose latest request failed or was refused, by its name and what failed (`TMX Money could not be reached.`, `Yahoo Finance refused the request (too many).`). A sync error appears once: on the "No activity yet" page it is shown in full beneath the page's button and the header keeps its plain status; everywhere else it takes the header's sync status.

**The menu.** Two of its items remove things. `Clear data` opens a dialog with a box for each kind of data the app keeps on this machine, none ticked: `Wealthsimple records` (activity, statements, balances, history, and what the pull had read, so the next pull reads everything again), `Your entries and imports` (Add trade, opening balances, event values, imported files, the watched folder), `Journal` (notes, grades, tags, saved groups), `Market data` (what the market sources answered), `Orders and brackets`, `Watchlist, tiles and notifications`, and `Wealthsimple login`; `Clear all` ticks every box, and the `Clear data` button, live once something is ticked, deletes what is ticked. An account, an instrument or a connection goes with the last thing that names it; a note whose trade's records were cleared is kept, its trade orphaned, for re-attaching. It is refused, saying why in the dialog, while Wealthsimple is being read, and, when orders or the login are ticked, while a bracket is live, naming it: its stop would be left resting at Wealthsimple with nothing watching it, so it is cancelled in the Orders panel first. The page's time zone stays, as the app's own version does. `Disconnect` deletes the Wealthsimple login and nothing else; the stored rows stay. It is always in the menu: live whenever a login is saved, connected or not (a login whose refresh Wealthsimple refused is still one to delete), and greyed when there is none. It asks once before acting.

**The page with nothing to show.** No image (the mark already sits in the header). Not connected: `No activity yet`, a line inviting the user to connect, and a Connect Wealthsimple button. Connected and syncing: `Pulling your history`, a line saying the first sync can take a minute, and no button (the header shows the sync step). Connected with nothing back: `No activity yet`, a line saying nothing has come back yet, and a Sync now button. A sync error, when there is one, sits beneath in full.

**Connecting.** Connect Wealthsimple opens one Chrome window on the app's own profile at Wealthsimple's sign-in page, and the header reads `Waiting for Wealthsimple login…` with a Cancel button beside it. The wait ends the moment any of these happens: the session is captured and made the app's own (the app refreshes the captured token at once, so the login on disk is one Wealthsimple has issued to the app, not a copy of the browser's; only then does it close the window itself and sync; a capture whose refresh Wealthsimple refuses is not a connection, and the watcher keeps looking while the window is up); the window is closed or Chrome quit, which the app notices as the absence of any window in its own Chrome, checked every 1.5 seconds, whether or not Chrome itself is still running (the header shows `The Chrome window closed before a session showed up. Choose Connect Wealthsimple to try again.`); Cancel is pressed (the app closes the window); or three minutes pass. Nothing is ever relaunched: a closed window stays closed until Connect is pressed again, and pressing Connect while the app's window is still open brings that window forward instead of opening another. The launch on Connect is the only way the app ever opens a browser window; it never asks a running Chrome to open one. Each Connect is a numbered attempt with its own watcher; the watcher looks for the window every half second with a half-second timeout, a separate thread tries a capture every 1.5 seconds so a capture call stuck on a window that just closed never delays the watcher, and the page asks the server every half second while it waits, so a closed window shows in the header within about a second; and a watcher from an earlier attempt exits without touching a later attempt's window. The server logs each of these steps to its terminal (`bagholder login: …`).

### Dashboard

Six KPI tiles in one row, all in CAD over the trades in scope. A trade is in scope for a date range when it was open at any time in it; a realized P&L counts in the range when its sale falls in it; a closed trade's statistics count when its close falls in it.

| Tile | Value | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Realized P&L | Sum of the P&L (CAD) realized in scope, each sale on its own day, open trades' partial sales included | Closed-trade count |
| Win rate | Winning closed trades ÷ closed trades | Wins, losses and breakevens |
| Profit factor | Gross wins ÷ gross losses over closed trades; `∞` with no losses | Gross W and L |
| Expectancy | Closed trades' P&L ÷ their count | Average win and average loss |
| Max drawdown | Drawdown percentage | CAD fall and trough month |
| Avg annualized | Annualized return | Number of years used |

Cards:

- **Equity curve.** Title `Equity curve` with a switch at the right, `P&L · Value`, remembered on this machine and not a filter. `P&L`, the default: the Realized P&L in scope as a running total, CAD, each part on the day it was realized, following every filter as the Realized tile does; a part that waits is left out; the line is in the positive colour while the total ends at or above zero, the negative colour below. `Value`: the accounts' value by day, following the account filter alone. Nothing is written under the chart. Returns, the years against the index and Max drawdown are always the value's. A `$` axis of four ticks from the lowest to the highest, reaching below zero where the P&L does, and six date labels; hover shows the figure and day, and the chart past the day read dims (the line and its fill keep their colour up to the pointer and fade after it), as the phone's does. Every line chart the page draws itself hovers this way.
- **Annualized returns.** Title `Annualized returns` with a switch at the right, `S&P 500`, `S&P/TSX` or `TSX 60`, choosing the index the years are compared against; the choice is remembered on this machine and is not a filter. Subtitle `Vs <index>`. Every year, newest first, each with the account's return and the index's return and two equal-height bars; the list scrolls inside the card, which takes its height from the equity curve beside it and never grows past it. Hovering a year shows the app's own tip with how far that year's return was over or under the index's, in percentage points to one place, signed and in the positive or negative colour, `+4.2 pts` over `vs <index>`; a year the index has no figure for has no tip. Footer, fixed below the list: `Outperformed <index> in N of M years.`
- **Monthly P&L.** One bar per calendar month of the P&L realized in it (each sale in its own month, open trades' partial sales included), CAD, six axis labels; hover shows the month and the number of trades realizing in it; click opens the trade or filters to that month. The value axis labels the top, the midpoint, zero and the bottom; a label that would touch the one above it is not shown, so the bottom label goes when the losing months are small next to the winning ones.
- **Grade vs P&L.** Four bars, A B C F, CAD sum of the closed trades per grade with the count under each.
- **By symbol.** Symbol, P&L (CAD) realized (as Realized P&L), Trades, Win rate and Avg hold over closed trades, grouped by underlying, sorted by P&L; click opens or filters.
- **Review queue.** Closed trades missing a grade or a thesis, newest first.

### Trades

Table columns in this order: Open · Close · Symbol · Exchange · Qty · Entry · Exit · FX · P&L · P&L % · Hold · Grade · Tags. Every column sorts. Grade sorts A first on the first click, F first on the second, ungraded trades last either way. FX is centred; numbers are right-aligned. Column widths are fixed proportions of the table so they do not shift with content; the table scrolls sideways only when its card is narrower than 1150 px. Long symbols truncate with an ellipsis and show in full on hover. Open trades are listed with closed ones, their Close reading `Open`; opening one opens the holding page. Newest activity first by default (an open trade by its latest fill, a closed one by its close).

Trade detail: symbol, then name, exchange and listing ticker as `Name · EXCHANGE: TICKER` (the underlying's ticker for an option, listing suffixes such as .TO dropped), with `· Entered by you` after it where its cost is one you entered (§2, What you enter); P&L and P&L % in the trade's currency; the trade chart; facts Open, Close, Entry, Exit, Hold, Account; an executions table with When · Side · Qty · FX · Price · Amount, Price the broker's own a unit where its row states one, else the fill's value over its units (quantity × the contract's size), as the trade's Entry is, where Side reads `BUY` or `SELL` for shares and crypto and, for options, what the fill did in this trade (`BUY TO OPEN`, `SELL TO CLOSE`, …); a fill that closed one trade and opened the next says `(close + open)`; the journal with thesis, grade and tags, and under them, while a corporate event on this holding waits on you, `Corporate event · <day>` with Spin-off (the holding it came out of, and this holding's share of that one's cost) or Return of capital (the capital returned a unit, in the instrument's currency), entered with Enter or the Enter button, a refusal said under it in the server's words; and, below them, the short interest card and the disclosures. The browser Back button returns to the list at the same scroll position.

**Short interest.** Under the trade's two panels, three tiles for a share listing on a market where short selling is reported; nothing at all is drawn for a coin, an index, a futures or currency contract or an option, since no one reports those. The venue decides the regulator through the same routing the quotes use, so a listing resolves here as it does everywhere: FINRA for a US listing, CIRO for a Canadian one, and a listing whose venue the book does not name follows its currency, as its quote does. A company listed on both markets has a position on each, measured against different trading, and every figure is the one for the listing held; the two are never added together or shown for one another. Each tile carries at its right the date of the figures it draws, and only that: they are not all on the same clock, and the regulator behind them is named here rather than three times over one page.

`Short volume` and `Short vs float`, side by side: both measures are a part of a whole, so both are the donut the Allocation, Sectors and Regions cards already draw (`donutPieces`), each with its centre reading the percentage under `Short`, its legend naming the two pieces on one line above the ring rather than beside it, so the ring itself sits in the middle of its tile (a ring and a legend centred as a pair leaves the ring off to one side), and one line of text below the ring carrying what the ring cannot. A slice under the pointer puts its own count in the centre, over its share, which is where the share numbers are read; the ring is drawn large enough, and the count set smaller than a percentage is, that the longest share count a listing has still sits clear of it.

`Short volume` is the part of a period's trading that was sold short: the legend reads `Short volume` and `Shares traded`, its header gives the span the figures cover — a single trading day for a US listing, the half-month period for a Canadian one — and the line beneath gives days to cover with the daily volume it is measured against. It is named for what it draws rather than for the position, which is a different figure with a different date and has its own tile below.

`Short vs float` is the position against the shares actually available to trade: the legend reads `Short interest` and `Float`, and the line beneath gives the shares short and the change since the previous report. The float is Yahoo's, read under the symbol forms of the market the venue settled on rather than of whatever currency a row happens to carry, since a watchlist row keeps none and the forms would then default to the wrong market. It is the float and never the shares in issue: those are a different figure, and a percentage whose denominator changed from listing to listing would not be the same figure twice. **A fund is the one instrument where the two are the same thing** — it creates and redeems units on demand and holds none back, so the units in issue are the units there are to trade — and where no float is published for one, the count its own exchange publishes stands in its place: TMX for the venues it carries counts for, and for a Cboe Canada listing that venue's own directory, which publishes every listing's market capitalisation beside its last price. A capitalisation is the count times the price, and dividing gives the count back whole for every one of the listings that directory carries — the exchange's own figure recovered, not a market value shaped into a count. The venue is asked for its own listings only, a symbol being one fund's there and another's elsewhere, and only for what it itself calls a fund, since for a company the shares in issue are not the float. A fund no exchange publishes a count for reads as a dash rather than an estimate, which no figure on this card is. The lookups keep Yahoo's own pace, the one every other Yahoo request the app makes keeps, since they are a burst by nature — one per listing whenever the sweep runs — and unpaced they were the burst Yahoo turned away, which read as a listing having no float when it has one. A float that answered is kept for half a day; one that answered with nothing is asked for again within the hour, since a slow source is not an absent figure. Reading it needs the browser handshake `curl_cffi` provides, the app's one optional dependency; without it, or for a fund, which has no float, the tile says none is published rather than substituting a number.

`Short interest over time`, across the width below them: the position at every report the regulator has, drawn exactly as the equity curve is — its own axis of four ticks naming figures the line reaches, the same grid lines and hairline, the same area under the line in the accent, and the dates along the foot. Hovering it reads that report: the crosshair and a dot on the point, and the equity curve's own two-line tip carrying the shares short and the date the report settled, the tip turning at either end to stay inside the card. Not bars, which is the other house idiom: a position moves by a few percent between reports and every bar would stand the same height. Every point is a figure a regulator published, never a shape between them. FINRA answers with every settlement at once, and Canada publishes a file per reporting date, so a Canadian listing's run is read on its own once the figures are already on screen rather than holding the tiles back, and the tile appears when there are at least three reports.

Days to cover is the position over the average daily volume **in the listing's own market** over the period its report covers: FINRA publishes that average itself, and for a Canadian listing it is the period's volume divided by the days the Canadian market actually traded, counted from the index series the app already keeps so a holiday is not counted as a day. That period volume is the short sale report's own where it carries the listing; the report lists only what was sold short and holds no zero rows, so a listing absent from it was **not sold short in the period** rather than unknown — its short volume reads as none of its trading, and what it did trade comes from the exchange's own daily series under the venue's form, which is that listing's volume and not another venue's. It cannot come from the app's own bars — those are stored under the bare ticker, so a company listed on both markets has one set of them and the US listing's days to cover would be measured against Canadian trading.

The two measures are never added together or folded into one number: a position is a count of shares outstanding short, short volume is a share of trading, and each carries its own date because they are published on different clocks. Nothing is estimated: every figure is the regulator's own, and a listing a report does not carry shows a dash rather than a number from somewhere else. The same figures, stacked under their labels to fit the narrower card, head the News card once it is scoped to a symbol (§4 Markets, News), so any ticker the search finds carries its short interest beside its news. **What has been read is stored**, per listing rather than per ticker, since a company listed on both markets has a position on each, and stamped with the version of the reading that wrote it. A row written by a version that could carry less than the current one — a float the app has since learned to find, say — is read again at once rather than waiting out its hours, so a figure that can now be had never sits as a dash until the row goes stale. Opening an instrument again — or opening one after the app restarts — draws its tiles with the page rather than after a round of reads, and a stored reading past six hours is still answered at once and refreshed behind the page. A sweep every half hour keeps every held and watched listing stored and current, so the reading is usually done before it is wanted. The page keeps what it was given for half an hour, so one left open for days is not still showing the day it opened on.

**Trade chart.** Real bars for the trade's span, ten days either side, drawn with TradingView Lightweight Charts served from the app itself. The chart is candlesticks or nothing: every bar drawn has an open, high, low and close from its source, and a line is never drawn through closes or through the executions. When there are no bars the chart's place says why, from what the sources actually answered: a source that failed is named with its failure (`TMX Money could not be reached.`, `Yahoo Finance refused the request (too many).`), otherwise `No bars for this span from TMX Money or Yahoo Finance.` naming the sources that were asked. Nobody has to look at a terminal. A timeframe switch above the chart offers whichever of 1H, 4H, 1D, 1W and 1M the instrument's source can supply, nothing else; the active one is highlighted. The default follows the trade's length: 1H up to 2 days, 4H up to 10, 1D up to 180, 1W up to about 3 years, 1M beyond, or the nearest coarser one offered; a timeframe the user picks is kept for that trade while the page is open. The chart opens framed on the trade with a margin of about 15% of its length either side, never stretched edge to edge. Weekly and monthly bars are aggregated from daily ones (Monday-start weeks, calendar months); 4H from hourly. Candlesticks when the source gives open, high, low and close; a line of closes when it gives closes only. Every execution is marked on its day at its price: an up arrow below the bar for a buy, a down arrow above for a sell, labelled with signed quantity and price. Wheel or pinch zooms the time axis, dragging pans, and the view a user set is kept while they stay on the trade. Labels appear once no more than 40 executions are in view; the arrows are always shown. A fill with no price, a staking reward, is marked on its day when bars exist. When no history source covers the instrument, the priced executions themselves are plotted on a zoomable time axis. Nothing on the chart is synthetic.

**Source chain.** No single free source covers every venue, so each instrument has an ordered list of candidate sources and symbol forms (the tables above). A fetch asks them in order and the first with bars for the span wins; a source whose intraday reach stops short of the span is skipped rather than asked for a partial answer. The winner is remembered per symbol and asked first next time; if it later has nothing for a new span, the rest of the chain is tried again. A symbol no source carries shows the chart's empty state. Nothing is hand-mapped per ticker.

Intraday bars and execution times are shown in the viewer's local time.

**Bar archive.** The sources keep bars for a limited time, so the app keeps its own. A background sweep fetches the hourly and four-hour bars of every instrument traded or held in the past year, and the daily bars of any whose source forgets them (none of the current sources does), a dozen instruments per pass with passes back to back while there is a backlog and every five minutes once there is none, and tops each one up once a day from its last stored bar. A chart request never waits on this: when the bars it wants are not stored yet it shows the daily chart and switches on its own once they are. When a fetch produces nothing for a timeframe, that timeframe is not offered for the next ten minutes and the sources are left alone; it is tried again after that. Bars once stored are never dropped, so a trade keeps its intraday chart however old it gets. The only trades without intraday bars are those already older than the source's reach when the app first saw them.

The TradingView credit the library's licence requires is the library's own small logo in the chart's bottom-left corner, nothing in the menu.

Bars are cached in the database. Closed days are written once and never rewritten; the newest day may be replaced. A span reaching the present is refetched once its copy is 20 hours old, in the background: the chart draws the stored copy at once and takes the new bars in place when the read ends, and a read that finds nothing is not asked again for ten minutes, as with an intraday timeframe.

### Portfolio

Tiles in the style of the dashboard tiles, CAD aggregates over the accounts in scope (every account when the filter names none). There is no Market value tile: the Allocation donut's centre carries that figure. The two margin tiles show when an open account in scope is a margin account (its type contains `MARGIN`), six tiles in all; otherwise Cash takes their place and the row is five:

**Allocation.** A donut of the open positions in scope by market value in CAD, one slice each up to ten, otherwise the ten largest and `Other (N)`, coloured from the eleven-colour palette; the centre reads `Market value` and the total, or the hovered slice's value and share; the legend is each slice's symbol and share. The card takes the height of the Holdings card beside it and does not grow. No toggle.

**Sectors and Regions.** A second row under Allocation and Holdings, one card the width of both: the open long positions in scope by market value in CAD, spread by sector and by country, as two donuts side by side at the card's centre, each up to 340 px across as the card's width allows, the Sectors legend (swatch, name, share) at the card's left edge under the heading `Sectors` and the Regions legend mirrored (share, name, swatch) at the right edge under `Regions`. Every figure comes from a record outside Wealthsimple, and nothing is guessed: a share is classified by its listing's own record (TMX Money's quote carries the sector and industry of Canadian and US listings; Nasdaq's summary is the fallback for a US listing; the country is the listing venue's); a fund is looked through, its holdings with their weights taken from the issuer's own published record and each holding classified the same way, a holding that is itself a fund looked through in turn, three levels deep at most. Issuers read directly: Vanguard Canada (its stated sector and country breakdowns), iShares Canada (the fund's holdings file, with sector and location per holding), Harvest (the page's holdings, or the single stock a fund references), Ninepoint (the underlying stock of a HighShares fund), Evolve (the page's holdings and sector breakdown); any other family, and any page that answers nothing, falls back to Yahoo Finance's fund profile, which states every fund's sector weightings and names its top holdings, from which the countries come. Sector names are folded onto one set (Information Technology, Financials, Health Care, Consumer Discretionary, Consumer Staples, Industrials, Energy, Materials, Utilities, Real Estate, Communication Services, Digital assets), country names likewise. The positions and values are Allocation's own, every position worth something, so the three cards agree. A coin is its own sector, `Digital assets`, and no country's; an option contract counts as its underlying, under the share's record. What no record covers, a family and fund neither read nor known to Yahoo, the uncovered remainder of a fund, or a coin's country, is one slice, `Not classified`, last and in the neutral grey, never spread over the others. Records are read by the app in the background, one request at a time with a pause between them, when a held security has none or its record is older than seven days, and kept in the database; the cards show what is known so far. Slices are the largest first in Allocation's palette, every sector (the set is twelve), ten countries at most plus `Other (N)`. Each donut's centre reads `Sectors` or `Regions` over the number of them the positions span (every sector or country with value, `Not classified` not counted), or the hovered slice's name and share, and hovering a slice or its legend row dims the rest of that donut, as Allocation's does.

**Holdings.** One row per open position: Symbol (`SHORT` on a short; the account is not shown in the row, and hovering the row names it in the app's own tooltip, never the browser's) · Avg (average cost per unit) · Last (the current price) · Book · Market · Change ($) · Change (%) · Unrealized P&L (`+$20.05 (+3.4%)`), sorted by Unrealized P&L; the two Change columns are the day's move on the position from its quote's change and percent change, `—` without a quote. Avg, Last, Book, Market and the changes are in the position's currency. The table shows ten rows and scrolls inside the card past that.

**A holding.** Clicking a row opens the holding on the page a trade opens (§Trades), the position standing in for the trade: the symbol, name and listing; the unrealized P&L and its percentage at the top right; the chart with the fills so far; Qty, Avg (the average cost), Book, Market (the last two in the position's currency), Hold (the days so far), Account, in place of a trade's Open, Close, Entry and Exit, which an open position does not have; the executions; the thesis, grade and tags. The ticket's Buy and Sell sit in the header beside the figure, as ⌘K's rows carry them and under the same rule — Buy on a share, Sell where a position is open, neither on an option contract or a coin — so a position can be added to, taken off, or opened from the page that shows it. The holding and the trade it becomes when it closes share one journal entry, keyed by the round trip that opened it, so what is written here is the trade's journal on that day.

**Refresh.** Net liquidation values, cash balances and buying power are read at every sync; cash and buying power again when a page opens and every five minutes while one is open with a login saved (on the phones, as soon as the stored book is on screen); a read that fails is said in the header, never swallowed, so the tiles move with the day instead of waiting for the daily sync. A margin account whose buying power has not been read yet makes Available margin wait (`— unread`), never read as no margin account.

### Markets

A glance at the day across the book and the symbols being followed: a row of market tiles, then three cards: the heatmap the width of the page, then News and the Watchlist side by side (`minmax(0,1fr) 420px`, 14 px gap, aligned at the top). Nothing on this page comes from Wealthsimple: quotes, sectors and news are read from the public sources the rest of the app uses.

**Market tiles.** A row of tiles above the Heatmap card, on the page's six-column tile grid and in the dashboard tiles' geometry (87 px, 12 px padding): the label is the instrument's short name (`SPX`, `NDX`, `DJI`, `VIX`, `GOLD`, `BITCOIN`, `TSX`, `WTI`, `USD/CAD`, `10Y`; the directory's symbol unless people know the instrument by a name), the value its last price, and beneath it the day's change in points and in percent, `+42.18 (+0.63%)`, signed and coloured, the points at the instrument's own scale (two decimals for an index, a future or a commodity, three for a rate, four for a pair, none for Bitcoin), the percent at two; a dash until a quote is known. The plus opens a picker under the app's header at the right, where ⌘K opens, so the box stays put as tiles are added: a search box (`Index, future, commodity, rate or pair`, filtering on symbol, label, name and alias), the heading `Market instruments` (`Matches` while filtering), the directory's rows (symbol over name, venue at the right, a bookmark lit for an instrument on the row), and a footer `N of 12 tiles used`; a row adds the instrument at the end (opening the second row when it is the seventh) or removes it when lit, the box stays open for the next pick, reaching twelve closes it, and at twelve unlit rows are dimmed and do nothing. Nothing on a tile opens anything: these are indices, futures, commodities, rates and pairs, which the app does not trade or chart.

**A listing's page.** Every listing on the tab opens its own page on click, the one §4 gives a holding: the holding's own where the book holds it, and otherwise the same page without what belongs to a trade — no size, cost or P&L, since it has none, and no executions, notes, grade or tags. Its price and day change stand where a holding's P&L does, its chart is a year of days with the executions of every trade closed on that listing marked on it (a listing never traded has none to mark), and its short interest and disclosures are the holding's own. The ticket's Buy is on it, as ⌘K carries it, so a position can be opened from the listing; Sell is live only where a position is open, which is the holding's page. The address is `markets/listing:SYMBOL@VENUE`, so the page is a link like any other, and the tab keeps the crumb the trade and holding pages keep. A tile a card folds into `Other (N)` is several listings and opens none.

**Fear & Greed.** The meter the media carries, as its publisher gives it. A card under the market tiles: the title `Fear & Greed`, a segmented control `Stocks · Crypto` (remembered), and at the right the publisher's name and when its reading is from. `Stocks` is CNN's index, the one the meter is known by; `Crypto` is alternative.me's. A half dial carries one arc per band of that scale, from extreme fear at the left to extreme greed at the right, in the heatmap's own ramp with the neutral band unpainted; a needle points at the score from a hub at the dial's centre, stopping short of the arc, and the score and its word sit below the dial rather than under the needle, in the band's colour. Beside it, `Where it stood`: the readings the publisher compares today against, each with its own score and word (CNN's previous close, week, month and year; the crypto index's own earlier days). Beside that, for the stock index, `What it is made of`: CNN's seven indicators — market momentum, stock price strength, stock price breadth, put and call options, market volatility, junk bond demand, safe haven demand — each with its score and CNN's word for it. The crypto index publishes no indicators, and that column is not drawn rather than shown empty. **Heatmap.** A card with the title `Heatmap`, two segmented controls and a legend on one header row, then at the row's right a 24 px ghost button carrying the arrows-out glyph that opens the heatmap on its own (below), over a 430 px box. The first control picks the universe, in two groups either side of a divider because they answer different questions. The book's: `Holdings` (every open position in scope, the Portfolio's own, sized by market value in CAD; one whose value waits on its price at the smallest holding's size, since it has no value to be sized by), `Watchlist` (the watched listings, every tile the same size, since a listing not held has no market value), `Both` (the holdings, plus each watched listing not held at the smallest holding's size). The market's: `Canada`, the S&P/TSX 60 from TMX Money's own constituents query, each tile sized by its index weight and coloured by the day change of TMX's quote, its sector TMX's; `US`, the hundred largest US companies by market cap from Nasdaq's screener, sized by market cap, day change and sector the screener's; `International`, the hundred largest companies listed in the US from outside the US and Canada, from the same screener. A market universe is read while a page shows it, however it came on show (picked, remembered in the browser, named in the heatmap's own address, or one a running slideshow goes through): at once when it has no rows or its rows are more than thirty minutes old, and again each time they come to thirty minutes for as long as a page still shows it; with no page showing it, it is not read at all, not at start and not through a night, since the heatmap is its only reader and its sources push nothing. The screener is one request and carries both `US` and `International`, so reading one reads the other; the TSX 60 is one plus a paced quote per constituent. A source is not asked again within thirty minutes of its last attempt, answered or failed, so one that is down costs one request each half hour, not one per page. The rows are kept in the database; a market universe with no rows reads `Not read yet.`, or, when its source's last read failed, what failed (`Nasdaq's screener could not be reached.`), and its tiles open nothing. The second picks the size, `Market value` or `Equal`; under `Equal` every tile has the same area. Both choices are remembered in the browser. The legend reads `−3%`, eight swatches through the ramp, `+3%`. The box is a two-level squarified treemap (Bruls, Huizing and van Wijk; largest first): one block per sector, sized by the sector's value, with a 19 px header naming the sector and, right-aligned, its value-weighted day change (on a block too narrow for both, the name gives way with an ellipsis and the change always shows; a name so cut, or a symbol cut by its tile, shows the whole on hover, the symbol over the listing's name, under the rule §6 sets for every cut line); inside, one tile per symbol, inset 3 px and 1 px more, sized by value, filled by the day change and carrying the symbol over the change (16 px over 12 px when the tile is wider than 128 and taller than 62; 13 over 11 when wider than 78 and taller than 40; 11 px alone below that). A symbol's sector is the one its exposure record gives most weight to (a fund under the sector it weights most, a coin under `Digital assets`, a contract under its underlying's record, and `Not classified` when there is no record); its day change is the quote's. Two rules keep tiles readable: within a block every position under 1.5% of the block's value folds into one `Other (N)` tile carrying their sum and value-weighted change (one alone keeps its name), and each tile's layout weight is raised to at least `min(total / count, total × minArea / blockArea)` with `minArea = min(52 × the block's short side, 22% of its area)`, so no tile computes much under 52 px on its short side and the smallest tiles are not exactly proportional to value. The box lays itself out for its width, again when the window resizes and on every model poll, keeping its place. ### Order ticket

A side panel for placing an order with Wealthsimple, with an optional stop loss and take profit that Bagholder itself will watch (Wealthsimple has neither a bracket nor a trailing stop). It opens from the ⌘K list: a symbol row whose listing is shares or an ETF carries a Buy (`+`) and a Sell (`−`) icon at its right, in place of the field name the other rows show there, and either opens the ticket on that symbol and side. A trade's or a holding's page opens it too, from its own Buy and Sell buttons, on that instrument (the owner kept them, 2026-09-25). Esc, the scrim and the × close it and keep what was typed: opening a ticket on the same symbol and side brings every field back as it was, across a reload too, until an order is sent or Cancel at the foot closes the ticket and discards the draft. While it is open the keyboard belongs to it: the tab arrows and ⌘K do nothing.

**Frame.** Anchored to the right edge of the app panel, its full height, 400 px wide, over a scrim on the rest of the panel; a header (`New order`, then `Review order`), a body that scrolls inside the panel, a footer that does not move; with both legs open the body fits an 850 px window without scrolling, and the page behind a panel does not scroll while it is open. The panel is painted in the page's colour and the cards in it (the quote card, the Orders panel's cards) in the tiles' colour with the tiles' outline, so a card in the panel is a tile on the dashboard. Colours are the theme's tokens, so it follows Midnight and Light.

**Quote card.** The symbol, `Name · Exchange`, the last price and the day's change (`+1.98 (+1.21%)`, coloured), then `Bid 165.38 × 100 · Mid 165.40 · Ask 165.42 × 300`, the number after × being the size of the last known bid or ask. Every figure is Wealthsimple's own quote for the security, in the security's currency, asked for when the ticket opens and again every five seconds while it is open, the same quote Wealthsimple's ticket shows and trades against; the change is against Wealthsimple's previous baseline. A quote that cannot be had says why in the panel (`Not connected.`, `Wealthsimple has no quote for X.`) and the figures are dashes.

**Order.** Buy or Sell, full width. Then a two-column grid: Account (the open self-directed Wealthsimple accounts that trade securities; crypto, predictions, managed and Manual accounts are not offered; a Sell opens on the account holding the position; a Buy on the account chosen last time in the ticket, remembered in the browser, else the account holding the symbol (held in more than one, the margin account among them, else the first by name), else the margin account when there is one; the account chosen stays through Review and Back) · Shares; Order type (only the types Wealthsimple allows for that security, of Market, Limit, Stop, Stop limit) · Limit price (Limit and Stop limit); Stop price (Stop and Stop limit) takes the next free cell; Time in force (Day · Good till cancelled, the order's alone; hidden for a Market order) · Amount. Shares and Amount each carry a `Max` button inside their right edge: on a Buy it sets the whole shares the account's buying power covers at the working price, on a Sell the position's shares (dimmed when neither is known). Shares and Amount are one quantity seen twice: typing an amount sets the whole shares it buys at the working price, typing shares sets the amount; the working price is the ask on a Market buy, the bid on a Market sell, the stop price on a Stop order, else the limit price. A field holds what was typed while it has focus and is formatted when it loses it (Enter commits it). Defaults: one share (a Sell, the position's quantity), the limit at the last price, an entry stop 2% past the last price.

**Stop loss and Take profit.** Only on a Buy: a Sell leaves nothing to protect, so neither section is shown. Each has a plus (add) or a cross (remove) at its right, the ⌘K rows' own icon buttons, and is open by default. Both go out good till cancelled whatever the order's time in force: a stop that lapsed at the close would leave the position unprotected overnight. Wealthsimple keeps a good-till-cancelled order ninety days, and the engine places it again at that boundary. Stop loss: Type `Stop` or `Trailing stop`, then one field, `Stop` or `Trail`, with a `$` · `%` switch inside its right edge: under `$` a stop price or a trail amount per share, under `%` a percent below the working price or a trail percent of price (5% either way by default; switching the unit resets the field to its default); the line beneath reads `Sells at 157.13` or `Starts at 157.13` and, in red, the loss if it fires with its percentage of the entry, `−$206.75 (−5.0%)`. A trail's loss is its distance exactly, whatever cent the start price rounds to. Take profit: one field, `Target`, with the same `$` · `%` switch, a limit price or a percent above the working price (10% by default); `Sells at 181.94` and, in green, the gain, `+$413.50 (+10.0%)`. A fixed stop or a target typed as a percent becomes the price the order carries, rounded to the cent, and its line reads that price's own loss or gain. Switching Buy and Sell recomputes the defaults for the new direction.

**Brackets after the fill.** A submitted ticket with a stop loss or a take profit becomes a bracket that waits for the entry to fill. Every five seconds while the app runs connected, each live bracket is checked, in this order: what Wealthsimple and the book say (an exit of the bracket's own filled ends it; the bracket's resting order cancelled at Wealthsimple by hand ends it too, the person having taken the position over, and a price or quantity changed by hand is adopted, the bracket following the new level; a stop or target Wealthsimple has ended, a Day stop from before every exit went good till cancelled or ninety days reached while the app was off, is placed again at the same level, good till cancelled; Wealthsimple refusing an exit for shares that are not there ends the bracket, the reason on the leg), then the arming, then the watching.

- *Arming.* While a bracket waits, its entry is read back from Wealthsimple on every check, so a fill is seen within five seconds. When the entry reads filled, or ended with a partial fill, the bracket arms for the filled quantity and the stop is placed at once. A stop loss goes to Wealthsimple as its own stop order for that quantity, with the ticket's time in force, when the security's allowed order types include a stop; when they do not, the stop is watched here (the row says so) and fires as a market sell when the bid reaches it. A take profit is always watched here.
- *Trailing.* The level follows the high of Wealthsimple's quote, from the fill price: high minus the trail (a percent of the high, or the amount). The resting stop is moved whenever the new level is at least half a percent above the current one, a move being a cancel and a new stop order at Wealthsimple; the level in the table moves with it. There is no other throttle.
- *The target.* Wealthsimple holds one order on the position's shares at a time: the stop, or the limit sell at the target, never both. When the bid reaches the target the resting stop is cancelled first; the stop is read back on every check, and the moment Wealthsimple confirms the cancel the limit sell at the target is placed. The stop stays in force until the cancel is confirmed. While the limit sell rests there is no stop at Wealthsimple, so Bagholder watches the stop level itself (a trailing level still following the high): if the bid falls to it, the limit sell is cancelled and, the moment Wealthsimple confirms, a market sell goes out; the stop row reads Watching meanwhile, then Placing. And the limit sell rests only while the target is in reach: once the bid is a percent under the target, the limit sell is cancelled and the stop order goes back to Wealthsimple as soon as the cancel is confirmed, so the state with no stop at Wealthsimple lasts seconds around a touch of the target, not the time until the price returns; the percent keeps a price hovering at the target from swapping the two orders back and forth. Nothing in the engine waits longer than it must: there is no pacing between its requests beyond the five-second check.
- *Ninety days.* Wealthsimple ends a good-till-cancelled order ninety days after it is placed and reports the moment with the order. A resting stop or target within seven days of its end is placed again before it: cancelled and placed anew at the same level (a trailing stop keeps its level and its high), outside the regular session when there is time and in any session in the last two days, the leg reading Placing between the confirmed cancel and the new order. Nothing waits for the expiry; a stop Wealthsimple has already ended, because the app was off, is placed again on the first check.
- *Nothing left behind.* Wealthsimple holds one resting order per share, and the bracket's stop or target is that order, so no exit of the bracket's outlives the position. When a bracket ends for any reason, the stop or target filled, the person cancelling from the panel or at Wealthsimple, a sale, the other leg's cancel is sent and the bracket reads closing until Wealthsimple confirms it gone, a refused cancel being sent again on every check; only then is it done. A Sell placed from the ticket on shares a bracket holds ends that bracket first and waits for the cancel's confirmation before the sell goes out. Every check also cancels any exit order of Bagholder's own that rests at Wealthsimple with no live bracket holding it. Only a bracket with nothing resting at Wealthsimple (a security Wealthsimple takes no stop order for, watched here) can see its shares sold elsewhere; that one ends when the sale shows in the activity feed, or when two successive balance reads omit the position, never on one read.
- *Failures.* An exit Wealthsimple rejects is tried again after a minute, then five, fifteen, and every hour after that for as long as the bracket lives, the leg reading `Retrying · <reason>` meanwhile: a stop placed again after the close, which Wealthsimple may refuse until the next session, is never given up on; every step is a line on the terminal (`bagholder bracket: …`). Under the development setting `BAGHOLDER_DRY_ORDERS=1` nothing is placed: the request each step would send is printed once, and the bracket keeps waiting. A bracket only fires while the app is running and connected; with the app closed the resting Wealthsimple stop is the only protection.

**Review.** `Buy 25 NVDA` with the side coloured, then `Limit 165.40 · Day · Trading` (a Market order: `Market · Trading`). Rows: Stop loss `Market at 157.13`, `Trails 5% under the high · starts at 157.13`, or `None`; Take profit `Limit at 181.94` or `None`; then At risk (the stop loss line's loss, red), Target (the take profit's gain, green), Risk / reward `1:2` (one decimal only when it is not zero; `—` without both brackets), Position size `5.6% of net asset value` (the order's cost in CAD at today's rate, the Bank of Canada's for the security's currency, over the net liquidation value of every open account; with no rate for that currency it, and the margin after that follows from it, waits on the rate), and either Available margin after (a margin account: its available margin minus the cost in CAD times the security's client margin rate from Wealthsimple, the full cost when the rate is unknown; red when negative) or Cash after (any other account: the account's cash in the order's currency, minus the cost); an account linked to a margin account as its collateral (Wealthsimple's Margin Boost: the account carries the feature MARGIN_BOOST, whose metadata names the margin account's custodian account, read with the accounts at every sync and kept with them) shows Cash after and then Available margin after for the margin account it backs, by the same arithmetic; a Sell adds instead of subtracting. Last, Estimated cost (Estimated proceeds on a Sell): the quantity times the working price, in the security's currency, `≈` before it for a Market order. Back returns to the form as it was.

**Submit.** The request is the one Wealthsimple's web app sends (`SoOrdersOrderCreate`: account, security, execution type, side and quantity, prices, time in force, and an external id the app makes up and keeps). The ticket is written to the store before anything is sent, then updated with Wealthsimple's order id, or its rejection, or the failure; every submit is a row, and the rows are the user's own record: `Clear data` does not touch them. Orders reach Wealthsimple; only when the app runs with `BAGHOLDER_DRY_ORDERS=1`, a development setting, does a submit record the ticket, print the request it would have sent on the terminal, and say so in the header instead. After a submit the panel closes and the header reads, for ten seconds (it names the whole order, so longer than the page's other notices), `Order placed · Buy 25 NVDA at 165.40 limit, stop 157.13, target 181.94` (`at market` for a Market order; the stop and target clauses only when set), or `Not sent (orders are off) · …` under the development setting. A rejection or failure stays in the review step with its reason. Nothing else on the page changes.

**Refused tickets.** A quantity of zero, a Limit or Stop order without its price, an account not offered, a symbol with no stored listing, or a bracket without its price is refused with the reason and nothing is recorded.

### Orders

A side panel, not a tab: the header carries an Orders button (a receipt icon) between the sync status and the filter button, with a count of the panel's Pending cards when there are any, in the ink's quiet tint rather than the accent (a resting order is nothing to acknowledge; the accent badge is the bell's, for what is unread, §2 Notifications), every entry resting at Wealthsimple and every live bracket whose entry has filled, so the badge and the panel agree; it opens the panel over the page in the ticket's frame (400 px, the right edge, a scrim), as does ⌘O (Ctrl+O elsewhere), which pressed again closes it. Esc, the scrim and the × close it; Esc closes an open editor first. Left and Right move between its tabs. ⌘K still opens the filter popover over the panel, so the accounts in scope can be changed without leaving it, and the panel follows the page's account filter: its heading row names the accounts in scope at the right, `All Accounts` when the filter names none, and every card and bracket outside them is left out. The panel lists every order the ticket submitted and every order Wealthsimple reports as pending from anywhere, its own app included, as cards under a segmented control:

- **Pending**: everything not yet filled, cancelled or rejected, under the heading `Pending orders`: the orders resting at Wealthsimple and, as a card of its own, each armed bracket, newest first together. Above them, while a ticket has been closed without being sent or cancelled, its draft as a card washed and outlined in amber: `Draft` in amber where the side would be, the exchange and symbol, the order's amount where the draft holds a price; the detail line `Buy 1 at 1.72 limit · Day`; the legs the draft holds as leg rows; the foot with when the ticket was closed, `Resume` in amber, which closes the panel and reopens the ticket as it was, and `Discard` in red. The draft is not an order and the badge does not count it. A bracket still waiting for its entry to fill shows as leg rows on that pending order's card; once armed it is its own card.
- **Filled**: filled orders under `Filled orders`, newest first, the order and its fill only; each card dates itself in its foot, so there are no date groups. A bracket that ended with an exit of its own filled is the sell that closed the position, so its card stands here too, dated when it ended, its legs saying how (the leg that exited reads its fill, the other `Cancelled`); a bracket leaves Pending only for one of the two ended tabs, never for nowhere.
- **Cancelled**: cancelled, expired, rejected, failed and not-sent orders under `Cancelled and rejected`, each with its state as a word at the right of its foot, where a live card carries its actions (`Rejected` in red); and each armed bracket that ended without exiting, its word there too: `Cancelled` when the person ended it (Cancel, or both legs removed), `Off` otherwise (the position sold from the ticket or elsewhere, a resting leg cancelled by hand at Wealthsimple). A bracket that never armed, its entry cancelled or refused, was only leg rows on that entry's card and has no card of its own.

**One grammar for every card.** Row one: the coloured word (green `Buy`, red `Sell`, accent `Bracket`), then the listing as exchange and symbol, `Buy NYSE: QNC`, `Bracket NYSE: QNC` (the symbol alone when the listing has no exchange stored), never the quantity; and the order's value at the right in the instrument's currency (quantity × the limit, the stop for a stop order, `≈` and the fill for a market order, contracts × 100 for an option, what it filled for once filled). Row two carries the quantity and how: `5 at 165.40 limit · Day`, `5 at market`, `5 stop 1.60 · limit 1.55 · GTC`, or how it went, `Filled 5 at 1.75`, and a bracket card has no second row, its legs saying it all (the fill has its own card on the Filled tab); its value at the right is the position's cost, what was paid for the shares under the bracket; the account is never on the card, it is the panel's heading. No status pills: the tab already says pending or filled, and the ended tab names the state in the foot. A third line only for what the second cannot say: `40 of 100 filled at 64.50` on a pending order partly filled, Wealthsimple's reason on a rejection; on a bracket, one row per leg. Then the foot: when, at the left (`Today 9:42 AM`, `Sep 8, 3:12 PM`); at the right, on every live card, `Edit` and `Cancel` in that order, Edit dimmed rather than absent where Wealthsimple cannot take a change (a stop order); a finished card carries its state word there instead (`Cancelled`, `Rejected`, `Expired`, `Not sent`). Nothing acts from inside a row. A bracket's own exit orders are never cards: the leg rows are their presentation.

**Bracket rows.** One row per leg, close under the one above with no rule between them (the only rule on a card is the one above its foot): the label in the leg's colour (`Stop loss` in the loss red, `Take profit` in the gain green), the quantity and level, `5 at 1.66`, `5 at 1.62 · trailing 5%` (the current level), `5 at 1.93`, and at the right the leg's amount in the same colour (quantity times level, times the contract multiplier), lined up under the card's value so the position's cost, the stop and the target read as a column. No state word while a leg simply rests at Wealthsimple, is watched here, or waits for the fill; a word only when something is under way or over, before the amount: `Placing` and `Cancelling` while a request is out, `Retrying · <reason>` after a refused one, at the end `Cancelled` on the leg that did not exit and `Off` on both when the bracket ended without exiting; the leg that exited reads how it went instead of its level, `Filled 5 at 1.64`, in a filled order's own grammar, its amount what it filled for, so the exit's fill is read where the bracket is. Edit on a bracket opens one editor in place with both legs' levels (Stop price, or Trail / Trail %; Limit price), `Remove stop loss` and `Remove take profit` beneath them, Save and Cancel. Save moves whichever leg changed (a resting stop is cancelled and placed again at the new level by the engine's next check; a placed target order is cancelled and the target watched again); Remove takes the leg off, its resting order cancelled, and a bracket with both legs removed ends. The header reads `Bracket changed · QNC`, `Stop loss removed · QNC`, for ten seconds. Cancel on a bracket asks once (`Cancel bracket`, naming the symbol and both legs; `Keep bracket` returns), cancels the bracket's resting orders at Wealthsimple and stops watching; the header reads `Bracket cancelled · QNC`.

**Edit and Cancel on an order.** Both are small outlined buttons at the foot's right: Edit in the ink with a faint outline, Cancel in the loss red with a red outline that fills lightly under the pointer, so the destructive action of the two reads as such; Cancel asks first. Edit opens Shares and, on a limit or stop-limit order, Limit price in place; Save sends Wealthsimple's own modify (`SoOrdersOrderModify` with the external id, `newLimitPrice`, `newQuantity`), and the header reads `Order changed · Buy 30 NVDA at 164.00 limit`. A stop order has no Edit, since Wealthsimple's modify carries no stop price. Cancel asks once (`Cancel order`, naming the order; `Keep order` returns), goes to Wealthsimple by the external id, and the card reads `Cancelling` on its second line until the next read says it is cancelled; the header reads `Cancel sent · Buy 1 QNC at 1.76 limit` for ten seconds, and a refusal keeps the card and puts Wealthsimple's reason in the header in red.

**Status** is Wealthsimple's, read back through the order's external id on the `TR` branch (their NEW, PENDING_SUBMISSION, PENDING_REVIEW, PENDING_FUND_TRANSFER, SUBMITTED, PLACED, CONTINGENT read as pending, PARTIALLY_FILLED as partially filled, CANCEL_PENDING as cancelling, FILLED and POSTED as filled, CANCELLED and DELETED as cancelled, EXPIRED, REJECTED with their reason); before Wealthsimple has answered a row reads as the app's own `Sent`, `Not sent` (a dry submit) or `Failed`. A row the app was still sending when it stopped may or may not have reached Wealthsimple: it is a Pending card reading `Sent` until the next read, which asks Wealthsimple for it by its external id; Wealthsimple's answer decides it (an entry that asked for a stop loss or a take profit then gets its bracket), and one Wealthsimple has no record of reads `Failed`, `Wealthsimple has no record of it: the app stopped while sending it.`

**Refresh.** Every open order is read back at once after it is sent and every 30 seconds while the app runs connected, and the pending-order feed with it, so an order placed in Wealthsimple's app appears (its time in force learned on the next read); with nothing open the feed alone is read every five minutes. The panel asks the server for the list every ten seconds while it is up, and its opening triggers a read when the last one is older than a tick. The header's badge follows the status poll. A read that fails is counted on the terminal (`bagholder orders: …`) and changes nothing. Under the development setting `BAGHOLDER_DRY_ORDERS=1` the app never writes to Wealthsimple: Cancel, Edit and a bracket's Save answer `Orders are off`.

**A fill reaches the book at once.** When an order Bagholder placed is read back filled — an entry, or a bracket's own stop or target — Wealthsimple is pulled at once for its own row of the fill, so the position changes within seconds, not at the next day's sync (a monitored stop that fills does not leave the position reading open until tomorrow). The book records the fill as Wealthsimple does, never as a row of the app's own making: nothing synthetic is written and no raw row is rewritten. Until the book holds Wealthsimple's row for that order (its order id is the row's key), it is pulled for again, soon at first (every ten seconds for the first minute, then every thirty seconds, two minutes, fifteen) and hourly after three hours, for as long as it is missing. The quantity asked for is kept on the order, so a later read of the same fill asks nothing more. An order Wealthsimple reports from its own app arrives with the sync like any other row.

### Disclosures

An instrument's regulatory filings, gathered from every source that covers it and merged into one list (the sources and their cadence are in §2, Market data). It is a card on the trade and holding detail (the same `tradeDetailHtml` serves both), full width, last on the page below the executions and thesis row; it is the only card whose height varies by instrument, so it goes where growth costs nothing and an absent card leaves no hole. The phone's presentation is in §8.

**The card.** Surface and elevation of the sibling cards. Title `Disclosures` at the left of the header. When the fetched set holds more than one category, a segmented control follows the title — `All` then each category present, in the vocabulary's order — filtering the list. When narrowed to one source (by a click on a source cell), a source chip in the News card's style (`Source`, the value, a `×` that clears it). At the right of the header: the source, when the list has only one, then the read stamp `read N min ago`, then a 24 px ghost re-read button carrying the sync glyph, the only way to force a read past the day-old cache. The list scrolls inside the card at 330 px, its header and column row fixed.

**The row and its columns.** One merged stream, newest first by default. A grid of Date, Document, Title, Summary, then Source and Size, then a trailing open affordance (Category is not a column — the header's filter carries it). **A column or control appears only when it carries a value:** one source and the Source column is gone and the header names that source; one category and the filter control is gone; no row carries a size and the Size column is gone; and no row has a summary yet and the Summary column is gone, appearing the moment the first one lands. Category is never a column (its filter is in the header). This keeps a single-source holding from showing scaffolding for regulators it will never have, and it is why a further source or category needs no change here. Date is the source's date as `5 Aug 2026`. Document is the filing's `type` alone (the SEC form code or the SEDAR+ document name), one line on every row. **Title** is its own column: the filing's title — the document's own when it exposes one (a PDF's metadata: `CHARBONE - Closing 2nd Drawdown`), else a short title the model derives from the filing's substance (`Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements`); a filename, exhibit label, or bare document id is rejected in favour of a real title, as is a title that does not read as text: a PDF whose strings are encrypted or compressed hands back bytes, and decoding them yields characters without yielding words (`\022 ð,0 ¿Oùÿ¯âUÀ<w°`), which the model's own title then replaces — a title is letters, digits and ordinary punctuation, and mostly letters, or it is no title. A structured filing the model reads poorly is parsed exactly instead: a Schedule 13G/13D becomes its stake and holder (`2.4% stake — Capital Ventures International`) from the raw XML, no model. A regulator's fill-in form is read the same way and for the same reason: it is mostly its own instructions — a Report of Exempt Distribution runs ten pages of guidance around a handful of filled boxes — and a model handed that text describes the guidance, which is how a report of a $1,500,000 placement came back as a sentence about National Instrument 81-106, the rule the form's own instructions cite. So: a form the app reads is read value by value and never reaches a model — `Exempt distribution of $1,500,000` over `$1,500,000 distributed from 1 purchaser on 8 September 2026, under NI 45-106 2.10 (minimum amount investment)`, each value the form's own and a value it does not carry left out rather than filled in — and a form the app does not read is not summarized at all, since the row's Document column already says what it is and a guess about it is worse than nothing. A document is a form when it carries three of the eight things only a fill-in form says (`(YYYY-MM-DD)`, `refer to Part`, `select only one`, `complete Schedule`, `if applicable`, `check the box`, `do not complete`, `of the Instructions`), so prose that happens to say one of them is not one. And wherever a model does answer, a hedged line is thrown away, title or summary: `likely`, `appears to`, `may be`, `it is not clear` mean it did not find out, and a summary states what a document says or says nothing. In the rare case nothing can be had, the Document type carries the row. Both Title and Summary shimmer while a row is being read. Summary is the one-sentence model summary, muted, clamped to two lines. Category and Source are plain text, Source in the accent and clickable to narrow; Size is right-aligned and tabular, blank where the source omits it, never a dash. Date, Document, Category, Source and Size sort (Summary does not), the arrow lit on the active one, first click descending, default Date descending. A source that both regulators carry (an event filed in two countries) is two rows, never folded.

**Reading, opening.** A refresh of the list never discards what was read from the documents: the list is refreshed far more often than a filed document changes, so a row the source still lists keeps its subject and summary while everything the source itself says about it (its type, date, size and the link, which SEDAR+ mints afresh each visit) is updated. Subject and summary are read from the documents themselves and filled into the columns on their own, top of the list first, one document at a time at SEDAR+'s pace; the issuer's document scope is cached for the run so the list is enriched in one walk, not one per row, and every result is stored so a later visit is instant. A document is read again only while there is something further to get: a row holding a title but no sentence, or the other way round, is read again while a model is up, since the two come from one read but not always in one pass; a row read for good is never read again — a regulator's form is read from its own boxes and no model adds to it, so a form that yields nothing is done, and the row says so rather than looking unfinished and being fetched from the regulator on every ask. The **subject** is the document's own title, which SEDAR+ hides behind a generic file name, drawn from the file's metadata with no setup. The **summary** is one sentence from a language model the app runs locally, the first whole sentence of what the model answers: a filing's summary opens by naming the issuer, and a company's name ends in a full stop far more often than a sentence does, so the end of a sentence is the first stop whose preceding word is neither an abbreviation nor an initial and which is followed by the start of a new one: the app uses a local model server the user already runs, or downloads and starts a small pinned one itself on first use, showing a shimmer in the cell meanwhile. Asking for a model is what starts one, so a document read that finds none still coming up waits the few seconds it needs rather than spending the read and returning for the same document later — without that, the first filing read in a session, which is the newest and the top of the list, was always the one left without a sentence. A model still downloading is never waited for, since that takes minutes: the row waits instead, and is read once the status says one is up. A summary needs the document's text — the HTML of a SEC filing, or, for a SEDAR+ PDF, whatever the app's PDF engine reads (a system `pdftotext` when present, otherwise `pdfminer.six`, which the app installs for itself in the background at startup, into `~/.bagholder/pylibs/`, a shimmer in the cell if a summary lands first); where the model or the text is genuinely unavailable the summary is simply absent (the Summary column does not appear), never a wrong guess. A click anywhere on a row expands it in place: its own Title and Summary cells, which otherwise clip to two lines, un-clip to show the full text (nothing is repeated, no separate panel); a second click collapses it. The trailing `↗` (or that link) opens the document as the source serves it — a SEC filing from its own page, a SEDAR+ document fetched live through the issuer's profile (a SEDAR+ URL dies with the session that minted it); while it hands off the arrow reads `Opening…`, no spinner or overlay. A row read by an older enrichment version is re-read once (its title and summary are treated as unread until the current logic has produced them). **A row counts as read only when it has both a title and a summary**, on every card that reads documents, which share one record of what has been read. Both come from one read but not always in the same pass, so a row holding one of them is read again rather than left half filled. What bounds the reading is not how many times the document was asked for but how many times the asking could have answered: the sentence comes from the local model, so a read made while no model is up says nothing about whether this document has one — it only failed to ask, and must not count against the document. The model's phase names are not consulted for this; the only questions are whether the read answered and whether a model was up for it. Two reads that could have answered is the bound: what those cannot produce, the document does not have, and the row settles with whichever half it has. A row that found no model to ask **waits** rather than finishing, asking for nothing further, and the app status the page already polls (`summaryReady`, read without starting anything) is what wakes it once a model is up. Reading again fills what is missing and never empties what is there, an empty result on a later pass meaning that read found nothing rather than that the stored line was wrong; only the first read under the current logic replaces both, so a stale junk title from an older version is still cleared.

**States.** *Reading* — the header names the read in progress and a single accent sweep runs under it over row-shaped skeletons, restful because several paced requests are normal. *List* — the merged stream. *Nothing filed* — a filer matched but nothing is in range: one line, `Nothing filed.`, the header naming the sources read. *No filer* — no regulator has this listing (a crypto holding, some ETFs): one line, `No regulatory filer for this listing.`, and the card stays so the page keeps its shape between instruments. A source that could not be reached (an outage, a maintenance page) is never taken for no filer: with nothing filed and no source naming a filer, the line names the sources that failed, `SEDAR+ is unavailable.` (`SEDAR+ · SEC EDGAR are unavailable.` for two). *Source off* — an unavailable source is simply absent and unnamed while the others read normally; nothing announces a failure. No captions, tooltips or helper text beyond these lines.

What the card reads, the phone apps and any tool (Claude included) read the same way, through the pipeline below:

- **The pipeline.** One pipeline holds the shared vocabulary and a registry of providers, each dispatched by the instrument's market: SEDAR+ (Canadian company filings, over a browser TLS handshake) and SEC EDGAR (US filings). Each provider normalizes its rows to one item — `{id, source, category, date, dateText, type, title, size, url}` — with `id` prefixed by source (`sedar:…`, `sec:…`). The categories are Financials, Material events, Governance, Offerings, Insider & ownership, News releases, Other. A new source is additive.
- **The store.** A `filings` table keyed by the instrument's symbol, one row per item, carrying its source and category; a source's rows are replaced on its own refresh so one source's failure never drops another's. A source that answers empty where it had rows before is a read that came back short, and the stored rows stand: a filed document never disappears. The SEDAR+ profile resolved for a symbol is remembered (`sedar_profile:<symbol>`) and the fetch time stamped (`filings_fetched:<symbol>`), separate from every other source's stamp.
- **The endpoint.** `GET /api/filings?symbol=<symbol>` returns the merged rows newest first, a per-source `sources` status (`available`, `matched`, and `filer` — whether a filer was found there at all, which tells "nothing filed in range" from "no filer"), the `categories` vocabulary, the SEDAR+ `profileNo` and the fetch stamp; `&refresh=1` fetches first; `&name`, `&exchange` and `&currency` carry the instrument's own identity so the sources are chosen and matched accurately (the page passes all three). `GET /api/filings/doc?symbol=<symbol>&id=<item id>` returns the document itself — a SEC document from its static URL, a SEDAR+ document fetched live through the issuer's profile since a SEDAR+ URL is session-bound. A list is refreshed when it is asked for and its stamp is older than 24 hours, or when forced, never on a loop.
- **The connector and the command line.** An MCP server exposes the merged pipeline as MCP tools for Claude Desktop and Claude Code; the `sedar` command prints SEDAR+ data as JSON for a shell. `DISCLOSURES.md` is the guide.
- **Availability, per source.** SEDAR+ needs `curl_cffi` to clear its bot gate; EDGAR needs only the standard library. A source that is unavailable simply contributes nothing while the others still show, and the endpoint reports each source's state; the rest of the app is unchanged.

## 5. Filters

One filter set applies to every page: Date (presets 1D 1W 1M 3M 6M YTD 1Y 5Y, years, or a range), Account, Symbol, Grade, Tag, Side, Kind, Exchange, Result, and ranges on Price, Hold, P&L and Qty. Trades are scoped by close date. Cashflow honours only Date, Account and Symbol and says which other filters it ignored. The filter popover opens on a search box above the field list. Typing lists every value from the list fields that contains the text (the book's symbols the text is a ticker of first, then account, grade, tag, side, kind, exchange, result, each row naming its field, then the symbols matched by name alone and the listings found outside the book), the first match highlighted; the keyboard stays in the box throughout, as in any command palette: the arrows move the highlight, which starts on the first match, Enter toggles the highlighted value on or off, Backspace edits the text (or, with the box empty, deselects the highlighted value), Tab goes straight to Done, then Clear all, then back to the box, and Shift+Tab runs that ring backwards, so Enter on either button finishes without the mouse; a click on a row toggles it too; the text and the list stay so several values can be picked in a row, a picked value marked by a short bar along the row's left edge in the chip colour of the theme (the accent, or neutral in Midnight where the accent is the gain green) on top of the row's shading, drawn inside the row so no text moves, and the box keeps focus throughout. When no value matches, Enter makes the text the free-text Search filter; typing alone never filters anything. Every symbol row, the book's and the ones found outside it, has one shape: the symbol, its name, its exchange at the right, then three icons in the same places on every row, each live only where it applies and dimmed, not clickable, otherwise: a funnel that narrows the book by that symbol (live for a symbol the book has traded — a listing it has never seen has nothing to filter), then the ticket's Buy and Sell (Buy on a share or ETF, Sell where a position is open, neither on an option contract). **The row itself goes to the ticker**, as a symbol does everywhere else on the page — the holding's page where the book holds it, the listing's own otherwise (§6, Markets) — so a click means the same thing whether or not the book has met the symbol; before this, a found listing opened its page while one the book had traded filtered the page behind the popover, which is two readings of one gesture. Filtering is the funnel, which leaves the popover open for the next pick, and an option contract, which has no page, keeps its row as its filter. The keyboard follows: Enter on the highlighted row does what a click does, and Shift+Enter narrows by it. The text is also looked up outside the book, never at Wealthsimple: 300 ms after the last keystroke, one round to the exchanges' own directories (Nasdaq's autocomplete for US listings on NYSE, NASDAQ and BATS, shares and ETFs; TSX's company directory for the TSX and the TSX-V), the three asked together and once per distinct text while the app runs (a round a source missed is asked again next time). The book's symbols the text is a ticker of come first, in order of relevance: exact tickers on any exchange, then tickers starting with the text, then tickers containing it, then the option contracts of a matched underlying, nearest expiry first; a contract row carries no name. The other fields' values follow them. Last come the book's symbols matched only by their name, then the listings found, less any the book already holds (same symbol without a Canadian venue suffix, same exchange), twelve at most, exact tickers, then tickers starting with the text, then name matches. A name is the weakest match, so a holding whose name merely contains the text never stands above the account the text spells, and a run of same-named companies never pushes that account out of the scroll box. Choosing a found listing (click or Enter) opens its page (§6, Markets), as a listing opens everywhere else; its Buy icon opens the ticket, which is what that icon is for on every symbol row and the only thing on a row that reaches an order. The same arrows move through every row. CSE and Cboe Canada listings have no directory yet and are reached only once the book holds them. ⌘K on a Mac, Ctrl+K elsewhere, opens the popover with that box focused, from anywhere on the page; Escape closes it. With nothing open and nothing being typed, Escape clears every filter, the same as Clear all. Inside a single list filter the same keys drive its own value list.

## 6. Layout

- The app sits inside a rounded panel with a 24 px margin on a darker mat, in all three themes: Nocturne (default), Midnight (green accents, monospace numerals), Light. The theme is chosen from the menu: a `Theme` row naming the current theme, with a chevron, opens beside it, on hover or on a tap, a list of the three, each with a round swatch of its background and the current one in the accent colour with a check; choosing one closes the list. The `Notifications` row sits under Theme and opens beside it the same way (§2, Notifications). The Bagholder wordmark is identical in every theme. Tag chips, the selected grade and tag suggestions take the accent colour, except in Midnight, where the accent is the gain green: there they are neutral grey, so a tag or grade never reads as a gain or a loss.
- No table may extend past its card or clip a cell at a window 1340 px wide or wider. Below that a table may scroll sideways inside its card, never the page.
- Table headers are uppercase, one line, level with each other regardless of alignment, and a centred header sits exactly over the centre of its values.
- Scrollbars appear only while scrolling and only inside tables.
- **Text the page had to cut reads in full on hover.** Wherever a line is clipped with an ellipsis and the reader cannot get at it another way — a watched listing's name, a headline, a heatmap block or tile, an order's listing, a suggestion's name — hovering it shows the whole in the charts' own tooltip, one tooltip for the page, above the line and inside the window. Nothing is marked up for it: a line qualifies by being cut, both sides measured in fractions — the text by a range over its contents, which the browser lays out in full however little of it it paints, and the room by the line's own box less its padding and border — rather than by the scroll and client widths the browser rounds to whole pixels, which report a line with 42.66 px of room and 43.05 px of text as fitting while drawing the ellipsis, so a line clipped by a fraction — the one that reads worst — is caught too. The cut line is the innermost box that *can* clip: an inline box cannot, whatever its `text-overflow` says, so a `<b>` inside a cell that declares the ellipsis is not the cut line — treating it as one measured a box that is never clipped, answered "not cut", and left a whole column of the Disclosures table without its hover. Otherwise the cut line is the innermost box that clips: a row holding one is not itself cut, since its own box fits and the text measured through it would be the inner line's. So a line that fits has no hover and a line a click un-clips (a disclosure's title and summary) has none either. A tile adds the listing's name under its symbol, the one line nothing else on that card shows. This is the only hover the page has beyond the charts' own, and no element anywhere carries a `title` tooltip.
- Colours come from theme tokens only.

### Motion

Two easings and three durations cover every transition, and the further a thing travels the longer it takes: movement and resizing use `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)`, colour and opacity use `ease` and are always quicker. Hover, colour and opacity take .12–.2 s; entrances, dialogs and panels .22–.3 s; the tab indicator and the heatmap's re-tiling .36–.42 s. Colours are the theme's own tokens, so every transition reads correctly in all three themes.

- **The heatmap re-tiles rather than snapping.** Changing the universe or the sizer moves each symbol's rectangle to its new slot over .42 s, with its sector frame travelling too; a rectangle that joins fades up from 94%, one that leaves is removed. Cells are kept under their symbol across a redraw, which is what makes them travel instead of the text swapping inside stationary boxes. Only a cell that leads to a holding takes a pointer and brightens on hover.
- **The tab indicator** is a 2 px bar under the active tab that slides and resizes between tabs over .36 s, measured from the tab itself and re-measured after a redraw, on resize and once the webfont settles. It fades out rather than collapsing when a trade or holding is open.
- **Panels and dialogs** arrive once: a side panel slides 26 px from the right over .3 s, a dialog rises 8 px and scales from 98% over .24 s, and their scrims fade over .18 s. A panel already on screen does not play its entrance again, whether the model reloads under it or it redraws itself (a tab switched, its list re-read).
- **Loading shows the shape of the page that is coming.** Each tab has its own silhouette — tiles, chart and list cards on the dashboard and cashflow; toolbar, header row and fourteen body rows on trades; index tiles, the treemap block and two lists on markets; tiles, a donut and a holdings list on portfolio — so nothing changes shape when the data lands: the page resolves rather than reflowing. The skeleton is not swapped for the page; it is lifted over it and faded out over .3 s while the page arrives underneath with its own entrance. The position and the fade are committed separately, and the unmount hangs off its own timer rather than a frame callback, which never fires in a background tab. Bars pulse on a stagger and vary in width, so the field reads as content rather than as a loading graphic. Spinners are kept for actions whose length is genuinely unknown.
- **A figure that changes rolls to its new value.** Every tile value, on every tab, is a row of digits; when it changes, each digit that is actually different slides to its new numeral over .58 s, the ones column leading and each column to its left following 18 ms later, which is what makes it read as a counter rather than a wave. Digits that did not change do not move, and a dollar sign, a separator or a minus is not a wheel. Two figures roll into one another only when they are the same shape — the same length, with the same non-digits in the same places; anything else is a different number rather than the same number moved, and is simply set. At rest the figure is ordinary text, so it reads and copies as it always did; the strips exist only for the second the roll takes, are hidden from screen readers, and change nothing about the size of the value or its tile.
- **A row just added to the watchlist** is tinted and the tint decays over .6 s. Row hover is instant and deliberately so.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps the colour and opacity transitions and drops the rest: the heatmap's geometry, the entrances' movement, the skeleton's pulse, and the digit roll, which sets its figure directly.

### What a published copy carries

The Python image copies every Python file in `python/`, not a list of them, and the web archive carries every tracked one, flat, beside the page and its assets. The Rust image and archives carry the four binaries and the same three assets. A list was kept by hand once: four modules were added after it was written, every image built for three days crashed on its first import, and nothing noticed until a user reported it. Tests now read each Dockerfile and the tracked files and fail if anything the app imports or serves would be missing, a copy of only the published files is asked to import the app, and the web archive is built and put through the updater's own extract and check.

### The store

One SQLite file, opened through a small pool of connections rather than a new one per read: creating what is missing and running the migrations is the same work every time on a database that has already been through it, and a single model request asked for it eighteen times. The stamped schema version is still read on every borrow, and the file at the path checked to be the one the kept connections hold, so a database replaced or rolled back under a running app is migrated as it always was. Durability is not traded for speed: every commit is still flushed, because a thesis or a grade is the one thing in the file that cannot be fetched again. The relabelling of newly synced option rows is stamped with the fingerprint of the rows it ran on, so it follows a sync instead of running on every read.

The header's counts — activities, accounts, last sync — are read as counts. Building the whole book to measure it cost a tenth of a second and thirty megabytes every thirty seconds, for two numbers and a date.

## 7. Verification before a web-page commit

1. Every displayed figure is traced to the model field this file names for it, and the meaning matches, not just the field's existence.
2. The page is rendered against a copy of real data at 1200, 1340, 1440 and 1680 px. On every page, including a trade detail, no table overflows its container and no cell content is clipped at 1340 px and above.
3. Header boxes are measured to be at one height, and centred headers at zero offset from their column's centre.
4. Any lookup between tables uses ids, and is exercised with a synthetic duplicate symbol in a second account.
5. Every interaction and keyboard behaviour this file gives — not only what renders — is exercised by actually driving it: each button, tab, field, drag, sort and drill-down clicked; each shortcut pressed (⌘/Ctrl+O the Orders panel, ⌘/Ctrl+K the filter, Left/Right the tabs and the Orders panel's own tabs, the Escape cascade); the filter's fields-search listing book *and* external (Yahoo) symbols, including tickers with two listings on different exchanges (AMZN, CNQ, ENB), and Enter committing free text; the menu's items (theme, notifications, connect, disconnect, clear, export, import, folder); and the Connecting flow (§4). "It renders correctly" is not "it works": a screen that looks right can still have a dead control, a dropped shortcut, or a keyed `{#each}` that crashes on a duplicate key — all of which pass a figures-and-widths check and fail a real click-through. A reimplementation on another framework is verified this way against §4 and §5 before it is called done.
6. `python3 -m unittest discover -s python/tests -t python` passes, and `cargo test --workspace` in `rust/` passes.
7. If any step fails, nothing is committed and the finding is reported first.

For the phone apps the equivalent is in `CLAUDE.md` ("How changes land", step 6) and `MOBILE.md`: both platforms in the same PR, every screen captured on both from the same seeded rows and compared.

## 8. The phone

The iOS and Android apps show the same figures as the web page, computed on the device by their own model implementations; §2 and §3 apply unchanged. A phone screen is a layout of those figures, not a new definition of them, and both apps lay them out the same way. Where the phone departs from §4 to §6 it is listed here; anything not listed is as on the web.

**Disclosures is desktop-only for now.** The Disclosures card (§4) is not on the phone yet. Unlike every other figure, it is not computed from the shared model but fetched from external regulators through the desktop pipeline, and SEDAR+ needs a browser TLS fingerprint the phones cannot present natively. The phone treatment (a two-line list, five newest, into a full list where the category filter lives) is designed and specified when its fetch path is settled — the phone reaching a companion desktop's `/api/filings`, or a hosted endpoint. Until then the phone apps are unchanged by this feature.

**Shell.** A header with the mark, the wordmark, the version, the sync status, a funnel button for the filters and a three-line button for the menu; The saved login is refreshed as on the desktop: one grant at a time, a login another read rotated meanwhile is adopted without a post, and a refresh token Wealthsimple has refused is never posted again, the status then reading `Saved login refused. Connect Wealthsimple again.` four tabs along the bottom (Dashboard, Trades, Portfolio, Cashflow), each with its symbol over its label and a 2 pt accent line over the current one. A trade or holding detail sits over its tab with the tab bar still below it and a round back button. The menu is a sheet: Connect Wealthsimple or Sync now and Disconnect, then the activity count and the version. The filters are a sheet whose search field takes focus as it opens.

**Cards.** 12 pt above and below the content, 16 pt at the sides, a 24 pt header row with a 15 pt semibold title at its top; a control at the right of the header (the index toggle, a sort menu, a figure) sits in that row. Nothing under a title: no captions, subtitles or helper lines, no "All accounts", no "Vs S&P 500" (the toggle itself reads `Vs S&P 500`), no "CAD", no "Outperformed … in N of M years", no "Dividend" beside a history row.

**Tiles.** Two per page in a row that swipes sideways, with the indicator below: 4 pt dots at 24 % ink, a 14 × 4 accent pill for the current page. Dashboard: Realized P&L, Win rate; Profit factor, Expectancy; Max drawdown, Avg annualized. Portfolio: Market value, Net asset value; Cost basis, Margin used; Available margin, Unrealized P&L. Cashflow: the year to date, Yield on cost; All time, Margin used (or Last 12 months without a margin account in scope); then the past years, newest first. The tile lines are the desktop's: `$x/mo` under Yield on cost, `total earned` under All time, `$x/mo margin interest` under Margin used.

**Charts.** No value axis. The card's figure sits at its top right: Equity shows the latest point, P&L the sum in scope, Distributions the projected month. The Equity line runs edge to edge, scaled from its low to its high, 160 pt tall, four date labels below. A long press reads the point under the finger from the first touch: the figure at the top right becomes that day's or month's, the date or month appears at the bottom under the finger, the rest of the chart dims (the equity line and its fill keep their colour up to the finger, the marker is a ring), and the page and any pager hold still until release. Bars fill their month's slot 4 pt apart on the web page's scale (§4); a tap on a P&L bar opens the month.

**Dashboard.** Tiles; Equity; then one swiping row of three equal cards, Annual returns (the years scrolling inside the card, three visible, no footer), P&L (the monthly bars, titled `P&L`) and Grade vs P&L; By symbol, whose header is a sort menu (P&L, Trades, Win rate, Avg hold; the current column again flips the order); Review queue.

**Portfolio.** Tiles; Allocation (the donut, one slice each up to ten holdings and otherwise the ten largest and `Other (N)`, with the symbols and their shares beside it); Holdings, each row the symbol (`SHORT` on a short) with `account · Book · Market` under it on the left, the unrealized P&L over its percentage on the right, then `Today` with the day's change and percent, or `—` without a quote; sorted by unrealized P&L. A tap opens the holding on the screen a trade opens, titled `Holding`: the chart with the fills, Open, Close (blank), Entry, Exit, Qty, Hold, Account, the executions and the journal.

**Cashflow.** Tiles; Cashflow (the monthly bars in the accent colour with the month's margin interest drawn over them from the same baseline at the same width in the negative colour, the legend at the card's top right; while a bar is pressed, the month's Distributions (in the accent colour), Margin interest and Net in a small card of its own, no larger than its four lines, outlined and shadowed, floating over the bars at the top left under the title, so nothing else moves); Allocation (the donut by projected monthly income, no switch); Positions, each row the symbol with `shares · avg · dist × freq` under it on the left, the month's payout over the yield on cost on the right, then Ex-Div and Pay Day; History, each row the symbol, `date · qty × per`, the amount and its currency, without the payment kind.

**Connecting.** Connect opens Wealthsimple's sign-in page in the app's own web view, kept loaded ahead of the tap, and captures the session from its cookies once signed in; sign in with email, password and the two-factor code. A passkey does not work inside an app's web view on either platform: iOS refuses the request unless the app is a browser or the site lists the app, and Android's web view hides the button; that is the platforms' rule, not a fault to fix.

**Verification.** Both platforms in the same PR; every screen and state captured on the iOS simulator and the Android emulator from the same seeded rows and compared side by side; speed judged on a phone launched from the home screen.
